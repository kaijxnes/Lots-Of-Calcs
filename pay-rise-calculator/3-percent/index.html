Frequently asked questions

How do I work out a 3% pay rise?

Multiply your salary by 0.03: 3% of £30,000 is £900, so the new salary is £30,900. After tax and National Insurance, the extra take-home pay is less — £648.00 a year on £30,000.

Why is a 3% rise worth less after tax?

The extra pay is taxed at your highest rates — 28% for most basic-rate taxpayers in England, Wales and Northern Ireland (20% income tax and 8% National Insurance), 42% for higher-rate, and an effective 60% plus NI between £100,000 and £125,140, where the personal allowance is withdrawn.

Is a 3% pay rise good?

It depends on inflation: if prices have risen faster than your pay, a rise can still leave you worse off in real terms. The inflation calculator shows how prices have changed.
