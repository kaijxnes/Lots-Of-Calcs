Frequently asked questions

How much of a pay rise do I keep?

Most of a rise is taxed at your top rates: on a basic-rate salary in England, Wales and Northern Ireland you keep about 72p of each extra pound (20% income tax, 8% National Insurance), and on a higher-rate salary about 58p (40% and 2%). Student loan repayments take another 9% above the threshold.

What is the 60% tax trap?

Between £100,000 and £125,140 you lose £1 of personal allowance for every £2 earned, so a rise in that band is taxed at an effective 60% plus National Insurance. Paying more into a pension can bring your income back under £100,000.

How do I work out a percentage pay rise?

Multiply your salary by the percentage and divide by 100: 4% of £35,000 is £1,400. The calculator also takes a rise in pounds and shows it as a percentage.

Does it include my pension?

Put in your pension contribution as a percentage of pay — it rises with your salary. Employer contributions aren't part of take-home pay.
