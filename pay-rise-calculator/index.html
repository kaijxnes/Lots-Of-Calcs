Frequently asked questions

How much of a pay rise do I keep?

Most of a rise is taxed at your top (marginal) rates, so you keep less of it than of your salary overall. In England, Wales and Northern Ireland a basic-rate taxpayer keeps about 72p of each extra pound (20% income tax, 8% National Insurance) and a higher-rate taxpayer about 58p; in Texas, with no state income tax, a 12% federal bracket plus 7.65% Social Security and Medicare leaves about 80 cents of each extra dollar. Does it work outside the UK?

Yes — pick where you're paid: every US state (with filing status), every Canadian province, Western Europe, Australia, New Zealand, India and Japan, using the same tax rules and options as the salary calculator, checked every month.

What is the UK's 60% tax trap?

Between £100,000 and £125,140 you lose £1 of personal allowance for every £2 earned, so a rise in that band is taxed at an effective 60% plus National Insurance. Paying more into a pension can bring your income back under £100,000.

How do I work out a percentage pay rise?

Multiply your salary by the percentage and divide by 100: 4% of £35,000 is £1,400. The calculator also takes a rise in pounds and shows it as a percentage.

Does it include my pension?

Put in your pension contribution as a percentage of pay — it rises with your salary. Employer contributions aren't part of take-home pay.
