Frequently asked questions

What is a 3% increase on £200,000?

£6,000 a year before tax, so your salary goes to £206,000. After tax and National Insurance that's £3,180.00 a year, or £265.00 a month, in England, Wales and Northern Ireland; £250.00 a month in Scotland.

Why do I only keep 53% of it?

Because a rise is taxed at your highest rates: at £200,000 each extra pound loses 47p to income tax and National Insurance (the higher rate). Student loan repayments take another 9% of the rise if you're above the threshold, and a pension contribution set as a percentage of pay goes up with it.

Which tax year is this?

2026/27, using the same rates as the salary calculators. A rise in April may fall in the next tax year, which can have different thresholds.
