How each figure is worked out

Month-on-month (MoM) compares each month with the one before: (this month − last month) ÷ last month × 100.

Year-on-year (YoY) compares each month with the same month a year earlier — the entry 12 rows above it — so it appears from your 13th month onwards.

Cumulative growth is the total change from your first entry to your last.

Worked example

A shop's sales were £40,000 in November, £58,000 in December and £36,000 in January. Month-on-month, December is up (58,000 − 40,000) ÷ 40,000 = 45% and January is down (36,000 − 58,000) ÷ 58,000 = −37.9%.

That January fall looks alarming, but if the previous January was £32,000, year-on-year January is up (36,000 − 32,000) ÷ 32,000 = 12.5%. The shop is growing; December is simply always busy. That is why seasonal businesses watch YoY.

Turning monthly growth into a yearly rate

Monthly growth compounds, so 12 months of 3% growth is not 36% a year. Multiply 1.03 by itself 12 times and you get 1.426 — 42.6% annual growth. Going the other way, 20% growth over a year is (1.20)1/12 − 1 = 1.53% a month, not 1.67%.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between MoM and YoY growth?

Month-on-month compares each figure to the previous month, so it reacts quickly but is noisy. Year-on-year compares it to the same month a year earlier, which strips out seasonal patterns and shows the underlying trend.

Why can month-on-month growth look terrible when the business is fine?

Seasonality. A quiet January after a busy December shows a steep monthly fall even in a growing business, which is exactly why the year-on-year comparison matters.

How do I calculate year-on-year growth?

Take last year's figure from this year's, divide by last year's and multiply by 100. March sales of £27,000 against £24,000 the March before is a 3,000 ÷ 24,000 × 100 = 12.5% YoY increase.

Why is there no YoY figure for my first 12 months?

Year-on-year needs the same month a year earlier to compare against, so it starts at the 13th entry. Start your list a year before the first month you want a YoY figure for.

Do the month labels matter?

No — labels such as “Jan 2026” are only for your reference. The calculation uses the order of the rows, so enter one month per row, in date order, with no months missing.

What if a month's value is zero?

Growth from zero can't be expressed as a percentage, because it means dividing by zero, so that comparison shows a dash. Growth to zero is shown as −100%.
