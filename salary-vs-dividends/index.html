A salary up to the National Insurance primary threshold (£12,570) costs no employee NI or income tax and counts towards your State Pension; above the secondary threshold (£5,000) the company pays 15% employer NI on it, but that and the salary reduce corporation tax. Which wins depends on your profit and whether you can claim the Employment Allowance — the calculator compares them for your figures.

Frequently asked questions

What is the most tax-efficient salary for a director?

Usually a salary at or near the personal allowance and NI primary threshold (£12,570 for 2026/27), with the rest as dividends. If your company can't claim the Employment Allowance, a salary of £5,000 (the secondary threshold) avoids employer NI but loses some corporation tax relief — compare both above.

Why are dividends taxed less than salary?

They aren't free of tax: the company pays corporation tax (19% to 25%) on profits first, then you pay dividend tax. But there's no National Insurance on dividends, which usually makes them cheaper than salary overall.

Can I claim the Employment Allowance as a one-person company?

No — if your company's only employee paid above the secondary threshold is you as the sole director, it can't claim the Employment Allowance. With another employee on the payroll, it usually can.

What corporation tax rate will my company pay?

19% on profits up to £50,000, 25% on profits over £250,000, and in between the main rate less marginal relief — an effective rate that rises gradually.
