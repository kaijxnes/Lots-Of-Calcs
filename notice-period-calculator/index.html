These are the legal minimums. Your employer must give at least the notice in your contract or the statutory minimum, whichever is longer: a week from one month's service, then a week for every complete year from 2 years, up to 12 weeks after 12 years. You must give at least a week once you've been in the job for more than a month; if your contract asks for more, you may be in breach of contract if you give less. Notice usually runs from the start of the day after it's handed in.

Pay during your notice

You'll usually get your normal pay rate during your notice, with bonuses and commission unless your contract says otherwise. When an employer works out notice pay for redundancy, it's based on your average weekly pay over the 12 weeks before notice starts. Your employer can ask you not to come in (garden leave) on the same pay and benefits, or pay you in lieu of notice if your contract allows it or you agree; tax and National Insurance come off that payment. If you're being made redundant, the redundancy pay calculator works out the lump sum.

Frequently asked questions

What is the statutory notice period?

If your employer ends your job, at least a week once you've worked there a month, then a week for every complete year from 2 years, up to 12 weeks after 12 years. If you resign, at least a week once you've worked there more than a month.

Does my contract's notice period or the statutory one apply?

Whichever is longer. GOV.UK: you must be given at least the notice stated in your contract or the statutory minimum notice period, whichever is longer.

How much notice do I get after 5 years?

At least 5 weeks from your employer, or more if your contract says so.

When does my notice period start?

Usually from the start of the day after you hand in (or are given) notice. Handing in a week's notice on a Monday, your last day is the following Monday.

Do I get paid during my notice period?

Usually your normal pay rate, plus bonuses and commission unless your contract says otherwise. Your employer can pay you in lieu of notice if it's in your contract or you agree.
