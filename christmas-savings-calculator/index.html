Making it easy

Set a budget first. Add up presents, food and drink, travel, cards and wrapping, and going out — then save for that figure rather than for "as much as possible".

Automate it. A weekly or payday standing order into a separate pot does the saving for you. If smaller, rising steps suit you better, the 52-week savings challenge started in January reaches £1,378 by Christmas Eve.

Keep the rest of the year in view. The 50/30/20 budget calculator shows how Christmas fits in your monthly pay, and an emergency fund stops January's bills going on a credit card.

Frequently asked questions

How much do I need to save a week for Christmas 2027?

Divide your budget by the weeks left. Starting on 1 December 2026, there are 53 weekly deposits before 1 December 2027, so £500 is £9.43 a week and £1,000 is £18.87.

Is it better to save weekly or monthly?

Whichever matches how you're paid. A standing order the day after payday is the easiest to stick to, because the money moves before it can be spent.

What if I start late?

The same budget is shared over fewer weeks, so each one is bigger — the table on each budget page shows how much. Starting even a month earlier makes a noticeable difference.

Where should I keep Christmas savings?

A separate easy-access savings account or pot, so it isn't spent by accident and you can take it out in December without a penalty.
