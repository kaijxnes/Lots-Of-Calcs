How much flooring to buy

Multiply the room's length by its width for the floor area, then add a waste allowance for cuts and offcuts. Round up to whole packs: flooring is sold by the pack, and the area each pack covers is printed on it.

Worked example: a 4.2 m × 3.6 m bedroom is 15.12 m². With 10% waste that is 15.12 × 1.1 = 16.63 m². If the laminate comes in packs covering 2.13 m², you need 16.63 ÷ 2.13 = 7.8, so buy 8 packs.

For an L-shaped room, split it into two rectangles, work each one out and add them together.

How much paint to buy

The wall area is the perimeter of the room multiplied by the wall height: 2 × (length + width) × height. Take off about 1.6 m² for each door and 1.4 m² for each window, then divide by the paint's coverage per litre and multiply by the number of coats.

Worked example: a 4 m × 3.5 m room with 2.4 m walls, one door and one window. The walls are 2 × 7.5 × 2.4 = 36 m², less 3 m² for the door and window leaves 33 m². Two coats at 10 m² per litre is 33 × 2 ÷ 10 = 6.6 litres — a 5 litre tin and a 2.5 litre tin.

The coverage figure is on the tin; check it, because it varies by paint. A ceiling is length × width, and ceiling paint usually covers less per litre than wall emulsion.

Frequently asked questions

How much paint do I need for a room?

Work out the wall area, subtract doors and windows, then divide by the coverage figure on the tin and multiply by the number of coats. Most emulsions cover somewhere around 10-12 square metres per litre per coat.

How much extra flooring should I order for wastage?

Around 10% extra is the usual allowance for cuts and offcuts, rising to roughly 15% for diagonal or herringbone layouts and awkwardly shaped rooms.

How many litres of paint for a 3 × 4 metre room?

With 2.4 m walls, one door and one window, the walls come to 2 × 7 × 2.4 = 33.6 m² less 3 m², so 30.6 m². Two coats at 10 m² per litre needs about 6.1 litres — a 5 litre tin plus a 2.5 litre tin.

How do I measure a room in square metres?

Measure the length and width in metres at floor level and multiply them. If you measure in feet, multiply feet by feet for square feet and divide by 10.764 for square metres. The unit converter handles feet and inches to metres.

Should I buy a little extra paint?

Yes, if it's a mixed colour. Tinted paint from different batches can differ slightly, so buying enough in one go avoids a visible join, and a little left over is handy for touching up later.

Do I need to include skirting boards and doors?

Not in the wall figure — woodwork is usually painted in gloss or satinwood, bought separately in smaller tins. A litre of gloss goes a long way on skirting and a couple of doors.
