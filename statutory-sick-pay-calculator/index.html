How much Statutory Sick Pay is

SSP is £123.25 a week or 80% of your average weekly earnings, whichever is lower, for up to 28 weeks (2026/27). On average earnings of £154.06 a week or more you get the full £123.25; below that, 80% of your pay. It's paid for every full day you're off sick that you'd normally have worked, through payroll on your usual payday, with tax and National Insurance taken off. Your employer can pay more under a company sick pay scheme, but not less.

SSP for each day off

The weekly amount is shared across your "qualifying days", the days you normally work. Working five days a week, each day off sick pays a fifth of the weekly SSP; working three, a third. Over 28 weeks that's 28 times your qualifying days.

Who gets it and how long for

You need to be an employee who has done some work for your employer and been ill for at least one full working day; if you work even a minute before going home sick, that day doesn't count. Tell your employer by their deadline, or within 7 days, and give a fit note if you're off more than 7 days in a row, counting days you don't work. Spells of sickness 8 weeks or less apart are linked, and the 28 weeks count across them. You can get SSP from each job if you have more than one. When SSP runs out you may be able to claim Universal Credit or Employment and Support Allowance. If you're on maternity pay, the maternity pay calculator covers that instead.

Frequently asked questions

How much is Statutory Sick Pay?

£123.25 a week, or 80% of your average weekly earnings if that's lower, for up to 28 weeks in 2026/27. Tax and National Insurance come off it like normal pay.

Do I get sick pay from the first day?

SSP is paid for every full day you're off sick that you'd normally have worked, once you've been ill for at least one full working day. A day you started work and went home sick doesn't count.

How much SSP do I get if I earn £120.00 a week?

£96.00 a week: 80% of £120.00, because that's less than £123.25. Working five days a week that's £19.20 for each day off.

How is SSP worked out for each day?

The weekly amount is divided by the number of days you normally work. On five days a week at the full rate, each day off pays £24.65.

What happens after 28 weeks of SSP?

SSP stops. You may be able to claim Universal Credit or Employment and Support Allowance, and your employer should give you form SSP1.

Can my employer pay less than SSP?

No. Your employer can pay more through a company (occupational) sick pay scheme in your contract, but never less than the statutory amount.
