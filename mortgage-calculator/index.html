Frequently asked questions

How is a mortgage repayment worked out?

With a repayment mortgage, every monthly payment is the same and covers that month's interest plus some of the loan. The payment is set so the loan is paid off exactly at the end of the term: payment = loan × r × (1 + r)ⁿ ÷ ((1 + r)ⁿ − 1), where r is the annual rate divided by 12 and n is the number of months. On £270,000 at 4.5% over 25 years that is £1,500.75.

Why do I pay mostly interest at the start?

Interest is charged on what you still owe, which is highest at the start. In the first year of the example, £12,028 of the payments is interest and only £5,981 reduces the loan; you don't owe less than half until year 16. Overpaying early has the biggest effect for the same reason.

What is the difference between repayment and interest-only?

Interest-only payments cover just the interest, so they are lower — £1,012.50 a month in the example against £1,500.75 — but the whole £270,000 is still owed at the end and has to be repaid from savings, investments or a sale. Over 25 years interest-only costs £303,750 in interest, against £180,224 on repayment.

Does a longer term make a mortgage cheaper?

It lowers the monthly payment but raises the total cost. The example over 35 years would cost £1,277.79 a month but £266,673 in interest, against £180,224 over 25 years.

Does this include fees, insurance or property tax?

No — it is the loan alone. Arrangement fees can be added to the loan or paid up front; buildings insurance, and in the US property tax, homeowners insurance and any mortgage insurance, are paid on top. The mortgage affordability calculator includes US property tax and insurance.
