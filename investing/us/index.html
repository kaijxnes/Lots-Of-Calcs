Frequently asked questions

How much will $200 a month grow to?

About $30,873 after 10 years and $163,075 after 30, at an example 5% a year after fees — of which $72,000 is what you paid in over 30 years. Each amount's page shows returns from 2% to 10%.

What could $10,000 grow to?

About $16,289 in 10 years and $43,219 in 30 at 5% a year, if the returns are left to compound.

What return should I expect?

No one knows: shares have averaged more than cash over long periods but can fall sharply, and returns aren't steady from year to year. The tables use a range so you can see the difference a percentage point makes; treat the figures as illustrations, not forecasts.

Do fees and inflation matter?

A lot, over decades: a 1% yearly fee can take a sixth to a quarter off what you end up with after 30 years, and inflation means a future pound buys less than today's. Each amount's page shows both; the fund fee drag calculator goes into fees in detail.
