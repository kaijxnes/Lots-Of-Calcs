How the Child Maintenance Service works it out

It starts from the paying parent's yearly gross income from HMRC (employment, self-employment and pension income), divides it by 365 and multiplies by 7 to get a weekly figure. Pension contributions not already taken off through the payroll can be deducted if the paying parent asks. Anything over £3,000 a week is ignored. Then one of these rates applies:

Gross weekly incomeRateWeekly amount
Not knownDefault£38 for 1 child, £51 for 2, £64 for 3 or more
Below £7Nil£0
£7 to £100, or on benefitsFlat£7
£100.01 to £199.99Reduced£7 plus a percentage of income over £100
£200 to £3,000Basic12%, 16%, 19% of the first £800 for 1, 2 or 3+ children, then 9%, 12%, 15%

Other children who live with the paying parent reduce the income counted at the basic rate by 11% for one, 14% for two and 16% for three or more. At the reduced rate, the percentage is 17% for one child, falling to 12.4% with three or more other children (31% to 23.8% for three or more children).

Shared care only changes the reduced and basic rates, and can't take the amount below £7 a week. If the paying parent gets benefits and a child stays 52 nights or more, nothing is paid for that child. Either parent can ask for extra income, assets or expenses to be taken into account (a variation).

Frequently asked questions

How is child maintenance calculated?

From the paying parent's gross income: yearly income ÷ 365 × 7 gives a weekly figure. From £200 a week, it's 12% for 1 child, 16% for 2 and 19% for 3 or more on the first £800, then 9%, 12%, 15% above, up to £3,000 a week. Other children and shared care reduce it.

How much child maintenance do you pay on £30,000?

About £69.04 a week (£299.17 a month) for 1 child, £92.05 for 2 and £109.31 for 3, with no shared care or other children.

What's the minimum child maintenance?

£7 a week: the flat rate, paid on gross income of £7 to £100 a week or on most benefits. Below £7 a week it's nil.

How does shared care reduce child maintenance?

From 52 nights a year, one-seventh comes off for each child; two-sevenths from 104 nights, three-sevenths from 156, and half plus £7 a week from 175. It can't go below £7 a week.

Is child maintenance based on gross or net income?

Gross: income before tax and National Insurance, as HMRC has it. Pension contributions can be taken off, and anything over £3,000 a week is ignored (the receiving parent can apply to the courts for more).
