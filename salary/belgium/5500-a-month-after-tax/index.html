How €5,500 a month after tax is worked out

Tax is worked out on the year, so the monthly figure is multiplied by twelve — €66,000 — the deductions are worked out on that, and the take-home is divided back by twelve. The yearly amounts below are for the whole year.

If your pay includes a 13th month or holiday pay on top of twelve monthly salaries, these figures leave them out: add them to the yearly salary in the calculator above for the full picture.

Professional expenses (lump sum): €6,070.00. 30% of pay after social security, up to €6,070.

Social security (RSZ/ONSS): €8,626.20. 13.07% of gross pay, with no ceiling.

Income tax (federal and regional): €16,227.40. 25% to €16,720, 40% to €29,510, 45% to €51,070 and 50% above, less 25% of the €11,550 tax-free amount (€2,887.50).

Municipal surcharge: €1,171.62. A percentage of the income tax set by each municipality: 7.22% on average in Flanders for 2026.

Special social security contribution (BBSZ): €660.12. The withholding scale for someone taxed alone: nothing on pay up to €1,945.38 a month, then a set amount plus 4.22%, 1.10%, 3.38% and 1.10% bands, at most €182.82 a quarter (€731.28 a year).

Frequently asked questions

How much is €5,500 a month after tax in Belgium?

€3,276.22 a month. €5,500 a month is €66,000 a year before tax; after income tax and social contributions that leaves €39,314.66 a year, or €756.05 a week.

How much is €5,500 a month a year?

€66,000 before tax — twelve times the monthly figure. Salaries are usually quoted per year, so this is the number to compare with a job advert.

How much tax do I pay on €5,500 a month?

About €2,223.78 a month, 40.4% of the gross, counting every deduction on this page. The next unit you earn is taxed at 61%.

Why might my payslip show a different amount?

Withholding in a single month can differ from a twelfth of the annual figure — for example in the first month of a job, after a pay rise, or where bonuses and one-off payments are taxed differently. Pension contributions and other deductions specific to you also come off a real payslip.

What does this leave out?

Werkbonus and the low-wage tax credit, which cut tax and contributions on lower salaries; Tax-free supplements for children and dependants; Meal vouchers, company cars and other benefits; Brussels and Walloon municipalities; The final settlement of the special social security contribution on the tax assessment, which uses household income; Pension savings, service vouchers and other tax reductions.
