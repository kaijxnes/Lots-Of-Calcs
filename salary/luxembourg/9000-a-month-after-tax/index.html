How €9,000 a month after tax is worked out

Tax is worked out on the year, so the monthly figure is multiplied by twelve — €108,000 — the deductions are worked out on that, and the take-home is divided back by twelve. The yearly amounts below are for the whole year.

Taxable income: €93,109.21. Pay less social contributions, the €540 minimum allowance for work expenses and the €480 minimum allowance for special expenses.

Health and pension insurance: €12,474.00. 11.55% (pension 8.50%, health 2.80%, cash sickness benefits 0.25%) of pay up to five times the minimum wage: €13,518.68 a month to May and €13,856.63 from June 2026 (€164,589.81 for the year).

Dependency insurance: €1,396.79. 1.4% of all pay, with no ceiling, after an allowance of a quarter of the minimum wage each month (€675.93 to May, €692.83 from June: €8,229.46 for the year).

Income tax (class 1): €24,077.09. Nothing to €13,230, then 8% rising in steps of 1–2 points to 39% from €54,090, 40% from €117,450, 41% from €176,160 and 42% above €234,870.

Solidarity surcharge (employment fund): €1,685.40. 7% of the income tax, or 9% when taxable income is over €150,000.

Why might my payslip show a different amount?

Withholding in a single month can differ from a twelfth of the annual figure — for example in the first month of a job, after a pay rise, or where bonuses and one-off payments are taxed differently. Pension contributions and other deductions specific to you also come off a real payslip.
