How €5,500 a month after tax is worked out

Tax is worked out on the year, so the monthly figure is multiplied by twelve — €66,000 — the deductions are worked out on that, and the take-home is divided back by twelve. The yearly amounts below are for the whole year.

Taxable income: €56,548.21. Pay less social contributions, the €540 minimum allowance for work expenses and the €480 minimum allowance for special expenses.

Health and pension insurance: €7,623.00. 11.55% (pension 8.50%, health 2.80%, cash sickness benefits 0.25%) of pay up to five times the minimum wage: €13,518.68 a month to May and €13,856.63 from June 2026 (€164,589.81 for the year).

Dependency insurance: €808.79. 1.4% of all pay, with no ceiling, after an allowance of a quarter of the minimum wage each month (€675.93 to May, €692.83 from June: €8,229.46 for the year).

Income tax: €9,818.30. The tariff: nothing to €13,230, then 8% rising in steps to 39% from €54,090, 40% from €117,450, 41% from €176,160 and 42% above €234,870. Class 2 pays twice the tax on half the income; class 1a applies the tariff to income less a quarter of its shortfall from €79,380, with the rise above €51,804 capped at 39–42%.

Solidarity surcharge (employment fund): €687.28. 7% of the income tax, or 9% when taxable income is over €150,000 (€300,000 in class 2).

Employee tax credit (CIS): −€210.00. €300 plus 2.9% of pay over €936 up to €600 at €11,266; €600 to €40,000, then down 1.5 cents per euro to nothing at €80,000. Paid even when there is no tax to set it against.

CO2 tax credit (CI-CO2): −€75.60. €216 up to €40,000 of pay, then down 0.54 cents per euro to nothing at €80,000.

Why might my payslip show a different amount?

Withholding in a single month can differ from a twelfth of the annual figure — for example in the first month of a job, after a pay rise, or where bonuses and one-off payments are taxed differently. Pension contributions and other deductions specific to you also come off a real payslip.
