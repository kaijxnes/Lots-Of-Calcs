What comes out of pay in Luxembourg

Taxable income: €42,620.21. Pay less social contributions, the €540 minimum allowance for work expenses and the €480 minimum allowance for special expenses.

Health and pension insurance: €5,775.00. 11.55% (pension 8.50%, health 2.80%, cash sickness benefits 0.25%) of pay up to five times the minimum wage: €13,518.68 a month to May and €13,856.63 from June 2026 (€164,589.81 for the year).

Dependency insurance: €584.79. 1.4% of all pay, with no ceiling, after an allowance of a quarter of the minimum wage each month (€675.93 to May, €692.83 from June: €8,229.46 for the year).

Income tax: €4,959.66. The tariff: nothing to €13,230, then 8% rising in steps to 39% from €54,090, 40% from €117,450, 41% from €176,160 and 42% above €234,870. Class 2 pays twice the tax on half the income; class 1a applies the tariff to income less a quarter of its shortfall from €79,380, with the rise above €51,804 capped at 39–42%.

Solidarity surcharge (employment fund): €347.18. 7% of the income tax, or 9% when taxable income is over €150,000 (€300,000 in class 2).

Employee tax credit (CIS): −€450.00. €300 plus 2.9% of pay over €936 up to €600 at €11,266; €600 to €40,000, then down 1.5 cents per euro to nothing at €80,000. Paid even when there is no tax to set it against.

CO2 tax credit (CI-CO2): −€162.00. €216 up to €40,000 of pay, then down 0.54 cents per euro to nothing at €80,000.

Frequently asked questions

How much is €50,000 after tax in Luxembourg?

€38,945.37 a year, or €3,245.45 a month, after income tax and social contributions. The deductions are health and pension insurance €5,775.00, dependency insurance €584.79, income tax €4,959.66, solidarity surcharge (employment fund) €347.18, employee tax credit (CIS) −€450.00, CO2 tax credit (CI-CO2) −€162.00 — 22.1% of the gross in all, and the next unit earned is taxed at 43%.

How much social security do employees pay in Luxembourg?

11.55% of pay: 8.50% for pension, 2.80% for health care and 0.25% for sickness cash benefits, on pay up to five times the minimum wage (€13,518.68 a month to May 2026, €13,856.63 from June). Dependency insurance adds 1.4% of all pay after an allowance of a quarter of the minimum wage each month.

Which tax class does this use?

Class 1 — single, no children — unless you pick another in the calculator. Class 2 (married or civil partners taxed jointly) pays twice the tax on half the income, which is much less on a single salary; class 1a (single parents, widowed, or 64 and over) also pays less, and single parents can add the single-parent credit of up to €3,504.

What tax credits do employees get?

The employee tax credit (CIS) is €600 a year on pay from €11,266 to €40,000, less below that, and falls to nothing at €80,000. The CO2 credit (CI-CO2) is €216 up to €40,000 and also falls to nothing at €80,000. Both are paid even if there is no tax to set them against.

What is the solidarity surcharge?

A contribution to the employment fund of 7% of your income tax, rising to 9% when taxable income is over €150,000 in class 1.

Does this work for cross-border workers?

No — these figures are for residents. Cross-border workers are taxed as non-residents, which can change their tax class and the final bill, and the tax treaties with France, Belgium and Germany also apply.

What does this leave out?

Child tax reductions (modération d'impôt pour enfants), given through family allowances; Reduction of the single-parent credit for maintenance or allowances the child receives above €2,712 a year; Couples where both partners earn (class 2 with two salaries); The temporary tax credit paid from June to December 2026 (crédit d'impôt conjoncture); Cross-border workers and non-resident tax; Travel expense allowance (frais de déplacement) and actual expenses; Rounding in the official tax tables and any smoothing at the solidarity surcharge threshold; Benefits in kind and bonuses taxed at special rates.
