What comes out of pay in Portugal

IRS Jovem exempt pay: €0.00. 100% of work income in the first year, 75% in years 2–4, 50% in years 5–7 and 25% in years 8–10, up to 55 × IAS (€29,542.15 in 2026) (CIRS art. 12.º-B).

Taxable income (rendimento coletável): €44,500.00. Pay less the specific deduction: €4,587.09 (8.54 × the 2026 social support index), or social security paid if more.

Social security (Segurança Social): €5,500.00. 11% of pay, with no ceiling.

Income tax (IRS): €11,264.89. 2026 rates after the October 2026 cut (backdated to January). With IRS Jovem, the rate on all pay applies only to the part that is not exempt. Less the deduction for dependants. Tax may not leave less than the €12,880 subsistence minimum.

Additional solidarity rate: €0.00. 2.5% of taxable income between €80,000 and €250,000, 5% above.

Frequently asked questions

How much is €50,000 after tax in Portugal?

€33,235.11 a year, or €2,769.59 a month, after income tax and social contributions. The deductions are Social security (Segurança Social) €5,500.00, income tax (IRS) €11,264.89 — 33.5% of the gross in all, and the next unit earned is taxed at 49%.

What does this leave out?

Non-habitual resident and IFICI regimes; Personal deductions (general family expenses, health, education, housing); The Azores and Madeira regional rates; The €126 extra for an only child under 3, and shared-custody halves of the dependant deduction; Other deductions from tax (general family expenses, health, education, housing) still apply to IRS Jovem claimants as usual.
