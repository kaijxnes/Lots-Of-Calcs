What comes out of pay in Japan

Basic deduction: ¥1,040,000. ¥1,040,000 for total income up to ¥4.89 million, ¥670,000 up to ¥6.55 million, ¥620,000 up to ¥23.5 million, then tapering away.

Employment income (after the employment income deduction): ¥3,560,000. The employment income deduction has a minimum of ¥740,000 for 2026 and 2027.

Health insurance: ¥246,250. Half of Kyōkai Kenpo's Tokyo rate (9.85%), plus half of the 1.62% long-term care rate for those aged 40 to 64.

Child and childcare support levy: ¥4,312. Half of 0.23%, from April 2026 (nine months of the year).

Employees' pension insurance: ¥457,500. Half of 18.3%, on monthly pay up to ¥650,000.

Employment insurance: ¥25,000. 0.5% for general businesses in FY2026.

Income tax (including reconstruction tax): ¥91,175. 5% to ¥1.95 million, then 10%, 20%, 23%, 33%, 40% and 45% above ¥40 million, plus the 2.1% special reconstruction income tax.

Resident tax (jūminzei): ¥242,100. 10% of taxable income less the ¥2,500 adjustment credit, plus the ¥5,000 per-capita levy (including the forest environment tax). It is charged in the following year; shown here as if the salary were the same both years.

About these figures

They use the 2026 tax year, which runs from 1 January 2026 to 31 December 2026. The rates have not yet been checked by a person against the official sources — check them here before relying on them. This is an estimate, not a payslip.

Assumes:

  • Salaried employee in Tokyo enrolled in Kyōkai Kenpo
  • Paid in 12 equal monthly amounts with no bonus
  • Single, no dependants

Leaves out:

  • Bonuses (social insurance and withholding work differently on them)
  • Spouse and dependant deductions
  • Furusato nōzei and other deductions
  • Corporate health insurance societies, whose rates differ

Not yet confirmed for this year:

  • Tokyo's 2026 health insurance rate of 9.85% comes from a third-party summary; Kyōkai Kenpo's own table confirms only that it fell from 9.91%
