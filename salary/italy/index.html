What comes out of pay in Italy

Social security (INPS): €4,595.00. 9.19%, plus 1% on pay above €56,224, on pay up to €122,295 (the ceiling for anyone who started contributing after 1995).

Income tax (IRPEF): €11,784.72. 23% up to €28,000, 33% to €50,000 (cut from 35% in 2026) and 43% above, less the tax credits.

Wage-cost relief payment: €0.00. Below €20,000 the relief is paid as a tax-free sum: 7.1% of income up to €8,500, 5.3% up to €15,000 and 4.8% up to €20,000.

Regional surcharge (addizionale regionale): €689.27. Lombardy: 1.23% to €15,000, 1.58% to €28,000, 1.72% to €50,000, 1.73% above.

Municipal surcharge (addizionale comunale): €363.24. Milan: 0.8% of all taxable income, with incomes up to €23,000 exempt.

About these figures

They use the 2026 tax year, which runs from 1 January 2026 to 31 December 2026. The rates have not yet been checked by a person against the official sources — check them here before relying on them. This is an estimate, not a payslip.

Assumes:

  • A private-sector employee in the main pension fund (FPLD) who started contributing after 1995
  • No dependants
  • Employment income only

Leaves out:

  • Surcharges for regions and cities other than Milan, which typically add 1.5–4% of taxable income (Rome's 2026 regional table could not be confirmed)
  • Dependant tax credits and the single allowance for children (assegno unico)
  • The supplementary payment (trattamento integrativo) of up to €1,200 for incomes up to €15,000
  • Severance pay accrual (TFR), which is not deducted from pay
  • Joint filing — Italy taxes each person separately

Not yet confirmed for this year:

  • Lombardy's regional bands and Milan's 0.8% with a €23,000 exemption come from secondary sources that broadly agree; Lombardy's top rate is given as 1.73% or 1.74%
