What comes out of pay in Italy

Taxable income (reddito imponibile): €45,405.00. Pay less INPS and deductible pension fund contributions.

Social security (INPS): €4,595.00. 9.19%, plus 1% on pay above €56,224, on pay up to €122,295 (the ceiling for anyone who started contributing after 1995).

Income tax (IRPEF): €11,784.72. 23% up to €28,000, 33% to €50,000 (cut from 35% in 2026) and 43% above, less the tax credits.

Regional surcharge (addizionale regionale): €689.27. Lombardy: 1.23% to €15,000, 1.58% to €28,000, 1.72% to €50,000, 1.73% above.

Municipal surcharge (addizionale comunale): €363.24. Milan: 0.8% of all taxable income, with incomes up to €23,000 exempt.

Frequently asked questions

How much is €50,000 after tax in Italy?

€32,567.77 a year, or €2,713.98 a month, after income tax and social contributions. The deductions are Social security (INPS) €4,595.00, income tax (IRPEF) €11,784.72, regional surcharge (addizionale regionale) €689.27, municipal surcharge (addizionale comunale) €363.24 — 34.9% of the gross in all, and the next unit earned is taxed at 49%.

What does this leave out?

Surcharges for regions and cities other than Milan, which typically add 1.5–4% of taxable income (Rome's 2026 regional table could not be confirmed); Dependant tax credits and the single allowance for children (assegno unico); The supplementary payment (trattamento integrativo) of up to €1,200 for incomes up to €15,000; Severance pay accrual (TFR), which is not deducted from pay; Joint filing — Italy taxes each person separately; The extra deduction for workers in their first job (contributions below the limit in the first five years).
