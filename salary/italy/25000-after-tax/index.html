How €25,000 after tax is worked out

Social security (INPS): €2,297.50. 9.19%, plus 1% on pay above €56,224, on pay up to €122,295 (the ceiling for anyone who started contributing after 1995).

Income tax (IRPEF): €1,826.65. 23% up to €28,000, 33% to €50,000 (cut from 35% in 2026) and 43% above, less the tax credits.

Wage-cost relief payment: €0.00. Below €20,000 the relief is paid as a tax-free sum: 7.1% of income up to €8,500, 5.3% up to €15,000 and 4.8% up to €20,000.

Regional surcharge (addizionale regionale): €306.20. Lombardy: 1.23% to €15,000, 1.58% to €28,000, 1.72% to €50,000, 1.73% above.

Municipal surcharge (addizionale comunale): €0.00. Milan: 0.8% of all taxable income, with incomes up to €23,000 exempt.

Frequently asked questions

How much is €25,000 after tax in Italy?

A €25,000 salary in Italy leaves €20,569.65 a year after income tax and social contributions, which is €1,714.14 a month or about €395.57 a week. The deductions are social security (INPS) €2,297.50, income tax (IRPEF) €1,826.65, regional surcharge (addizionale regionale) €306.20 — €4,430.35 in all, or 17.7% of the gross. That is for Regional and municipal surcharges: Milan (Lombardy).

How much is €25,000 a month after tax in Italy?

€1,714.14 a month — the annual take-home of €20,569.65 divided by twelve. Actual pay slips can differ where pay is split into more than twelve instalments or where withholding is settled at the end of the year.

What is the marginal tax rate on €25,000 in Italy?

40% — that is what is taken from the next unit you earn, against 17.7% of the salary overall. The marginal rate is the one that matters when weighing a pay rise or overtime.

What does this leave out?

Surcharges for regions and cities other than Milan, which typically add 1.5–4% of taxable income (Rome's 2026 regional table could not be confirmed); Dependant tax credits and the single allowance for children (assegno unico); The supplementary payment (trattamento integrativo) of up to €1,200 for incomes up to €15,000; Severance pay accrual (TFR), which is not deducted from pay; Joint filing — Italy taxes each person separately.
