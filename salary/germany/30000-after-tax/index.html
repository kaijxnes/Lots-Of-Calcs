How €30,000 after tax is worked out

Taxable income (zu versteuerndes Einkommen): €22,704.00. After the €1,230 employee allowance for work expenses, the €36 (€72 married) special expenses allowance, and insurance contributions.

Pension insurance (Rentenversicherung): €2,790.00. Half of 18.6%, on earnings up to €101,400.

Unemployment insurance (Arbeitslosenversicherung): €390.00. Half of 2.6%, on earnings up to €101,400.

Health insurance (Krankenversicherung): €2,625.00. Half of the 14.6% general rate plus half of the 2.9% average additional contribution, on earnings up to €69,750. Each fund sets its own additional contribution.

Long-term care insurance (Pflegeversicherung): €720.00. 1.8% (half of 3.6%) plus the 0.6% surcharge for childless employees aged 23 or over, on earnings up to €69,750.

Income tax (Einkommensteuer): €2,252.00. § 32a EStG 2026: nothing up to €12,348, rising through two progressive zones to 42% from €69,879 and 45% from €277,826.

Solidarity surcharge: €0.00. 5.5% of income tax, but only once the tax exceeds €20,350 (€40,700 married), phased in at 11.9% of the excess.

Church tax (Kirchensteuer): €0.00. Only for members of a church that levies it: 8% of income tax in Bavaria and Baden-Württemberg, 9% elsewhere.

Frequently asked questions

How much is €30,000 after tax in Germany?

A €30,000 salary in Germany leaves €21,223.00 a year after income tax and social contributions, which is €1,768.58 a month or about €408.13 a week. The deductions are pension insurance (Rentenversicherung) €2,790.00, unemployment insurance (Arbeitslosenversicherung) €390.00, health insurance (Krankenversicherung) €2,625.00, long-term care insurance (Pflegeversicherung) €720.00, income tax (Einkommensteuer) €2,252.00 — €8,777.00 in all, or 29.3% of the gross. That is for single (tax class I); Church tax: None.

How much is €30,000 a month after tax in Germany?

€1,768.58 a month — the annual take-home of €21,223.00 divided by twelve. Actual pay slips can differ where pay is split into more than twelve instalments or where withholding is settled at the end of the year.

What is the marginal tax rate on €30,000 in Germany?

22% — that is what is taken from the next unit you earn, against 29.3% of the salary overall. The marginal rate is the one that matters when weighing a pay rise or overtime.

How much is €30,000 after tax in Germany if I'm married?

€23,475.00 a year (€1,956.25 a month) as "Married, one earner (joint assessment)", against €21,223.00 for a single person — a difference of €2,252.00. That assumes one spouse earns the whole salary.

What does this leave out?

Children: child allowances, child benefit (Kindergeld) and the lower care rate for parents; Private health insurance (an option above the €77,400 compulsory insurance threshold); Saxony's different split of care insurance between employer and employee; Work expenses above the €1,230 allowance (commuting, home office) and other deductions; The church tax deduction and church tax caps; Company pension contributions and capital gains.

How accurate are these Germany figures?
