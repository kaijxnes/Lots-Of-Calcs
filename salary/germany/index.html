What comes out of pay in Germany

Taxable income (zu versteuerndes Einkommen): €38,684.00. After the €1,230 employee allowance for work expenses, the €36 (€72 married) special expenses allowance, and insurance contributions.

Pension insurance (Rentenversicherung): €4,650.00. Half of 18.6%, on earnings up to €101,400.

Unemployment insurance (Arbeitslosenversicherung): €650.00. Half of 2.6%, on earnings up to €101,400.

Health insurance (Krankenversicherung): €4,375.00. Half of the 14.6% general rate plus half of the 2.9% average additional contribution, on earnings up to €69,750. Each fund sets its own additional contribution.

Long-term care insurance (Pflegeversicherung): €1,200.00. 1.8% (half of 3.6%) plus the 0.6% surcharge for childless employees aged 23 or over, on earnings up to €69,750.

Income tax (Einkommensteuer): €6,796.00. § 32a EStG 2026: nothing up to €12,348, rising through two progressive zones to 42% from €69,879 and 45% from €277,826.

Solidarity surcharge: €0.00. 5.5% of income tax, but only once the tax exceeds €20,350 (€40,700 married), phased in at 11.9% of the excess.

Church tax (Kirchensteuer): €0.00. Only for members of a church that levies it: 8% of income tax in Bavaria and Baden-Württemberg, 9% elsewhere.

About these figures

They use the 2026 tax year, which runs from 1 January 2026 to 31 December 2026. The rates have not yet been checked by a person against the official sources — check them here before relying on them. This is an estimate, not a payslip.

Assumes:

  • Statutory health insurance at the average additional contribution of 2.9%
  • No children, aged 23 or over (so the childless care surcharge applies)
  • Calculated as the annual income tax assessment; monthly payroll withholding approximates it and the difference is settled by the annual return
  • Married means one spouse earns the whole salary and the couple is jointly assessed

Leaves out:

  • Children: child allowances, child benefit (Kindergeld) and the lower care rate for parents
  • Private health insurance (an option above the €77,400 compulsory insurance threshold)
  • Saxony's different split of care insurance between employer and employee
  • Work expenses above the €1,230 allowance (commuting, home office) and other deductions
  • The church tax deduction and church tax caps
  • Company pension contributions and capital gains
