What comes out of pay in Spain

Personal and family minimum (state): €5,550.00. €5,550 for you, plus €2,400, €2,700, €4,000 and €4,500 for the first, second, third and each further child, and €2,800 more for each child under 3 (arts. 57 and 58).

Taxable base (base liquidable): €44,750.00. Net earnings less the reduction for employment income and any pension plan reduction. Married couples filing jointly take a further €3,400 off. Under the Beckham law it is simply your gross pay: no allowances or reductions.

Social security (Seguridad Social): €3,250.00. 4.70% common contingencies, 1.55% unemployment, 0.10% training and 0.15% intergenerational equity (MEI), on pay up to the maximum base of €5,101.20 a month.

Income tax — state share (IRPF estatal): €5,602.25. The state scale on the base, less the same scale applied to the personal and family minimum. Under the Beckham law the whole tax is 24% of gross pay up to €600,000 and 47% above, with half shown as the state share and half as the regional share, as the Modelo 151 return splits it.

Income tax — regional share (IRPF autonómico): €4,957.54. Each autonomous community sets its own scale for its half of IRPF. Valencia's 2026 scale is the one cut in July 2026 with effect from January (Ley 5/2026). Under the Beckham law the regional scale doesn't apply: this is the other half of the flat 24% / 47%.

Frequently asked questions

How much is €50,000 after tax in Spain?

€36,190.21 a year, or €3,015.85 a month, after income tax and social contributions. The deductions are Social security (Seguridad Social) €3,250.00, income tax — state share (IRPF estatal) €5,602.25, income tax — regional share (IRPF autonómico) €4,957.54 — 27.6% of the gross in all, and the next unit earned is taxed at 40%.

What does this leave out?

Regions other than Madrid, Catalonia, Andalusia and Valencia, and the separate Basque and Navarre systems; Regional and state deductions (rent, family, donations) and higher regional personal minimums; The solidarity contribution on pay above the maximum base (a fraction of 1% for the employee); Employer pension contributions and the higher €8,500 limit for company plans; Regional deductions for children (e.g. Madrid's birth and childcare deductions); Beckham law: savings income (taxed at 19%–30%), foreign income, and the deductions for donations and double taxation.
