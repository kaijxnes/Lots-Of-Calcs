How €80,000 after tax is worked out

Taxable regular pay: €56,048.57. Twelve monthly payments less social insurance and the €132 allowance for work expenses.

Social insurance on monthly pay: €12,390.86. 18.07% (health 3.87%, pension 10.25%, unemployment 2.95%, chamber 0.5%, housing 0.5%) of each of the 12 monthly payments up to €6,930. Unemployment insurance is lower on monthly pay up to €2,630.

Social insurance on the 13th and 14th salaries: €1,950.86. The same rates less the chamber and housing contributions, on up to €13,860.

Income tax on monthly pay (Lohnsteuer): €13,370.63. 2026 tariff: nothing to €13,539, then 20%, 30%, 40%, 48% and 50%, and 55% above €1 million. Less the €496 commuter tax credit (Verkehrsabsetzbetrag) and its €804 supplement for lower incomes.

Income tax on the 13th and 14th salaries: €531.46. Taxed separately: the first €620 free, then 6% up to €25,000, 27% to €50,000, 35.75% to €83,333.

Frequently asked questions

How much is €80,000 after tax in Austria?

A €80,000 salary in Austria leaves €51,756.19 a year after income tax and social contributions, which is €4,313.02 a month or about €995.31 a week. The deductions are social insurance on monthly pay €12,390.86, social insurance on the 13th and 14th salaries €1,950.86, income tax on monthly pay (Lohnsteuer) €13,370.63, income tax on the 13th and 14th salaries €531.46 — €28,243.81 in all, or 35.3% of the gross.

How much is €80,000 a month after tax in Austria?

€4,313.02 a month — the annual take-home of €51,756.19 divided by twelve. Actual pay slips can differ where pay is split into more than twelve instalments or where withholding is settled at the end of the year.

What is the marginal tax rate on €80,000 in Austria?

47% — that is what is taken from the next unit you earn, against 35.3% of the salary overall. The marginal rate is the one that matters when weighing a pay rise or overtime.

What does this leave out?

Family bonus (Familienbonus Plus) and other credits for children or sole earners; Commuter allowance and the higher commuter credit; Refund of social insurance for very low incomes (negative tax); The €2,100 exemption limit for small special payments.

How accurate are these Austria figures?

About these figures

They use the 2026 tax year, which runs from 1 January 2026 to 31 December 2026. The rates have not yet been checked by a person against the official sources — check them here before relying on them. This is an estimate, not a payslip.

Assumes:

  • Salaried employee (Angestellter) paid in 14 equal instalments, the usual Austrian pattern
  • No children; each person is taxed individually
  • No commuter allowance (Pendlerpauschale)

Leaves out:

  • Family bonus (Familienbonus Plus) and other credits for children or sole earners
  • Commuter allowance and the higher commuter credit
  • Refund of social insurance for very low incomes (negative tax)
  • The €2,100 exemption limit for small special payments
