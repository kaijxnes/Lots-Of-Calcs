What comes out of pay in Zurich, Switzerland

Simple state tax (einfache Staatssteuer): CHF 5,072.00. Zurich's 2026 single tariff: nothing on the first CHF 7,000, then 2% rising in steps to 13% above CHF 266,700.

Taxable income (Zurich): CHF 87,800.00. Net salary less the professional costs lump sum and the CHF 2,900 insurance premiums deduction, rounded down to CHF 100.

Taxable income (federal): CHF 88,900.00. Net salary less the professional costs lump sum and the CHF 1,800 insurance premiums deduction, rounded down to CHF 100.

Other professional costs (lump sum): CHF 2,808.00. 3% of net salary, at least CHF 2,000 and at most CHF 4,000 — the same for federal and Zurich tax.

AHV/IV/EO (old age, disability, loss of earnings): CHF 5,300.00. 5.3% of all salary, with no ceiling (the employer pays another 5.3%).

Unemployment insurance (ALV): CHF 1,100.00. 1.1% of salary up to CHF 148,200; nothing on salary above that.

Federal income tax (direkte Bundessteuer): CHF 1,951.75. 2026 single tariff: nothing up to CHF 15,200, then a set amount plus a rate per CHF 100 in each band, rising to 13.2%; from CHF 794,000 a flat 11.5% of all taxable income.

Cantonal and city tax (Zurich): CHF 10,854.08. The simple state tax times the 2026 multipliers: 95% for the canton and 119% for the city of Zurich (214% in all). No church tax.

Frequently asked questions

How much is CHF 100,000 after tax in Zurich, Switzerland?

CHF 80,794.17 a year, or CHF 6,732.85 a month, after income tax and social contributions. The deductions are AHV/IV/EO (old age, disability, loss of earnings) CHF 5,300.00, unemployment insurance (ALV) CHF 1,100.00, federal income tax (direkte Bundessteuer) CHF 1,951.75, cantonal and city tax (Zurich) CHF 10,854.08 — 19.2% of the gross in all, and the next unit earned is taxed at 6%.

Why are these figures for Zurich?

Swiss income tax is charged three times — by the federal government, the canton and the municipality — and cantonal and municipal rates vary a lot across the country. These figures are for the city of Zurich: federal tax, plus Zurich's simple state tax multiplied by 95% for the canton and 119% for the city in 2026. Zug, Basel, Bern and Lausanne have their own pages.

What social contributions come out of Swiss pay?

5.3% for AHV/IV/EO (old age, disability and loss of earnings) on all salary, and 1.1% unemployment insurance (ALV) on salary up to CHF 148,200. Pension fund (BVG) contributions and non-occupational accident insurance also come off most payslips; their rates depend on your employer.

Are pension fund (BVG) contributions included?

Only if you enter your share in the options, as a percentage of your coordinated salary: salary up to CHF 90,720 less the CHF 26,460 coordination deduction (at least CHF 3,780). It is set to 0 by default because the rate depends on your age and your employer's fund. Contributions are deducted before tax.

What deductions are allowed before tax?

Here: the lump sum for professional costs (3% of net salary, between CHF 2,000 and CHF 4,000) and the insurance premiums deduction of CHF 1,800 for federal tax and CHF 2,900 for Zurich tax. Commuting, meals, pillar 3a contributions and other deductions you can claim would lower the tax further.

Do foreign employees pay tax this way?

Swiss citizens and C-permit holders are assessed like this. Most other foreign employees have tax deducted at source (Quellensteuer) using separate tables, which aren't modelled here.

What does this leave out?

Pillar 3a contributions and other personal deductions; Commuting and meal cost deductions; Non-occupational accident insurance (NBU), which depends on the employer's insurer; Daily sickness benefit insurance; Zurich's personal tax and church tax; Tax at source (Quellensteuer) tables; Other cantons and municipalities.

How accurate are these figures?

They apply the 2026 rates and thresholds to a CHF 100,000 salary. The rates come from published sources listed on this page but have not yet been checked by a person against the official tax authority, so confirm them before relying on them. Pensions, credits and deductions specific to you will change the result.

About these figures

They use the 2026 tax year, which runs from 1 January 2026 to 31 December 2026. The rates have not yet been checked by a person against the official sources — check them here before relying on them. This is an estimate, not a payslip. When every rate was last checked · How we check our figures.

Assumes:

  • Single, no children, living and working in the city of Zurich
  • No church tax (members of a recognised church pay about 10% of the simple state tax more)
  • No commuting or meal costs claimed; only the lump sum for other professional costs
  • No occupational pension contribution unless you enter one, though most employees earning over CHF 22,680 pay one
  • Taxed through an ordinary assessment, as Swiss citizens and C-permit holders are (most other foreign employees pay tax at source instead)

Leaves out:

  • Pillar 3a contributions and other personal deductions
  • Commuting and meal cost deductions
  • Non-occupational accident insurance (NBU), which depends on the employer's insurer
  • Daily sickness benefit insurance
  • Zurich's personal tax and church tax
  • Tax at source (Quellensteuer) tables
  • Other cantons and municipalities
