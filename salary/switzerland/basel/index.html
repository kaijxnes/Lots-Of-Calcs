What comes out of pay in Basel, Switzerland

Taxable income (Basel-Stadt): CHF 65,700.00. Net salary less the CHF 4,200 professional costs lump sum, the CHF 4,200 insurance premiums lump sum and the CHF 19,500 social deduction for a single person, rounded down to CHF 100.

Taxable income (federal): CHF 88,900.00. Net salary less the professional costs lump sum and the CHF 1,800 insurance premiums deduction, rounded down to CHF 100.

Other professional costs (lump sum): CHF 2,808.00. 3% of net salary, at least CHF 2,000 and at most CHF 4,000 — the same for federal and Zurich tax.

AHV/IV/EO (old age, disability, loss of earnings): CHF 5,300.00. 5.3% of all salary, with no ceiling (the employer pays another 5.3%).

Unemployment insurance (ALV): CHF 1,100.00. 1.1% of salary up to CHF 148,200; nothing on salary above that.

Federal income tax (direkte Bundessteuer): CHF 1,951.75. 2026 single tariff: nothing up to CHF 15,200, then a set amount plus a rate per CHF 100 in each band, rising to 13.2%; from CHF 794,000 a flat 11.5% of all taxable income.

Cantonal income tax (Basel-Stadt): CHF 13,797.00. Tariff A: 21% up to CHF 212,500, 27.25% up to CHF 316,300 and 28.25% above, charged at 100%. The city of Basel levies no income tax of its own. No church tax.

Frequently asked questions

How much is CHF 100,000 after tax in Basel, Switzerland?

CHF 77,851.25 a year, or CHF 6,487.60 a month, after income tax and social contributions. The deductions are AHV/IV/EO (old age, disability, loss of earnings) CHF 5,300.00, unemployment insurance (ALV) CHF 1,100.00, federal income tax (direkte Bundessteuer) CHF 1,951.75, cantonal income tax (Basel-Stadt) CHF 13,797.00 — 22.1% of the gross in all, and the next unit earned is taxed at 6%.

How is income tax worked out in Basel?

Federal tax is the same everywhere in Switzerland. Basel-Stadt then charges its own income tax: 21% of taxable income up to CHF 212,500, 27.25% up to CHF 316,300 and 28.25% above. The city of Basel levies no income tax of its own, so that's the whole cantonal and local bill.

What deductions are allowed in Basel-Stadt?

Here: a CHF 4,200 lump sum for professional costs, a CHF 4,200 lump sum for insurance premiums and a CHF 19,500 social deduction for a single person. Federal tax uses its own deductions.

Did the amounts change for 2026?

No — the Basel-Stadt government decided there would be no inflation adjustment for 2026, so the tariff and deductions are the ones in force from 2025.

Does this apply to Riehen and Bettingen?

No — those two communes add their own communal income tax. These figures are for the city of Basel.

What does this leave out?

Pillar 3a contributions and other personal deductions; Commuting and meal cost deductions; Non-occupational accident insurance (NBU), which depends on the employer's insurer; Daily sickness benefit insurance; Zurich's personal tax and church tax; Tax at source (Quellensteuer) tables; Other cantons and municipalities; Actual professional costs above the lump sum; Church tax; Riehen and Bettingen, which add their own communal tax.
