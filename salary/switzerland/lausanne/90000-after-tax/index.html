How CHF 90,000 after tax is worked out

Cantonal base tax (Vaud): CHF 6,027.41. Vaud's 2026 tariff: 1% on the first CHF 1,600, rising in steps to 15.5% above CHF 291,700.

Taxable income (Vaud): CHF 76,712.80. Net salary less the professional costs lump sum and CHF 5,000 for health and accident insurance premiums.

Taxable income (federal): CHF 79,900.00. Net salary less the professional costs lump sum and the CHF 1,800 insurance premiums deduction, rounded down to CHF 100.

Other professional costs (lump sum): CHF 2,527.20. 3% of net salary, at least CHF 2,000 and at most CHF 4,000 — the same for federal and Zurich tax.

AHV/IV/EO (old age, disability, loss of earnings): CHF 4,770.00. 5.3% of all salary, with no ceiling (the employer pays another 5.3%).

Unemployment insurance (ALV): CHF 990.00. 1.1% of salary up to CHF 148,200; nothing on salary above that.

Federal income tax (direkte Bundessteuer): CHF 1,372.28. 2026 single tariff: nothing up to CHF 15,200, then a set amount plus a rate per CHF 100 in each band, rising to 13.2%; from CHF 794,000 a flat 11.5% of all taxable income.

Cantonal tax (Vaud): CHF 8,875.36. The base tax times the 155% cantonal coefficient, less the 5% reduction for 2026.

City tax (Lausanne): CHF 4,731.52. 78.5% of the cantonal base tax, Lausanne's rate for 2026. No church tax in Vaud.

Frequently asked questions

How much is CHF 90,000 after tax in Lausanne, Switzerland?

A CHF 90,000 salary in Lausanne, Switzerland leaves CHF 69,260.85 a year after income tax and social contributions, which is CHF 5,771.74 a month or about CHF 1,331.94 a week. The deductions are AHV/IV/EO (old age, disability, loss of earnings) CHF 4,770.00, unemployment insurance (ALV) CHF 990.00, federal income tax (direkte Bundessteuer) CHF 1,372.28, cantonal tax (Vaud) CHF 8,875.36, city tax (Lausanne) CHF 4,731.52 — CHF 20,739.15 in all, or 23.0% of the gross.

How much is CHF 90,000 a month after tax in Lausanne, Switzerland?

CHF 5,771.74 a month — the annual take-home of CHF 69,260.85 divided by twelve. Actual pay slips can differ where pay is split into more than twelve instalments or where withholding is settled at the end of the year.

What is the marginal tax rate on CHF 90,000 in Lausanne, Switzerland?

29% — that is what is taken from the next unit you earn, against 23.0% of the salary overall. The marginal rate is the one that matters when weighing a pay rise or overtime.

What is a 3% pay rise on CHF 90,000 worth after tax in Lausanne, Switzerland?

A 3% rise takes CHF 90,000 to CHF 92,700 — CHF 2,700 more before tax. After deductions you keep CHF 1,774.59 a year, or CHF 147.88 a month.

Assumes:

  • Single, no children, living and working in the city of Zurich
  • No church tax (members of a recognised church pay about 10% of the simple state tax more)
  • No commuting or meal costs claimed; only the lump sum for other professional costs
  • No occupational pension contribution unless you enter one, though most employees earning over CHF 22,680 pay one
  • Taxed through an ordinary assessment, as Swiss citizens and C-permit holders are (most other foreign employees pay tax at source instead)
