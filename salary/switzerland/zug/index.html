What comes out of pay in Zug, Switzerland

Simple cantonal tax (Zug): CHF 3,473.48. Zug's single tariff: 0.5% on the first CHF 1,100, rising in steps to 10%, then 9% and 8% on income over CHF 149,900.

Taxable income (Zug): CHF 74,192.00. Net salary less the professional costs lump sum, the CHF 4,600 insurance premiums deduction and the CHF 12,000 personal deduction for a single person.

Taxable income (federal): CHF 88,900.00. Net salary less the professional costs lump sum and the CHF 1,800 insurance premiums deduction, rounded down to CHF 100.

Other professional costs (lump sum): CHF 2,808.00. 3% of net salary, at least CHF 2,000 and at most CHF 4,000 — the same for federal and Zurich tax.

AHV/IV/EO (old age, disability, loss of earnings): CHF 5,300.00. 5.3% of all salary, with no ceiling (the employer pays another 5.3%).

Unemployment insurance (ALV): CHF 1,100.00. 1.1% of salary up to CHF 148,200; nothing on salary above that.

Federal income tax (direkte Bundessteuer): CHF 1,951.75. 2026 single tariff: nothing up to CHF 15,200, then a set amount plus a rate per CHF 100 in each band, rising to 13.2%; from CHF 794,000 a flat 11.5% of all taxable income.

Cantonal and city tax (Zug): CHF 4,515.52. The simple tax times the 2026 multipliers: 78% for the canton and 52% for the city of Zug (130% in all). No church tax.

Frequently asked questions

How much is CHF 100,000 after tax in Zug, Switzerland?

CHF 87,132.73 a year, or CHF 7,261.06 a month, after income tax and social contributions. The deductions are AHV/IV/EO (old age, disability, loss of earnings) CHF 5,300.00, unemployment insurance (ALV) CHF 1,100.00, federal income tax (direkte Bundessteuer) CHF 1,951.75, cantonal and city tax (Zug) CHF 4,515.52 — 12.9% of the gross in all, and the next unit earned is taxed at 14%.

How is income tax worked out in Zug?

Federal tax is the same everywhere in Switzerland. On top, Zug charges its simple cantonal tax — 0.5% on the first CHF 1,100, rising in steps to 10%, then 9% and 8% on income over CHF 149,900 — multiplied by 78% for the canton (lowered from 82% for 2026 to 2029) and 52% for the city of Zug in 2026.

What deductions are allowed in Zug?

Here: the professional costs lump sum (3% of net salary, between CHF 2,000 and CHF 4,000), the CHF 4,600 insurance premiums deduction and the CHF 12,000 personal deduction for a single person. Federal tax uses its own deductions, as in the rest of Switzerland.

How does Zug compare with Zurich?

On the same salary, the cantonal and city tax in Zug is much lower than in the city of Zurich: compare the two on the Swiss salary pages. Federal tax and social contributions are the same in both.

What social contributions come out of pay?

The same as anywhere in Switzerland: 5.3% for AHV/IV/EO and 1.1% unemployment insurance (ALV) on salary up to CHF 148,200, plus pension fund (BVG) contributions and accident insurance that depend on your employer.

What does this leave out?

Pillar 3a contributions and other personal deductions; Commuting and meal cost deductions; Non-occupational accident insurance (NBU), which depends on the employer's insurer; Daily sickness benefit insurance; Zurich's personal tax and church tax; Tax at source (Quellensteuer) tables; Other cantons and municipalities; Zug's tenant deduction (30% of rent, up to CHF 10,800); Church tax; Other Zug municipalities.
