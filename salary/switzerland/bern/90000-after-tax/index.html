How CHF 90,000 after tax is worked out

Simple tax (Bern): CHF 2,986.19. Bern's 2026 tariff for single people: 1.95% on the first CHF 3,300, rising in steps to 6.5% on income above CHF 470,600.

Taxable income (Bern): CHF 71,562.80. Net salary less the professional costs lump sum, the CHF 2,450 insurance premiums deduction, the CHF 5,300 general deduction and CHF 2,400 for someone living alone in their own household.

Taxable income (federal): CHF 79,900.00. Net salary less the professional costs lump sum and the CHF 1,800 insurance premiums deduction, rounded down to CHF 100.

Other professional costs (lump sum): CHF 2,527.20. 3% of net salary, at least CHF 2,000 and at most CHF 4,000 — the same for federal and Zurich tax.

AHV/IV/EO (old age, disability, loss of earnings): CHF 4,770.00. 5.3% of all salary, with no ceiling (the employer pays another 5.3%).

Unemployment insurance (ALV): CHF 990.00. 1.1% of salary up to CHF 148,200; nothing on salary above that.

Federal income tax (direkte Bundessteuer): CHF 1,372.28. 2026 single tariff: nothing up to CHF 15,200, then a set amount plus a rate per CHF 100 in each band, rising to 13.2%; from CHF 794,000 a flat 11.5% of all taxable income.

Cantonal and city tax (Bern): CHF 13,482.65. The simple tax times the 2026 multipliers: 2.975 for the canton and 1.54 for the city of Bern (4.515 in all). No church tax.

Frequently asked questions

How much is CHF 90,000 after tax in Bern, Switzerland?

A CHF 90,000 salary in Bern, Switzerland leaves CHF 69,385.07 a year after income tax and social contributions, which is CHF 5,782.09 a month or about CHF 1,334.33 a week. The deductions are AHV/IV/EO (old age, disability, loss of earnings) CHF 4,770.00, unemployment insurance (ALV) CHF 990.00, federal income tax (direkte Bundessteuer) CHF 1,372.28, cantonal and city tax (Bern) CHF 13,482.65 — CHF 20,614.93 in all, or 22.9% of the gross.

How much is CHF 90,000 a month after tax in Bern, Switzerland?

CHF 5,782.09 a month — the annual take-home of CHF 69,385.07 divided by twelve. Actual pay slips can differ where pay is split into more than twelve instalments or where withholding is settled at the end of the year.

What is the marginal tax rate on CHF 90,000 in Bern, Switzerland?

27% — that is what is taken from the next unit you earn, against 22.9% of the salary overall. The marginal rate is the one that matters when weighing a pay rise or overtime.

What is a 3% pay rise on CHF 90,000 worth after tax in Bern, Switzerland?

A 3% rise takes CHF 90,000 to CHF 92,700 — CHF 2,700 more before tax. After deductions you keep CHF 1,829.93 a year, or CHF 152.49 a month.
