What changes on 6 April 2027

For pay, the Budget changed the Plan 5 student loan threshold (£25,000 to £26,025). Everything else stays as it is: the Personal Allowance of £12,570, the £37,700 basic rate band, the additional rate from £125,140, and the National Insurance thresholds (£12,570 and £50,270 a year) and rates (8% and 2%). So unless you're on a Plan 5 student loan, you'll take home the same from the same salary, and with the thresholds frozen a pay rise is taxed as it would be now.

Plan 5 student loans

Plan 5 repayments are 9% of what you earn over the threshold, which rises from £25,000 to £26,025. That's £92.25 a year less to repay for anyone earning over £26,025: on £40,000, £1,257.75 instead of £1,350.00, so £92.25 more take-home.

Frequently asked questions

Will my take-home pay change in 2027/28?

Not unless you have a Plan 5 student loan. The Personal Allowance (£12,570), income tax bands and National Insurance thresholds stay the same from 6 April 2027, so the same salary takes home the same. On £35,000 that's £28,719.60 a year in England, Wales and Northern Ireland.

What is the Personal Allowance for 2027/28?

£12,570, the same as 2026/27. It's reduced by £1 for every £2 of income over £100,000.

What are the income tax bands for 2027/28?

20% on the first £37,700 above the Personal Allowance, 40% above that and 45% over £125,140: the same as 2026/27 (England, Wales and Northern Ireland).

What is the Plan 5 student loan threshold for 2027/28?

£26,025, up from £25,000. On £40,000 that's £92.25 a year less to repay.

Are the 2027/28 figures final?

They're the rates announced at the Budget, for the tax year starting 6 April 2027. Plan 1 (£26,900) and Plan 4 (£33,795) student loan thresholds are the 2026/27 figures: the 2027/28 ones haven't been announced yet.
