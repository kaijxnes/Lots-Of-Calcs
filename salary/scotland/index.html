What comes out of pay in Scotland

Personal allowance: £12,570.00. Withdrawn by £1 for every £2 of income over £100,000, which is what creates the 60% effective band up to £125,140. The £100,000 taper is measured on adjusted net income, which pension contributions reduce.

Scottish income tax: £4,501.07. Replaces the rest-of-UK bands. Thresholds are on taxable income: with the full £12,570 allowance the bands start at £12,571, £16,538, £29,527, £43,663, £75,001 and £125,141 of gross pay. The personal allowance and National Insurance are set UK-wide and are unchanged.

National Insurance: £1,794.40. Class 1 employee contributions, charged on gross pay rather than taxable income. Weekly thresholds of £241.73 and £966.73 are £12,570 and £50,270 a year.

About these figures

They use the 2026/27 tax year, which runs from 6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027. The rates have not yet been checked by a person against the official sources — check them here before relying on them. This is an estimate, not a payslip. When every rate was last checked.

Assumes:

  • Standard tax code (1257L) and personal allowance
  • Employment income only, paid evenly through the year
  • National Insurance category A
  • No student or postgraduate loan unless one is chosen; repayments worked out on the year's earnings, where payroll works per pay period
  • No pension contribution unless one is entered

Leaves out:

  • Tax codes other than the standard personal allowance
  • Benefits in kind, salary sacrifice and taxable expenses
  • Blind person's allowance, marriage allowance and other reliefs
  • Extra relief on relief-at-source pensions for higher-rate taxpayers (claimed through self assessment)
  • Employer pension contributions, and auto-enrolment's qualifying earnings band (contributions are a percentage of the whole salary)
  • Pension contributions (workplace or salary sacrifice)
  • Student loan and postgraduate loan repayments
