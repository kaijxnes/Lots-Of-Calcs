How £4,500 a month after tax is worked out

Tax is worked out on the year, so the monthly figure is multiplied by twelve — £54,000 — the deductions are worked out on that, and the take-home is divided back by twelve. The yearly amounts below are for the whole year.

Personal allowance: £12,570.00. Withdrawn by £1 for every £2 of income over £100,000, which is what creates the 60% effective band up to £125,140.

Scottish income tax: £10,662.05. Replaces the rest-of-UK bands. Thresholds are on taxable income: with the full £12,570 allowance the bands start at £12,571, £16,538, £29,527, £43,663, £75,001 and £125,141 of gross pay. The personal allowance and National Insurance are set UK-wide and are unchanged.

National Insurance: £3,090.60. Class 1 employee contributions, charged on gross pay rather than taxable income. Weekly thresholds of £241.73 and £966.73 are £12,570 and £50,270 a year.

Why might my payslip show a different amount?

PAYE works cumulatively across the tax year, so the first month of a new job, a mid-year pay rise or an emergency tax code can make one month's figure differ. Pension contributions, student loan repayments and benefits in kind also come off a real payslip.

What does this leave out?

Pension contributions (workplace or salary sacrifice); Student loan and postgraduate loan repayments; Tax codes other than the standard personal allowance; Benefits in kind, salary sacrifice and taxable expenses; Blind person's allowance, marriage allowance and other reliefs.
