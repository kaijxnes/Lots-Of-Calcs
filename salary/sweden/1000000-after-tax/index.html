A salary of kr 1,000,000 in Sweden leaves kr 666,304.44 a year once income tax and social contributions are taken off — kr 55,525.37 a month. The deductions come to kr 333,695.56, or 33.4% of the gross, and the next unit earned is taxed at 0%.

How kr 1,000,000 after tax is worked out

Basic allowance (grundavdrag): kr 17,400.00. Depends on income: up to 0.77 × the price base amount (SEK 59,200 in 2026), falling to 0.293 × at high incomes.

General pension contribution (allmän pensionsavgift): kr 47,100.00. 7% of income up to 8.07 × the income base amount (SEK 673,038). It is credited back against tax, so on its own it costs nothing.

Income tax: kr 286,595.56. Municipal and state tax, less the pension contribution credit and the earned income tax credit.

Frequently asked questions

How much is kr 1,000,000 after tax in Sweden?

A kr 1,000,000 salary in Sweden leaves kr 666,304.44 a year after income tax and social contributions, which is kr 55,525.37 a month or about kr 12,813.55 a week. The deductions are general pension contribution (allmän pensionsavgift) kr 47,100.00, income tax kr 286,595.56 — kr 333,695.56 in all, or 33.4% of the gross.

How much is kr 1,000,000 a month after tax in Sweden?

kr 55,525.37 a month — the annual take-home of kr 666,304.44 divided by twelve. Actual pay slips can differ where pay is split into more than twelve instalments or where withholding is settled at the end of the year.

What is the marginal tax rate on kr 1,000,000 in Sweden?

0% — that is what is taken from the next unit you earn, against 33.4% of the salary overall. The marginal rate is the one that matters when weighing a pay rise or overtime.

What does this leave out?

Individual municipal rates; Burial fee (about 0.3%) and church fee for Church of Sweden members; Public service fee (1% of income, capped at about SEK 1,250); Deductions for travel and other work expenses.
