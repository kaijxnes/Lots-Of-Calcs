A salary of kr 300,000 in Denmark leaves kr 203,063.65 a year once income tax and social contributions are taken off — kr 16,921.97 a month. The deductions come to kr 96,936.35, or 32.3% of the gross, and the next unit earned is taxed at 38%.

How kr 300,000 after tax is worked out

ATP pension contribution: kr 1,193.76. DKK 99.48 a month for full-time employees.

Labour market contribution (AM-bidrag): kr 23,904.50. 8% of pay after ATP.

Municipal tax (kommuneskat): kr 45,319.80. The national average rate, on taxable income above the DKK 54,100 personal allowance. Rates range from about 23.4% to 26.3%.

Church tax (kirkeskat): kr 0.00. Members of the Church of Denmark only; about 0.87% on average.

State tax (bundskat, mellemskat, topskat): kr 26,518.29. Bottom tax 12.01% above the allowance; middle tax 7.5% above DKK 641,200; top tax 7.5% above DKK 777,900; top-top tax 5% above DKK 2,592,700 (the new 2026 structure).

Frequently asked questions

How much is kr 300,000 after tax in Denmark?

A kr 300,000 salary in Denmark leaves kr 203,063.65 a year after income tax and social contributions, which is kr 16,921.97 a month or about kr 3,905.07 a week. The deductions are aTP pension contribution kr 1,193.76, labour market contribution (AM-bidrag) kr 23,904.50, municipal tax (kommuneskat) kr 45,319.80, state tax (bundskat, mellemskat, topskat) kr 26,518.29 — kr 96,936.35 in all, or 32.3% of the gross. That is for Municipality: National average; Church member: No.

How much is kr 300,000 a month after tax in Denmark?

kr 16,921.97 a month — the annual take-home of kr 203,063.65 divided by twelve. Actual pay slips can differ where pay is split into more than twelve instalments or where withholding is settled at the end of the year.

What is the marginal tax rate on kr 300,000 in Denmark?

38% — that is what is taken from the next unit you earn, against 32.3% of the salary overall. The marginal rate is the one that matters when weighing a pay rise or overtime.

What does this leave out?

Individual municipal tax rates; Labour-market and private pension contributions; Senior and single-parent employment deductions; Interest, union dues, commuting and other deductions.
