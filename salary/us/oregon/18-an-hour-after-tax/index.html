How $18 an hour after tax is worked out

The hourly rate is turned into a yearly salary first — $18 × 40 hours × 52 weeks = $37,440 — because tax is worked out on yearly income. The take-home is then spread back over the year.

Federal tax subtraction: $2,312.80. Federal income tax paid is deductible up to $8,750, phasing out from $125,000 ($250,000 married).

Federal income tax: $2,312.80.

Social Security: $2,321.28. 6.2% of wages up to the 2026 wage base of $184,500.

Medicare: $542.88.

Additional Medicare tax: $0.00. 0.9% on wages above $200,000 ($250,000 married filing jointly). Employers withhold it from $200,000 whatever the status; the threshold here is the liability.

Oregon income tax: $2,250.57. Less the $256 exemption credit each, for incomes up to $100,000 ($200,000 married).

Statewide transit tax: $37.44.

Paid Leave Oregon: $224.64. 60% of the 1% contribution, on wages up to the Social Security wage base.

Frequently asked questions

How much is $18 an hour a year?

$18 an hour for 40 hours a week over 52 weeks is $37,440 a year before tax. After federal and state taxes and payroll deductions in Oregon, that leaves $29,750.39 a year.

How much is $18 an hour after tax a month?

$2,479.20 a month, or $572.12 a week, working 40 hours a week. Each hour worked takes home $14.30 once 20.5% has gone in deductions.

What if I work part-time at $18 an hour?

At 20 hours a week, $18 an hour is $18,720 a year and leaves $16,102.87 after tax, or $1,341.91 a month. Lower earnings are taxed at a lower average rate, so take-home doesn't fall quite in proportion to the hours. The table above covers other weekly hours.

How much of an overtime hour do I keep?

At this level of pay your marginal rate is 28%, so an extra hour at $18 leaves about $12.95 after tax. Overtime paid at a higher rate keeps the same share of the higher figure.
