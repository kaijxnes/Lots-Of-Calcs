What comes out of pay in Idaho

Idaho taxable income: $58,900.00. Idaho uses the federal standard deduction.

Federal income tax: $7,670.00.

Social Security: $4,650.00. 6.2% of wages up to the 2026 wage base of $184,500.

Medicare: $1,087.50.

Additional Medicare tax: $0.00. 0.9% on wages above $200,000 ($250,000 married filing jointly). Employers withhold it from $200,000 whatever the status; the threshold here is the liability.

Idaho income tax: $2,711.72. 5.3% above the first $4,811 ($9,622 married), less the $155 grocery credit per person, which is refundable.

About these figures

They use the 2026 tax year, which runs from 1 January 2026 to 31 December 2026. The rates have not yet been checked by a person against the official sources — check them here before relying on them. This is an estimate, not a payslip.

Assumes:

  • Wages are the only income
  • Married filing jointly means one spouse earns the whole salary and the other has no income
  • Standard deduction, no itemizing
  • No dependants, and under 65

Leaves out:

  • Pre-tax 401(k), HSA and health insurance contributions
  • Tax credits, including the child tax credit and earned income credit
  • Itemized deductions, including state and local taxes (SALT) — high earners in high-tax states may pay less federal tax by itemizing
  • The deductions for tips, overtime, car loan interest and seniors introduced for 2025–2028
  • Alternative minimum tax
  • Head of household, married filing separately and qualifying surviving spouse
  • Permanent building fund tax ($10)

Not yet confirmed for this year:

  • The $4,811 / $9,622 zero-rate band is the 2025 amount
