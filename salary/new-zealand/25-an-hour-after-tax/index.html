How $25 an hour after tax is worked out

The hourly rate is turned into a yearly salary first — $25 × 40 hours × 52 weeks = $52,000 — because tax is worked out on yearly income. The take-home is then spread back over the year.

Independent earner tax credit: $520.00. $520 a year for incomes from $24,000 to $66,000, falling by 13 cents per dollar to nothing at $70,000.

Income tax (PAYE): $7,488.00. 10.5% to $15,600, 17.5% to $53,500, 30% to $78,100, 33% to $180,000, 39% above.

ACC earners' levy: $910.00. 1.75% of earnings up to $156,641 in 2026–27.

Frequently asked questions

How much is $25 an hour a year?

$25 an hour for 40 hours a week over 52 weeks is $52,000 a year before tax. After income tax and social contributions in New Zealand, that leaves $43,602.00 a year.

How much is $25 an hour after tax a month?

$3,633.50 a month, or $838.50 a week, working 40 hours a week. Each hour worked takes home $20.96 once 16.2% has gone in deductions.

What if I work part-time at $25 an hour?

At 20 hours a week, $25 an hour is $26,000 a year and leaves $22,607.00 after tax, or $1,883.92 a month. Lower earnings are taxed at a lower average rate, so take-home doesn't fall quite in proportion to the hours. The table above covers other weekly hours.

How much of an overtime hour do I keep?

At this level of pay your marginal rate is 19%, so an extra hour at $25 leaves about $20.19 after tax. Overtime paid at a higher rate keeps the same share of the higher figure.

What does this leave out?

KiwiSaver contributions (employee default rate 3–4%); Student loan repayments (12% above the threshold); Secondary tax codes.
