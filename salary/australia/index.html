What comes out of pay in Australia

Taxable income: $89,000.00. After the new $1,000 standard deduction for work-related expenses, which applies from 2026–27 unless you claim more in actual expenses.

Income tax: $17,220.00. Nothing to $18,200, 15% to $45,000 (cut from 16% on 1 July 2026), 30% to $135,000, 37% to $190,000, 45% above. Less the low income tax offset of up to $700.

Medicare levy: $1,780.00. 2% of taxable income, phased in from $28,011 at 10 cents per dollar.

Medicare levy surcharge: $0.00. Only without private hospital cover: 1% above $101,000, 1.25% above $118,000, 1.5% above $158,000.

About these figures

They use the 2026-27 tax year, which runs from 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027. The rates have not yet been checked by a person against the official sources — check them here before relying on them. This is an estimate, not a payslip.

Assumes:

  • Australian resident for tax purposes, under pension age
  • Employer superannuation is paid on top of the salary, not out of it
  • Single, no dependants

Leaves out:

  • HECS-HELP and other study loan repayments
  • Salary sacrifice and concessional super contributions
  • Family Medicare thresholds and the senior and pensioner offset
  • Actual work-related expenses above $1,000

Not yet confirmed for this year:

  • Medicare levy thresholds are the 2025–26 ones ($28,011 / $35,013); the 2026–27 thresholds are usually announced in the following May budget
  • The $1,000 standard deduction is described by the ATO as starting in 2026–27, but no source confirmed the bill had passed
  • Medicare levy surcharge thresholds are the 2025–26 ones
