What comes out of pay in Ireland

Tax credits: €4,000.00. Personal credit €2,000 (€4,000 married) and employee (PAYE) credit €2,000.

Income tax: €7,200.00. 20% up to €44,000 (€53,000 for a married couple with one income), 40% above, less tax credits.

Universal Social Charge: €1,032.82. 0.5% to €12,012, 2% to €28,700, 3% to €70,044, 8% above. Nothing is due on incomes of €13,000 or less.

PRSI: €2,118.75. Class A: 4.2% until September and 4.35% from 1 October 2026, 4.2375% over the year. Nothing below €352 a week, and a tapering PRSI credit up to €424 a week.

About these figures

They use the 2026 tax year, which runs from 1 January 2026 to 31 December 2026. The rates have not yet been checked by a person against the official sources — check them here before relying on them. This is an estimate, not a payslip.

Assumes:

  • PAYE employee paying Class A PRSI
  • No children; married means one spouse earns the whole salary and the couple is jointly assessed
  • Paid evenly through the year

Leaves out:

  • Pension contributions (tax relief at the marginal rate)
  • Other tax credits (rent, home carer, single person child carer)
  • Benefit-in-kind
  • Reduced USC rates for medical card holders and over-70s
