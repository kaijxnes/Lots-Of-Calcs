What comes out of pay in the Netherlands

Labour tax credit (arbeidskorting): €5,398.04. Builds up to €5,685 at €45,592 of pay, then falls by 6.51% of pay above that, reaching nothing at €132,920.

General tax credit (algemene heffingskorting): €1,818.51. €3,115, reduced by 6.398% of income above €29,736.

Taxable wage: €50,000.00. Employee pension premiums, and the tax-free allowance under the 30% ruling, come off pay before wage tax and the tax credits are worked out.

30% ruling tax-free allowance: €0.00. Under the 30% ruling (expatregeling) up to 30% of pay, including the allowance, is paid tax-free, on pay up to €262,000 (a maximum of €78,600 for a full year). The pay left after the allowance must stay above the salary norm — €48,013 in 2026, or €36,497 if you're under 30 with a master's degree — so near the norm the allowance is cut back to keep it there. It lasts up to 5 years.

Income tax and national insurance (loonheffing): €10,859.67. The tax credits can only reduce the tax to zero.

Frequently asked questions

How much is €50,000 after tax in the Netherlands?

€39,140.33 a year, or €3,261.69 a month, after income tax. The deductions are income tax and national insurance (loonheffing) €10,859.67 — 21.7% of the gross in all, and the next unit earned is taxed at 50%.

What does this leave out?

The franchise and pensionable-salary rules of individual schemes (the percentage here applies to all pay); Other credits (income-dependent combination credit for parents) and benefits (toeslagen); Box 2 and Box 3 income; The 30% ruling for part of a year (the allowance and its €78,600 maximum are pro rata), its transitional rules, and the cut to 27% planned from 2027.
