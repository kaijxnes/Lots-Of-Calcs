How $40 an hour after tax is worked out

The hourly rate is turned into a yearly salary first — $40 × 40 hours × 52 weeks = $83,200 — because tax is worked out on yearly income. The take-home is then spread back over the year.

Federal basic personal amount: $16,452.00. $16,452, falling to $14,829 as taxable income rises from $181,440 to $258,482.

Taxable income: $82,145.00. Salary less the enhanced (first and second additional) CPP contributions.

Canada Pension Plan (CPP): $4,230.45. 5.95% (4.95% base plus 1% first additional) of earnings above $3,500, up to $74,600: at most $4,230.45 in 2026.

Second additional CPP (CPP2): $344.00. 4% of earnings between $74,600 and $85,000: at most $416 in 2026.

Employment Insurance (EI): $1,123.07. 1.63% of insurable earnings up to $68,900: at most $1,123.07 in 2026.

Federal income tax: $9,872.36. 14% to $58,523, 20.5% to $117,045, 26% to $181,440, 29% to $258,482 and 33% above, less the credits.

Yukon income tax: $4,425.34. 6.4% to $58,523, 9% to $117,045, 10.9% to $181,440, 12.8% to $500,000, 15% above, less credits.

Frequently asked questions

How much is $40 an hour a year?

$40 an hour for 40 hours a week over 52 weeks is $83,200 a year before tax. After federal and provincial income tax, pension contributions and EI in Yukon, that leaves $63,204.78 a year.

How much is $40 an hour after tax a month?

$5,267.07 a month, or $1,215.48 a week, working 40 hours a week. Each hour worked takes home $30.39 once 24.0% has gone in deductions.

What if I work part-time at $40 an hour?

At 20 hours a week, $40 an hour is $41,600 a year and leaves $34,431.77 after tax, or $2,869.31 a month. Lower earnings are taxed at a lower average rate, so take-home doesn't fall quite in proportion to the hours. The table above covers other weekly hours.

How much of an overtime hour do I keep?

At this level of pay your marginal rate is 32%, so an extra hour at $40 leaves about $27.07 after tax. Overtime paid at a higher rate keeps the same share of the higher figure.
