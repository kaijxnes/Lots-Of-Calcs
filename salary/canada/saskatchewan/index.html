What comes out of pay in Saskatchewan

Federal basic personal amount: $16,452.00. $16,452, falling to $14,829 as taxable income rises from $181,440 to $258,482.

Taxable income: $74,273.00. Salary less the enhanced (first and second additional) CPP contributions.

Canada Pension Plan (CPP): $4,230.45. 5.95% (4.95% base plus 1% first additional) of earnings above $3,500, up to $74,600: at most $4,230.45 in 2026.

Second additional CPP (CPP2): $16.00. 4% of earnings between $74,600 and $85,000: at most $416 in 2026.

Employment Insurance (EI): $1,123.07. 1.63% of insurable earnings up to $68,900: at most $1,123.07 in 2026.

Federal income tax: $8,258.60. 14% to $58,523, 20.5% to $117,045, 26% to $181,440, 29% to $258,482 and 33% above, less the credits.

Saskatchewan income tax: $5,566.02. 10.5% to $54,532, 12.5% to $155,805, 14.5% above, less credits.

About these figures

They use the 2026 tax year, which runs from 1 January 2026 to 31 December 2026. The rates have not yet been checked by a person against the official sources — check them here before relying on them. This is an estimate, not a payslip. When every rate was last checked.

Assumes:

  • Employment income from one job, all year, is the only income
  • Only the basic personal amount is claimed (TD1 claim code 1), as the CRA's payroll formulas assume
  • Worked out with the CRA's Payroll Deductions Formulas (T4127), so it matches tax withheld on a payslip; the return can differ

Leaves out:

  • RRSP, pension plan and union dues deductions
  • Credits other than the basic personal amount, CPP/EI and the Canada employment amount (e.g. spouse, dependants, tuition, medical)
  • The Canada Workers Benefit and other refundable credits and benefits
  • Provincial low-income tax reductions that are claimed on the return rather than through payroll
  • Provincial credits other than the basic personal amount and CPP/EI
