Federal tax, CPP and EI are the same in every province except Quebec, which has its own pension plan (QPP), parental insurance (QPIP), a lower EI rate and a 16.5% cut in federal tax; the rest of the difference is provincial income tax.

What comes out of pay in Quebec

Quebec taxable income: $72,823.00. Salary less the deduction for workers (6%, up to $1,450) and the enhanced QPP contributions.

Federal basic personal amount: $16,452.00. $16,452, falling to $14,829 as taxable income rises from $181,440 to $258,482.

Taxable income: $74,273.00. Salary less the enhanced (first and second additional) CPP contributions.

Quebec Pension Plan (QPP): $4,479.30. 6.30% (5.30% base plus 1% first additional) of earnings above $3,500, up to $74,600: at most $4,479.30 in 2026.

Second additional QPP (QPP2): $16.00. 4% of earnings between $74,600 and $85,000: at most $416.

Employment Insurance (EI): $895.70. Quebec's reduced rate of 1.30% of insurable earnings up to $68,900 (at most $895.70), because QPIP pays parental benefits.

Federal income tax: $6,855.72. Basic federal tax less the 16.5% Quebec abatement.

Quebec Parental Insurance Plan (QPIP): $322.50. 0.430% of insurable earnings up to $103,000: at most $442.90.

Quebec income tax: $8,465.84. 14% to $54,345, 19% to $108,680, 24% to $132,245 and 25.75% above, less 14% of the $18,952 basic personal amount.

About these figures

They use the 2026 tax year, which runs from 1 January 2026 to 31 December 2026. The rates have not yet been checked by a person against the official sources — check them here before relying on them. This is an estimate, not a payslip. When every rate was last checked.

Assumes:

  • Employment income from one job, all year, is the only income
  • Only the basic personal amount is claimed (TD1 claim code 1), as the CRA's payroll formulas assume
  • Worked out with the CRA's Payroll Deductions Formulas (T4127), so it matches tax withheld on a payslip; the return can differ

Leaves out:

  • RRSP, pension plan and union dues deductions
  • Credits other than the basic personal amount, CPP/EI and the Canada employment amount (e.g. spouse, dependants, tuition, medical)
  • The Canada Workers Benefit and other refundable credits and benefits
  • Provincial low-income tax reductions that are claimed on the return rather than through payroll
  • Quebec credits other than the basic personal amount
  • The Quebec work premium and solidarity tax credit
  • The Health Services Fund, which only employers pay
