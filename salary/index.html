How it works

Pick where you're paid and type your salary before any deductions. The calculator applies that place's current tax year — income tax, social contributions and the local taxes most employees pay — and shows each one. Some places have options, such as filing status in the US or region in Spain; they appear under the salary when they apply.

The rates come from published sources listed on each location's own page. They have not yet been checked by a person against the official tax authority, so treat the figures as estimates and confirm them before relying on them.

Frequently asked questions

How do I work out my take-home pay?

Pick where you're paid, type your gross salary (before any deductions) and choose any options that apply, such as filing status, a pension or a student loan. The calculator applies that place's income tax and social contributions for the current tax year and shows each deduction, the take-home pay a year, month, week and working day, and your effective and marginal rates.

How much is £35,000 after tax in the UK?

£28,719.60 a year, or £2,393.30 a month, in England, Wales and Northern Ireland after income tax and National Insurance, with no pension or student loan.

How much is $75,000 after tax in the US?

It depends on the state: $61,592.50 a year in Texas, which has no state income tax, and $57,842.93 in California, after federal tax, Social Security, Medicare and state taxes, filing single.

How much is €50,000 after tax in Germany?

€32,329.00 a year (€2,694.08 a month) for a single person with no children, after income tax, the solidarity surcharge where it applies, and social insurance.

Which countries does it cover?

The UK (with Scotland's own bands), every US state and Washington DC, every Canadian province and territory, Ireland, Germany, France, Spain, Italy, the Netherlands, Belgium, Luxembourg, Switzerland (Zurich), Portugal, Austria, Finland, Sweden, Norway, Denmark, Australia, New Zealand, India and Japan.

What's the difference between the effective and marginal rate?

The effective rate is the share of your whole salary that goes in deductions. The marginal rate is what's taken from the next unit you earn — the one that matters for a pay rise or overtime, and always the higher of the two.

How up to date are the rates?

Each place's rates are for its current tax year and are checked against published sources every month; the tax rates page lists when each was last checked.
