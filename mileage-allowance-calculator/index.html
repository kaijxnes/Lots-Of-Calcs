HMRC's approved mileage rates

Vehicle2026 to 2027Before 6 April 2026
Car or van, first 10,000 miles55p45p
Car or van, after 10,000 miles25p25p
Motorcycle24p24p
Bicycle20p20p

Your employer can pay you up to these rates for business miles in your own vehicle without tax: the "approved amount", business miles times the rate. In GOV.UK's example, 12,000 miles in a car is £6,000 (10,000 × 55p plus 2,000 × 25p). The rate covers the whole cost of owning and running the vehicle, so you can't claim fuel, electricity, vehicle tax, MOTs or repairs on top. Electrically assisted pedal bikes use the bicycle rate; other electric bikes the motorcycle rate. An employer can also pay up to 5p a mile tax-free for each colleague you carry on a business journey.

If your employer pays less: Mileage Allowance Relief

If your employer pays less than the approved amount, or nothing, you can claim tax relief on the difference. The relief is worth your tax rate: GOV.UK's example is a £60 claim at 20%, worth £12. HMRC usually adjusts your tax code for the current year, or refunds or adjusts it for earlier years, and you can claim for the current tax year and the 4 before it, sending your mileage logs. If you fill in a Self Assessment return, claim there instead.

If your employer pays more

Anything over the approved amount is taxable: your employer reports it on a P11D or puts it through payroll. National Insurance works differently: it has one rate for every mile (55p for cars and vans from 6 April 2026, with no drop after 10,000 miles), worked out for each pay period. So on high mileage, pay between 25p and 55p a mile above 10,000 miles is taxed but free of National Insurance. To see what the miles themselves cost you, try the fuel cost calculator.

Frequently asked questions

What is the HMRC mileage rate for 2026/27?

55p a mile for the first 10,000 business miles in a car or van and 25p after that, up from 45p before 6 April 2026. Motorcycles are 24p and bicycles 20p a mile.

Can I claim mileage if my employer pays 25p a mile?

Yes: you can claim tax relief on the difference between what they pay and the approved amount. For 8,000 miles at 30p that's £2,000.00, worth £400.00 at 20%. At 25p it would be £2,400.00, worth £480.00.

Can I claim mileage for my commute?

No. Travelling to and from your normal workplace doesn't count, unless you're going to a temporary place of work.

How far back can I claim Mileage Allowance Relief?

For the current tax year and the 4 previous tax years, if you're eligible. HMRC asks for copies of your mileage logs, including the reason for every journey and where it started.

Can I claim for fuel as well as mileage?

Not in your own vehicle: the approved mileage rate covers fuel, electricity, vehicle tax, MOTs and repairs. In a company car you can claim relief on fuel or electricity you paid for on business trips.

Is mileage allowance taxable?

Not up to the approved amount. Anything your employer pays above it is taxable, and above the National Insurance rate per mile it also counts for National Insurance.
