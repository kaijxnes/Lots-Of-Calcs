How UK savings interest is taxed

Most people pay no tax on their savings interest, because three allowances come first, each for the tax year:

  • Personal Allowance: any of your £12,570 not used by wages or pension covers interest first.
  • Starting rate for savings: up to £5,000 of interest at 0% if your other income is under £17,570; every £1 of other income above the Personal Allowance cuts it by £1. GOV.UK's example: £16,000 of wages leaves £1,570 of starting rate, so £200 of interest is tax-free.
  • Personal Savings Allowance: £1,000 for basic-rate taxpayers, £500 for higher-rate and nothing for additional-rate, with your band worked out on your other income plus all your interest.

Interest above those is taxed at your usual rate: 20%, 40%, 45% in 2026 to 2027, rising to 24%, 44%, 49% from 6 April 2027. Interest in ISAs isn't taxed at all, and Scottish taxpayers pay the same tax on savings interest as the rest of the UK. Your bank tells HMRC what you earned after the tax year ends, and if there's tax to pay HMRC usually collects it through your tax code.

Frequently asked questions

How much savings interest can I earn tax-free?

Up to £1,000 a year as a basic-rate taxpayer (£500 higher-rate, nothing additional-rate), plus up to £5,000 more at 0% if your other income is under £17,570, plus any unused Personal Allowance. ISA interest is always tax-free.

How much tax will I pay on £2,000 of savings interest?

With £35,000 of other income you're a basic-rate taxpayer, so the first £1,000 is tax-free and the rest is taxed at 20%: £200.00 in 2026 to 2027.

What is the tax rate on savings interest from April 2027?

24%, 44%, 49% for basic, higher and additional-rate taxpayers, on interest above your allowances.

Do I need to tell HMRC about my savings interest?

Banks and building societies report the interest they pay to HMRC after the tax year ends. If you're employed or get a pension, HMRC works out any tax and usually collects it through your tax code; if you file Self Assessment, you report it there.

How is savings interest taxed in other countries?

It varies: Ireland takes 33% DIRT, Germany 26.375% above a €1,000 allowance, France a 31.4% flat tax, Spain 19% to 30%, Japan 20.315% withheld, while the US and Canada tax it with your other income.

Do Scottish taxpayers pay Scottish rates on savings interest?

No. Scottish Income Tax applies to wages and pensions, but you pay the same tax as the rest of the UK on savings interest and dividends.
