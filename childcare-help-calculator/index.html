How the two compare

Tax-Free Childcare adds £2 for every £8 you pay into a childcare account: 20% of the cost, up to £500 every 3 months per child (£2,000 a year), or £1,000 (£4,000 a year) for a disabled child. You usually need to be working, each earn at least £2,643.68 over 3 months if you're 21 or over, and neither of you can have adjusted net income over £100,000. Children qualify until the September after they turn 11 (16 if disabled), and the provider must be signed up.

Universal Credit pays back up to 85% of childcare costs if you're working (both of you, if you're a couple, unless one can't work because of a disability or health condition), up to £1,071.09 a month for one child or £1,836.16 for two or more. But it's part of your Universal Credit award, which goes down by 55p for each £1 you earn above any work allowance, so on higher earnings only part of it gets through, and above a certain income none of it does. You can't get both at once.

Frequently asked questions

Can I get Tax-Free Childcare and Universal Credit at the same time?

No. If you claim Universal Credit you can't get Tax-Free Childcare at the same time. If you switch, wait for a decision on your Tax-Free Childcare application before cancelling Universal Credit.

Which is better, Tax-Free Childcare or Universal Credit?

Universal Credit pays back up to 85% of costs and Tax-Free Childcare 20%, so Universal Credit usually gives more while you still get a decent Universal Credit award. As earnings rise, the 55% taper eats into it, and at higher incomes Tax-Free Childcare can be worth more.

How much is Tax-Free Childcare?

£2 for every £8 you pay in, up to £2,000 a year per child (£4,000 if the child is disabled). On £800 a month of childcare that's £160.00 a month.

How much childcare does Universal Credit pay?

Up to 85% of costs, to a maximum of £1,071.09 a month for one child and £1,836.16 for two or more (2026/27). You pay the provider first and claim it back.

Who can get Tax-Free Childcare?

Working parents who each expect to earn at least £2,643.68 over the next 3 months (if 21 or over), with neither earning over £100,000 adjusted net income, for children up to the September after they turn 11 (16 if disabled).
