From a score to a grade

A score becomes a percentage by dividing the marks you got by the marks available and multiplying by 100: 47 out of 60 is 47 ÷ 60 × 100 = 78.3%. That percentage is then matched against the grade boundaries — the highest boundary it reaches is your grade.

The boundaries start as a common US-style scale (A from 90%, B from 80%, C from 70%, D from 60%). Edit, add or remove them to match your school, exam board or university.

Weighted grades, worked through

When pieces of work count for different shares of the final mark, each score is multiplied by its weight before adding up. Suppose coursework is worth 20%, a mid-term 30% and the final exam 50%:

AssessmentScoreWeightCounts for
Coursework68%20%13.6
Mid-term74%30%22.2
Final exam81%50%40.5
Overall100%76.3%

A plain average of the three scores would be 74.3%. The weighted figure is higher because the strongest score, the final, counts for half the grade.

UK degree classifications

UK universities usually class an honours degree by its weighted average mark:

ClassificationAverage mark
First (1st)70% and above
Upper second (2:1)60–69%
Lower second (2:2)50–59%
Third (3rd)40–49%

To use these, set the boundaries to 1st 70, 2:1 60, 2:2 50, 3rd 40 and Fail 0. Universities differ in which years count and how borderline marks are treated, so check your own regulations. GCSE and A-level grade boundaries change with every exam series and are published by each exam board.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate a weighted average grade?

Multiply each score by its weight, add those together, then divide by the total of the weights. A piece worth 60% of the module counts three times as heavily as one worth 20%.

What score do I need on the final to reach a target grade?

Work out how many weighted points you already have, subtract that from your target, and divide by the final's weight. With 68% on coursework worth 20% and 74% on a mid-term worth 30%, you have 35.8 points; to reach 70% overall you need (70 − 35.8) ÷ 0.5 = 68.4% on a final worth 50%.

How do I turn a score into a percentage?

Divide the marks you got by the marks available and multiply by 100. 38 out of 45 is 38 ÷ 45 × 100 = 84.4%.

What if my weights don't add up to 100%?

The calculator divides by the total weight you have entered, so you can work out your average so far before every mark is in. Three assessments worth 20%, 30% and 10% give your average across that 60% of the module.

What percentage is a first-class degree?

An average of 70% or more at most UK universities. 60–69% is a 2:1, 50–59% a 2:2 and 40–49% a third.

Can I use letter grades like A* or 9–1?

Yes. Grade names are free text, so add a row for each grade with the minimum percentage for it — A* at 90, 9 at 85, or whatever your boundaries are — and the calculator matches your score against them.
