How dividends are taxed (2026/27)

Dividends are added on top of your other income. The first £500 is tax-free (the dividend allowance), but it still uses up part of your tax band. The rest is taxed at 10.75% in the basic rate band, 35.75% in the higher rate band and 39.35% above £125,140. Any personal allowance your salary doesn't use covers dividends first.

Frequently asked questions

How much tax do I pay on dividends?

For 2026/27: nothing on the first £500, then 10.75% on dividends that fall in the basic rate band, 35.75% in the higher rate band and 39.35% in the additional rate band. Where they fall depends on your other income, because dividends are counted last.

Did dividend tax go up in April 2026?

Yes: the basic and higher dividend rates rose by 2 percentage points from 6 April 2026, from 8.75% and 33.75% to 10.75% and 35.75%. The additional rate stayed at 39.35%.

Do I pay National Insurance on dividends?

No. Dividends are subject to income tax only, which is why company directors often take a small salary and the rest as dividends.

Do I need to tell HMRC about dividends?

Dividends within the £500 allowance need no action. Above it, HMRC can usually collect tax on up to £10,000 through your tax code; above £10,000 you need to file a Self Assessment tax return.
