How the trip cost is worked out

With MPG: divide the distance in miles by the car's miles per gallon to get the gallons used, convert to litres if fuel is priced per litre, and multiply by the price.

UK example: a 200-mile trip in a car doing 45 MPG, with petrol at 142.9p a litre. 200 ÷ 45 = 4.44 UK gallons, and at 4.546 litres to the gallon that is 20.2 litres. 20.2 × £1.429 = £28.87, or about 14.4p a mile.

With L/100km: divide the distance in kilometres by 100, multiply by the litres per 100 km, then by the price per litre. A 350 km drive at 6.2 L/100km with fuel at €1.75 a litre uses 21.7 litres and costs €37.98.

UK gallons, US gallons and L/100km

A UK (imperial) gallon is 4.546 litres; a US gallon is 3.785 litres. The same car therefore shows a higher MPG figure in the UK than in the US — 40 UK MPG is about 33 US MPG. Mixing them up makes a trip look about 20% cheaper or dearer than it is, which is why the calculator asks which you mean.

Getting a realistic answer

The official MPG for a car comes from a standard laboratory test and is usually better than you'll see on the road, especially on short trips, in cold weather or at motorway speeds above 70 mph. Your car's trip computer, or a brim-to-brim check, gives a truer figure: fill the tank, note the mileage, drive normally, then refill and divide the miles by the litres you put in, times 4.546.

For a return journey, double the distance. To share the cost between passengers, divide the result by the number of people — or use the split bill calculator. Comparing with an electric car? The EV charging cost calculator shows the cost per mile and the yearly difference.

Frequently asked questions

How do I work out the fuel cost of a trip?

Divide the distance by your miles per gallon to get the gallons needed, convert to litres if fuel is priced per litre (4.546 litres to a UK gallon), and multiply by the price. In metric, multiply distance by the L/100km figure, divide by 100, and multiply by the price per litre.

Is a higher or lower number better for fuel efficiency?

It depends which measure you use. Higher MPG is better because you travel further per gallon, but lower L/100km is better because you burn less fuel over the same distance.

How much does it cost to drive 100 miles?

At 45 UK MPG and 142.9p a litre, about £14.44. A car doing 35 MPG at the same price costs about £18.56 for the same 100 miles. Put your own car's figures in the MPG tab for an exact answer.

How do I convert MPG to litres per 100 km?

Divide 282.5 by the UK MPG, or 235.2 by the US MPG. 50 UK MPG is 282.5 ÷ 50 = 5.6 L/100km.

Why is my car's MPG lower than the advertised figure?

Official figures come from a standardised test that can't match every driving condition. Short journeys with a cold engine, heavy traffic, high motorway speeds, roof boxes, under-inflated tyres and air conditioning all use more fuel.

How do I work out cost per mile?

Divide the price per litre by the miles you get from a litre (MPG ÷ 4.546 for UK gallons). At 142.9p a litre and 45 MPG, a litre takes you 9.9 miles, so each mile costs about 14.4p.
