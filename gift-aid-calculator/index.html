How Gift Aid works

When you tick the Gift Aid box, the charity or community amateur sports club claims an extra 25p for every £1 you give, from the basic rate tax you've paid. It doesn't cost you anything extra. A declaration can cover your current and future donations and all donations made in the last 4 years.

Higher and additional rate taxpayers can claim back the difference between the tax they paid on the donation and what the charity got back, on a Self Assessment tax return or by asking HMRC to change their tax code. It works by extending your basic rate band (and higher rate limit) by the grossed-up gift, so less of your income is taxed at 40% or 45%. GOV.UK's example: you give £100, the charity gets £125, and as a 40% taxpayer you claim back £25.00 (£125 × 20%).

Over £100,000: the grossed-up gift also comes off your adjusted net income, so it can win back Personal Allowance you'd lose, and lower a High Income Child Benefit Charge.

Paying enough tax: donations qualify as long as they're not more than 4 times the tax you've paid in the tax year (on income or capital gains). If the charity gets back more tax than you've paid, HMRC may ask you to pay the difference. Through Payroll Giving, donations come out of your pay before Income Tax instead: £1 to charity costs 80p for a basic rate taxpayer, 60p at the higher rate and 55p at the additional rate.

Frequently asked questions

How much does Gift Aid add?

25p for every £1 you give, so a £100 donation is worth £125 to the charity. It doesn't cost you anything extra.

Can higher rate taxpayers claim Gift Aid back?

Yes: you can claim back the difference between the higher rate and the basic rate on the grossed-up gift. On £100 at 40% that's £25.00, so the gift really costs you £75. Claim on your Self Assessment tax return or ask HMRC to change your tax code.

How much can I claim back on £1,000?

At 40%, £250.00 (20% of the £1,250 the charity gets); at 45%, £312.50. Basic rate taxpayers don't get anything extra back: the charity gets the relief.

Can I use Gift Aid if I don't pay tax?

No: you should only make a Gift Aid declaration if you've paid enough tax to cover what the charity reclaims. Donations qualify if they're not more than 4 times the tax you paid that tax year.

Does Gift Aid help with the £100,000 Personal Allowance trap?

Yes: the grossed-up gift comes off your adjusted net income. With £110,000 of income, giving £8,000 (£10,000 with Gift Aid) brings it back to £100,000, and you can claim back £4,000.00 (2026/27).
