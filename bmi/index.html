About this calculator

BMI is calculated as weight (kg) ÷ height (m)², or 703 × weight (lb) ÷ height (in)² in imperial units.

It's a quick screening tool, not a diagnosis — it doesn't distinguish muscle from fat, so very muscular people can show a higher BMI without carrying excess body fat. The standard categories are under 18.5 (underweight), 18.5–24.9 (normal), 25–29.9 (overweight), and 30+ (obese).

Frequently asked questions

What is a healthy BMI range?

The standard adult categories are under 18.5 underweight, 18.5 to 24.9 normal weight, 25 to 29.9 overweight, and 30 or above obese.

Is BMI accurate for everyone?

No. BMI only compares height and weight, so it cannot tell muscle from fat and can misclassify very muscular people, older adults, and children. Treat it as a rough screening figure rather than a diagnosis.
