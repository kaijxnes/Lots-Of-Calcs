How BMI is calculated

Body Mass Index is your weight divided by the square of your height:

BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)²

In imperial units the same thing is 703 × weight (lb) ÷ height (in)², where 703 converts pounds and inches into kilograms and metres. Stones are turned into pounds first: one stone is 14 pounds.

Worked example (metric). Someone 175 cm tall weighing 70 kg: 1.75 × 1.75 = 3.0625, and 70 ÷ 3.0625 = 22.9, which is in the healthy range.

Worked example (stones and pounds). Someone 5ft 10in weighing 12st: that is 70 inches and 168 pounds. 70 × 70 = 4,900, and 703 × 168 ÷ 4,900 = 24.1 — just inside the healthy range, which tops out at 24.9.

What BMI does and doesn't tell you

BMI is a quick screening figure, not a diagnosis. It compares weight with height and nothing else, so it can't tell muscle from fat or say where fat is carried. A very muscular person can have an “overweight” BMI with little body fat, and an older adult who has lost muscle can have a healthy BMI while carrying more fat than it suggests.

Waist size adds what BMI misses. The NHS suggests measuring your waist and comparing it with your height: a waist of less than half your height is the healthy target for most adults.

If your BMI is outside the healthy range, or you are worried about your weight, your GP or practice nurse can look at the whole picture.

Frequently asked questions

What is a healthy BMI range?

For adults, 18.5 to 24.9. Below 18.5 is underweight, 25 to 29.9 is overweight, and 30 or above is obese. Some groups are advised to use lower thresholds — see the question on ethnicity below.

How do I work out my BMI in stones and pounds?

Turn your weight into pounds (stones × 14, plus the pounds) and your height into inches (feet × 12, plus the inches). Then multiply the pounds by 703 and divide by the inches squared. 11st 7lb at 5ft 8in is 161 lb and 68 in, so 703 × 161 ÷ 4,624 = 24.5. The calculator does this for you on the Imperial tab.

What is a healthy weight for my height?

Multiply your height in metres squared by 18.5 for the bottom of the healthy range and by 24.9 for the top. At 1.75 m that is 56.7 kg to 76.3 kg, or about 8st 13lb to 12st. The calculator shows the range for your height as you type, and the table above covers common heights.

Are the BMI ranges different for different ethnic groups?

Yes. The NHS advises that people of South Asian, Chinese, other Asian, Middle Eastern, Black African or African-Caribbean family background have a higher risk of type 2 diabetes and other conditions at a lower BMI, and uses 23 or more as overweight and 27.5 or more as obese for these groups.

Can I use this BMI calculator for a child?

No. Children and teenagers are still growing, so their BMI is compared with other children of the same age and sex using centile charts rather than the adult categories. The NHS has a separate healthy weight calculator for under-18s.

Is BMI accurate for everyone?

No. BMI only compares height and weight, so it cannot tell muscle from fat and can misclassify very muscular people, older adults and pregnant women. Treat it as a rough screening figure, and use waist measurement alongside it.
