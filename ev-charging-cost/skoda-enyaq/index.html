How it's worked out

The battery: the Škoda Enyaq 85 84 kWh (MY27) has 79 kWh of usable capacity. Some energy is lost as heat while charging, so filling it from empty draws about 87.8 kWh from the grid at home.

The price: 87.8 kWh × 26.32p, the Ofgem price cap unit rate for 1 October to 31 December 2026 (Direct Debit, average for England, Scotland and Wales; no VAT on electricity from 1 October 2026 to 31 March 2027), is £23.10. Your tariff may be different; the table shows other prices.

Per mile: EV Database estimates 272 Wh a mile in real-world driving, so each mile takes 0.302 kWh from the grid, 8.0p at 26.32p.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to charge a Škoda Enyaq at home?

About £23.10 from empty to full at the Oct–Dec 2026 price cap rate of 26.32p per kWh: 87.8 kWh from the grid to put 79 kWh into the battery, allowing for 10% lost in charging. On an overnight EV tariff at 7.5p it would be £6.58.

How much does a Škoda Enyaq cost per mile?

About 8.0p a mile charged at home at the price cap rate, using EV Database's real-world estimate of 272 Wh per mile (3.7 miles per kWh). That's £795.45 for 10,000 miles a year.

How much is a rapid charge for a Škoda Enyaq?

Charging from 10% to 80% — the usual rapid-charger stop — puts in 55.3 kWh, about £45.99 at 79p per kWh. Rapid charger prices vary a lot by network and many have membership rates, so check the price on the charger.

How long does a Škoda Enyaq take to charge at home?

About 11h 52m from empty to full on a 7.4 kW home wallbox, or roughly 70% of that for a 10–80% top-up. A three-pin plug at around 2.3 kW takes about three times as long.

How far does a Škoda Enyaq go on a full charge?

373 miles on the official WLTP test, and about 290 miles in mixed real-world driving by EV Database's estimate. Cold weather and motorway speeds cut range most.
