How it's worked out

The battery: the Hyundai Kona Electric 65 kWh (MY24-25) has 65.4 kWh of usable capacity. Some energy is lost as heat while charging, so filling it from empty draws about 72.7 kWh from the grid at home.

The price: 72.7 kWh × 26.32p, the Ofgem price cap unit rate for 1 October to 31 December 2026 (Direct Debit, average for England, Scotland and Wales; no VAT on electricity from 1 October 2026 to 31 March 2027), is £19.13. Your tariff may be different; the table shows other prices.

Per mile: EV Database estimates 273 Wh a mile in real-world driving, so each mile takes 0.303 kWh from the grid, 8.0p at 26.32p.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to charge a Hyundai Kona Electric at home?

About £19.13 from empty to full at the Oct–Dec 2026 price cap rate of 26.32p per kWh: 72.7 kWh from the grid to put 65.4 kWh into the battery, allowing for 10% lost in charging. On an overnight EV tariff at 7.5p it would be £5.45.

How much does a Hyundai Kona Electric cost per mile?

About 8.0p a mile charged at home at the price cap rate, using EV Database's real-world estimate of 273 Wh per mile (3.7 miles per kWh). That's £798.37 for 10,000 miles a year.

How much is a rapid charge for a Hyundai Kona Electric?

Charging from 10% to 80% — the usual rapid-charger stop — puts in 45.8 kWh, about £38.07 at 79p per kWh. Rapid charger prices vary a lot by network and many have membership rates, so check the price on the charger.

How long does a Hyundai Kona Electric take to charge at home?

About 9h 49m from empty to full on a 7.4 kW home wallbox, or roughly 70% of that for a 10–80% top-up. A three-pin plug at around 2.3 kW takes about three times as long.

How far does a Hyundai Kona Electric go on a full charge?

319 miles on the official WLTP test, and about 240 miles in mixed real-world driving by EV Database's estimate. Cold weather and motorway speeds cut range most.
