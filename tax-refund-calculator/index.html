Why people are owed tax back

PAYE spreads your tax-free Personal Allowance (£12,570) evenly across the year. If you stop working part way through, you've only used part of it, so you've paid tax on income that turns out to be tax-free. Leave a job paying £2,500 a month after 6 months and PAYE takes about £1,743.00, but the tax due on £15,000 for the whole year is only £486.00: £1,257.00 back.

Other common reasons: being on the wrong tax code, an emergency code (W1, M1 or X) that taxed each payday on its own, finishing one job and starting another paid in the same month, starting a work pension, or work expenses you haven't claimed relief on. The reverse happens too: two jobs both using the full allowance, or untaxed income, leave you owing tax.

How you get it back

After the tax year ends on 5 April, HMRC compares what you earned with what was taken and sends a tax calculation letter (P800) if it doesn't match. The letters go out between June and the end of November. If yours says you can claim online, the money arrives within 5 working days; a cheque takes up to 6 weeks if you ask for one, or comes within 14 days if the letter says it'll be sent. If you owe less than £3,000 and still pay tax through PAYE, HMRC usually collects it through your tax code over the next tax year. GOV.UK's refund checker shows how to claim sooner in your situation.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if I'm owed a tax refund?

If more tax was taken through PAYE than is due on your whole year's income. It's most likely if you stopped working part way through the tax year, were on an emergency or wrong tax code, or had two jobs end and start in the same month. This estimator compares the two.

When will HMRC send my tax refund?

HMRC sends tax calculation letters (P800s) between June and the end of November after the tax year ends on 5 April. Claimed online, the refund arrives within 5 working days.

How much tax will I get back if I stop working after 6 months?

On £2,500 a month with the standard 1257L code, about £1,257.00: £1,743.00 taken against £486.00 due on £15,000 for the year, if you have no other income before 5 April.

What if I've paid too little tax?

HMRC usually collects underpaid tax of less than £3,000 by changing your tax code, so it comes out of your pay or pension in equal amounts over the next tax year. Otherwise it writes to you about how to pay.

Does an emergency tax code mean I'll get a refund?

Not always. An emergency code taxes each payday as if you were paid that much every week or month, so it overcharges if your pay jumps around or you start part way through the year. If too much was taken, it comes back once HMRC updates your code or at the end of the year.
