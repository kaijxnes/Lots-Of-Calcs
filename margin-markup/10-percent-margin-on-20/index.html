10% margin on £20

To make a 10% margin on something that costs £20, sell it for £22.22.

Careful: 10% markup is different

Adding 10% to the cost (a 10% markup) gives £22 — a profit of £2, which is only a 9.1% margin. If you meant a 10% markup, that's your price; if you need 10% of the selling price as profit, it's £22.22.

Prices before VAT. Work out any cost and price in the margin vs markup calculator; the sums are the same in any currency.

How to work it out

Margin: price = cost ÷ (1 − margin) = £20 ÷ 0.9 = £22.22. Check: £2.22 profit ÷ £22.22 = 10%.

Markup: price = cost × (1 + markup) = £20 × 1.1 = £22.

Frequently asked questions

What price gives a 10% margin on £20?

£22.22: £20 ÷ (1 − 0.1) = £20 ÷ 0.9. Profit £2.22 is 10% of £22.22.

What is a 10% markup on £20?

£22: £20 × 1.1. That's a £2 profit, which is only a 9.1% margin.

What markup do I need for a 10% margin?

11.1%. Markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin): 0.1 ÷ 0.9 = 0.11.

What's the difference between margin and markup?

Both measure the same profit. Margin divides it by the selling price; markup divides it by the cost. So a margin is always smaller than the matching markup: 50% markup is a 33.3% margin, and a 50% margin needs a 100% markup.
