30% margin on £30

To make a 30% margin on something that costs £30, sell it for £42.86.

Price for a 30% margin£42.86
Profit£12.86
The markup that is42.9%

Careful: 30% markup is different

Adding 30% to the cost (a 30% markup) gives £39 — a profit of £9, which is only a 23.1% margin. If you meant a 30% markup, that's your price; if you need 30% of the selling price as profit, it's £42.86.

Prices before VAT. Work out any cost and price in the margin vs markup calculator; the sums are the same in any currency.

How to work it out

Margin: price = cost ÷ (1 − margin) = £30 ÷ 0.7 = £42.86. Check: £12.86 profit ÷ £42.86 = 30%.

Markup: price = cost × (1 + markup) = £30 × 1.3 = £39.

Frequently asked questions

What price gives a 30% margin on £30?

£42.86: £30 ÷ (1 − 0.3) = £30 ÷ 0.7. Profit £12.86 is 30% of £42.86.

What is a 30% markup on £30?

£39: £30 × 1.3. That's a £9 profit, which is only a 23.1% margin.

What markup do I need for a 30% margin?

42.9%. Markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin): 0.3 ÷ 0.7 = 0.43.

What's the difference between margin and markup?

Both measure the same profit. Margin divides it by the selling price; markup divides it by the cost. So a margin is always smaller than the matching markup: 50% markup is a 33.3% margin, and a 50% margin needs a 100% markup.
