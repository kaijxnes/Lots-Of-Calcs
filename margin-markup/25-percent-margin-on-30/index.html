25% margin on £30

To make a 25% margin on something that costs £30, sell it for £40.

Profit£10

Careful: 25% markup is different

Adding 25% to the cost (a 25% markup) gives £37.50 — a profit of £7.50, which is only a 20% margin. If you meant a 25% markup, that's your price; if you need 25% of the selling price as profit, it's £40.

Prices before VAT. Work out any cost and price in the margin vs markup calculator; the sums are the same in any currency.

How to work it out

Margin: price = cost ÷ (1 − margin) = £30 ÷ 0.75 = £40. Check: £10 profit ÷ £40 = 25%.

Markup: price = cost × (1 + markup) = £30 × 1.25 = £37.50.

Frequently asked questions

What price gives a 25% margin on £30?

£40: £30 ÷ (1 − 0.25) = £30 ÷ 0.75. Profit £10 is 25% of £40.

What is a 25% markup on £30?

£37.50: £30 × 1.25. That's a £7.50 profit, which is only a 20% margin.

What markup do I need for a 25% margin?

33.3%. Markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin): 0.25 ÷ 0.75 = 0.33.

What's the difference between margin and markup?

Both measure the same profit. Margin divides it by the selling price; markup divides it by the cost. So a margin is always smaller than the matching markup: 50% markup is a 33.3% margin, and a 50% margin needs a 100% markup.
