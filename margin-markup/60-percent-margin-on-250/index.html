60% margin on £250

To make a 60% margin on something that costs £250, sell it for £625.

Price for a 60% margin£625
Profit£375
The markup that is150%

Careful: 60% markup is different

Adding 60% to the cost (a 60% markup) gives £400 — a profit of £150, which is only a 37.5% margin. If you meant a 60% markup, that's your price; if you need 60% of the selling price as profit, it's £625.

Prices before VAT. Work out any cost and price in the margin vs markup calculator; the sums are the same in any currency.

How to work it out

Margin: price = cost ÷ (1 − margin) = £250 ÷ 0.4 = £625. Check: £375 profit ÷ £625 = 60%.

Markup: price = cost × (1 + markup) = £250 × 1.6 = £400.

Frequently asked questions

What price gives a 60% margin on £250?

£625: £250 ÷ (1 − 0.6) = £250 ÷ 0.4. Profit £375 is 60% of £625.

What is a 60% markup on £250?

£400: £250 × 1.6. That's a £150 profit, which is only a 37.5% margin.

What markup do I need for a 60% margin?

150%. Markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin): 0.6 ÷ 0.4 = 1.5.

What's the difference between margin and markup?

Both measure the same profit. Margin divides it by the selling price; markup divides it by the cost. So a margin is always smaller than the matching markup: 50% markup is a 33.3% margin, and a 50% margin needs a 100% markup.
