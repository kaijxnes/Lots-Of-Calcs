10% margin on £15

To make a 10% margin on something that costs £15, sell it for £16.67.

Price for a 10% margin£16.67
Profit£1.67
The markup that is11.1%

Careful: 10% markup is different

Adding 10% to the cost (a 10% markup) gives £16.50 — a profit of £1.50, which is only a 9.1% margin. If you meant a 10% markup, that's your price; if you need 10% of the selling price as profit, it's £16.67.

Prices before VAT. Work out any cost and price in the margin vs markup calculator; the sums are the same in any currency.

How to work it out

Margin: price = cost ÷ (1 − margin) = £15 ÷ 0.9 = £16.67. Check: £1.67 profit ÷ £16.67 = 10%.

Markup: price = cost × (1 + markup) = £15 × 1.1 = £16.50.

Frequently asked questions

What price gives a 10% margin on £15?

£16.67: £15 ÷ (1 − 0.1) = £15 ÷ 0.9. Profit £1.67 is 10% of £16.67.

What is a 10% markup on £15?

£16.50: £15 × 1.1. That's a £1.50 profit, which is only a 9.1% margin.

What markup do I need for a 10% margin?

11.1%. Markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin): 0.1 ÷ 0.9 = 0.11.

What's the difference between margin and markup?

Both measure the same profit. Margin divides it by the selling price; markup divides it by the cost. So a margin is always smaller than the matching markup: 50% markup is a 33.3% margin, and a 50% margin needs a 100% markup.
