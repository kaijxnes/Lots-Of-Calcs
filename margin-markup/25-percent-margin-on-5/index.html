25% margin on £5

To make a 25% margin on something that costs £5, sell it for £6.67.

Price for a 25% margin£6.67
Profit£1.67
The markup that is33.3%

Careful: 25% markup is different

Adding 25% to the cost (a 25% markup) gives £6.25 — a profit of £1.25, which is only a 20% margin. If you meant a 25% markup, that's your price; if you need 25% of the selling price as profit, it's £6.67.

Prices before VAT. Work out any cost and price in the margin vs markup calculator; the sums are the same in any currency.

How to work it out

Margin: price = cost ÷ (1 − margin) = £5 ÷ 0.75 = £6.67. Check: £1.67 profit ÷ £6.67 = 25%.

Markup: price = cost × (1 + markup) = £5 × 1.25 = £6.25.

Frequently asked questions

What price gives a 25% margin on £5?

£6.67: £5 ÷ (1 − 0.25) = £5 ÷ 0.75. Profit £1.67 is 25% of £6.67.

What is a 25% markup on £5?

£6.25: £5 × 1.25. That's a £1.25 profit, which is only a 20% margin.

What markup do I need for a 25% margin?

33.3%. Markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin): 0.25 ÷ 0.75 = 0.33.

What's the difference between margin and markup?

Both measure the same profit. Margin divides it by the selling price; markup divides it by the cost. So a margin is always smaller than the matching markup: 50% markup is a 33.3% margin, and a 50% margin needs a 100% markup.
