45% margin on £500

To make a 45% margin on something that costs £500, sell it for £909.09.

Price for a 45% margin£909.09

The markup that is81.8%

Careful: 45% markup is different

Adding 45% to the cost (a 45% markup) gives £725 — a profit of £225, which is only a 31% margin. If you meant a 45% markup, that's your price; if you need 45% of the selling price as profit, it's £909.09.

Prices before VAT. Work out any cost and price in the margin vs markup calculator; the sums are the same in any currency.

How to work it out

Margin: price = cost ÷ (1 − margin) = £500 ÷ 0.55 = £909.09. Check: £409.09 profit ÷ £909.09 = 45%.

Markup: price = cost × (1 + markup) = £500 × 1.45 = £725.

Frequently asked questions

What price gives a 45% margin on £500?

£909.09: £500 ÷ (1 − 0.45) = £500 ÷ 0.55. Profit £409.09 is 45% of £909.09.

What is a 45% markup on £500?

£725: £500 × 1.45. That's a £225 profit, which is only a 31% margin.

What markup do I need for a 45% margin?

81.8%. Markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin): 0.45 ÷ 0.55 = 0.82.

What's the difference between margin and markup?

Both measure the same profit. Margin divides it by the selling price; markup divides it by the cost. So a margin is always smaller than the matching markup: 50% markup is a 33.3% margin, and a 50% margin needs a 100% markup.
