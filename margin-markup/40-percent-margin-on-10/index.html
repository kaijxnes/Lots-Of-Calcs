40% margin on £10

To make a 40% margin on something that costs £10, sell it for £16.67.

Price for a 40% margin£16.67
Profit£6.67
The markup that is66.7%

Careful: 40% markup is different

Adding 40% to the cost (a 40% markup) gives £14 — a profit of £4, which is only a 28.6% margin. If you meant a 40% markup, that's your price; if you need 40% of the selling price as profit, it's £16.67.

Prices before VAT. Work out any cost and price in the margin vs markup calculator; the sums are the same in any currency.

How to work it out

Margin: price = cost ÷ (1 − margin) = £10 ÷ 0.6 = £16.67. Check: £6.67 profit ÷ £16.67 = 40%.

Markup: price = cost × (1 + markup) = £10 × 1.4 = £14.

Frequently asked questions

What price gives a 40% margin on £10?

£16.67: £10 ÷ (1 − 0.4) = £10 ÷ 0.6. Profit £6.67 is 40% of £16.67.

What is a 40% markup on £10?

£14: £10 × 1.4. That's a £4 profit, which is only a 28.6% margin.

What markup do I need for a 40% margin?

66.7%. Markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin): 0.4 ÷ 0.6 = 0.67.

What's the difference between margin and markup?

Both measure the same profit. Margin divides it by the selling price; markup divides it by the cost. So a margin is always smaller than the matching markup: 50% markup is a 33.3% margin, and a 50% margin needs a 100% markup.
