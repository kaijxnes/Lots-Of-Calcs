Careful: 35% markup is different

Adding 35% to the cost (a 35% markup) gives £13.50 — a profit of £3.50, which is only a 25.9% margin. If you meant a 35% markup, that's your price; if you need 35% of the selling price as profit, it's £15.38.

Prices before VAT. Work out any cost and price in the margin vs markup calculator; the sums are the same in any currency.

How to work it out

Margin: price = cost ÷ (1 − margin) = £10 ÷ 0.65 = £15.38. Check: £5.38 profit ÷ £15.38 = 35%.

Markup: price = cost × (1 + markup) = £10 × 1.35 = £13.50.

Frequently asked questions

What price gives a 35% margin on £10?

£15.38: £10 ÷ (1 − 0.35) = £10 ÷ 0.65. Profit £5.38 is 35% of £15.38.

What is a 35% markup on £10?

£13.50: £10 × 1.35. That's a £3.50 profit, which is only a 25.9% margin.

What markup do I need for a 35% margin?

53.8%. Markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin): 0.35 ÷ 0.65 = 0.54.

What's the difference between margin and markup?

Both measure the same profit. Margin divides it by the selling price; markup divides it by the cost. So a margin is always smaller than the matching markup: 50% markup is a 33.3% margin, and a 50% margin needs a 100% markup.
