Careful: 75% markup is different

Adding 75% to the cost (a 75% markup) gives £131.25 — a profit of £56.25, which is only a 42.9% margin. If you meant a 75% markup, that's your price; if you need 75% of the selling price as profit, it's £300.

Prices before VAT. Work out any cost and price in the margin vs markup calculator; the sums are the same in any currency.

How to work it out

Margin: price = cost ÷ (1 − margin) = £75 ÷ 0.25 = £300. Check: £225 profit ÷ £300 = 75%.

Markup: price = cost × (1 + markup) = £75 × 1.75 = £131.25.

Frequently asked questions

What price gives a 75% margin on £75?

£300: £75 ÷ (1 − 0.75) = £75 ÷ 0.25. Profit £225 is 75% of £300.

What is a 75% markup on £75?

£131.25: £75 × 1.75. That's a £56.25 profit, which is only a 42.9% margin.

What markup do I need for a 75% margin?

300%. Markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin): 0.75 ÷ 0.25 = 3.

What's the difference between margin and markup?

Both measure the same profit. Margin divides it by the selling price; markup divides it by the cost. So a margin is always smaller than the matching markup: 50% markup is a 33.3% margin, and a 50% margin needs a 100% markup.
