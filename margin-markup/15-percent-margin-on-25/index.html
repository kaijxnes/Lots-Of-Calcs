15% margin on £25

To make a 15% margin on something that costs £25, sell it for £29.41.

Careful: 15% markup is different

Adding 15% to the cost (a 15% markup) gives £28.75 — a profit of £3.75, which is only a 13% margin. If you meant a 15% markup, that's your price; if you need 15% of the selling price as profit, it's £29.41.

Prices before VAT. Work out any cost and price in the margin vs markup calculator; the sums are the same in any currency.

How to work it out

Margin: price = cost ÷ (1 − margin) = £25 ÷ 0.85 = £29.41. Check: £4.41 profit ÷ £29.41 = 15%.

Markup: price = cost × (1 + markup) = £25 × 1.15 = £28.75.

Frequently asked questions

What price gives a 15% margin on £25?

£29.41: £25 ÷ (1 − 0.15) = £25 ÷ 0.85. Profit £4.41 is 15% of £29.41.

What is a 15% markup on £25?

£28.75: £25 × 1.15. That's a £3.75 profit, which is only a 13% margin.

What markup do I need for a 15% margin?

17.6%. Markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin): 0.15 ÷ 0.85 = 0.18.

What's the difference between margin and markup?

Both measure the same profit. Margin divides it by the selling price; markup divides it by the cost. So a margin is always smaller than the matching markup: 50% markup is a 33.3% margin, and a 50% margin needs a 100% markup.
