25% margin on £15

To make a 25% margin on something that costs £15, sell it for £20.

Careful: 25% markup is different

Adding 25% to the cost (a 25% markup) gives £18.75 — a profit of £3.75, which is only a 20% margin. If you meant a 25% markup, that's your price; if you need 25% of the selling price as profit, it's £20.

Prices before VAT. Work out any cost and price in the margin vs markup calculator; the sums are the same in any currency.

How to work it out

Margin: price = cost ÷ (1 − margin) = £15 ÷ 0.75 = £20. Check: £5 profit ÷ £20 = 25%.

Markup: price = cost × (1 + markup) = £15 × 1.25 = £18.75.

Frequently asked questions

What price gives a 25% margin on £15?

£20: £15 ÷ (1 − 0.25) = £15 ÷ 0.75. Profit £5 is 25% of £20.

What is a 25% markup on £15?

£18.75: £15 × 1.25. That's a £3.75 profit, which is only a 20% margin.

What markup do I need for a 25% margin?

33.3%. Markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin): 0.25 ÷ 0.75 = 0.33.

What's the difference between margin and markup?

Both measure the same profit. Margin divides it by the selling price; markup divides it by the cost. So a margin is always smaller than the matching markup: 50% markup is a 33.3% margin, and a 50% margin needs a 100% markup.
