30% margin on £75

To make a 30% margin on something that costs £75, sell it for £107.14.

Price for a 30% margin£107.14
Profit£32.14
The markup that is42.9%

Careful: 30% markup is different

Adding 30% to the cost (a 30% markup) gives £97.50 — a profit of £22.50, which is only a 23.1% margin. If you meant a 30% markup, that's your price; if you need 30% of the selling price as profit, it's £107.14.

Prices before VAT. Work out any cost and price in the margin vs markup calculator; the sums are the same in any currency.

How to work it out

Margin: price = cost ÷ (1 − margin) = £75 ÷ 0.7 = £107.14. Check: £32.14 profit ÷ £107.14 = 30%.

Markup: price = cost × (1 + markup) = £75 × 1.3 = £97.50.

Frequently asked questions

What price gives a 30% margin on £75?

£107.14: £75 ÷ (1 − 0.3) = £75 ÷ 0.7. Profit £32.14 is 30% of £107.14.

What is a 30% markup on £75?

£97.50: £75 × 1.3. That's a £22.50 profit, which is only a 23.1% margin.

What markup do I need for a 30% margin?

42.9%. Markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin): 0.3 ÷ 0.7 = 0.43.

What's the difference between margin and markup?

Both measure the same profit. Margin divides it by the selling price; markup divides it by the cost. So a margin is always smaller than the matching markup: 50% markup is a 33.3% margin, and a 50% margin needs a 100% markup.
