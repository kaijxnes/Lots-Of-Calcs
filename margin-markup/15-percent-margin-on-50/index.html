15% margin on £50

To make a 15% margin on something that costs £50, sell it for £58.82.

Price for a 15% margin£58.82
Profit£8.82
The markup that is17.6%

Careful: 15% markup is different

Adding 15% to the cost (a 15% markup) gives £57.50 — a profit of £7.50, which is only a 13% margin. If you meant a 15% markup, that's your price; if you need 15% of the selling price as profit, it's £58.82.

Prices before VAT. Work out any cost and price in the margin vs markup calculator; the sums are the same in any currency.

How to work it out

Margin: price = cost ÷ (1 − margin) = £50 ÷ 0.85 = £58.82. Check: £8.82 profit ÷ £58.82 = 15%.

Markup: price = cost × (1 + markup) = £50 × 1.15 = £57.50.

Frequently asked questions

What price gives a 15% margin on £50?

£58.82: £50 ÷ (1 − 0.15) = £50 ÷ 0.85. Profit £8.82 is 15% of £58.82.

What is a 15% markup on £50?

£57.50: £50 × 1.15. That's a £7.50 profit, which is only a 13% margin.

What markup do I need for a 15% margin?

17.6%. Markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin): 0.15 ÷ 0.85 = 0.18.

What's the difference between margin and markup?

Both measure the same profit. Margin divides it by the selling price; markup divides it by the cost. So a margin is always smaller than the matching markup: 50% markup is a 33.3% margin, and a 50% margin needs a 100% markup.
