Careful: 50% markup is different

Adding 50% to the cost (a 50% markup) gives £37.50 — a profit of £12.50, which is only a 33.3% margin. If you meant a 50% markup, that's your price; if you need 50% of the selling price as profit, it's £50.

Prices before VAT. Work out any cost and price in the margin vs markup calculator; the sums are the same in any currency.

How to work it out

Margin: price = cost ÷ (1 − margin) = £25 ÷ 0.5 = £50. Check: £25 profit ÷ £50 = 50%.

Markup: price = cost × (1 + markup) = £25 × 1.5 = £37.50.

Frequently asked questions

What price gives a 50% margin on £25?

£50: £25 ÷ (1 − 0.5) = £25 ÷ 0.5. Profit £25 is 50% of £50.

What is a 50% markup on £25?

£37.50: £25 × 1.5. That's a £12.50 profit, which is only a 33.3% margin.

What markup do I need for a 50% margin?

100%. Markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin): 0.5 ÷ 0.5 = 1.

What's the difference between margin and markup?

Both measure the same profit. Margin divides it by the selling price; markup divides it by the cost. So a margin is always smaller than the matching markup: 50% markup is a 33.3% margin, and a 50% margin needs a 100% markup.
