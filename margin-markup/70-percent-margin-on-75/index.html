Careful: 70% markup is different

Adding 70% to the cost (a 70% markup) gives £127.50 — a profit of £52.50, which is only a 41.2% margin. If you meant a 70% markup, that's your price; if you need 70% of the selling price as profit, it's £250.

Prices before VAT. Work out any cost and price in the margin vs markup calculator; the sums are the same in any currency.

How to work it out

Margin: price = cost ÷ (1 − margin) = £75 ÷ 0.3 = £250. Check: £175 profit ÷ £250 = 70%.

Markup: price = cost × (1 + markup) = £75 × 1.7 = £127.50.

Frequently asked questions

What price gives a 70% margin on £75?

£250: £75 ÷ (1 − 0.7) = £75 ÷ 0.3. Profit £175 is 70% of £250.

What is a 70% markup on £75?

£127.50: £75 × 1.7. That's a £52.50 profit, which is only a 41.2% margin.

What markup do I need for a 70% margin?

233.3%. Markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin): 0.7 ÷ 0.3 = 2.33.

What's the difference between margin and markup?

Both measure the same profit. Margin divides it by the selling price; markup divides it by the cost. So a margin is always smaller than the matching markup: 50% markup is a 33.3% margin, and a 50% margin needs a 100% markup.
