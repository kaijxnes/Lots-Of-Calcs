15% margin on £150

To make a 15% margin on something that costs £150, sell it for £176.47.

The markup that is17.6%

Careful: 15% markup is different

Adding 15% to the cost (a 15% markup) gives £172.50 — a profit of £22.50, which is only a 13% margin. If you meant a 15% markup, that's your price; if you need 15% of the selling price as profit, it's £176.47.

Prices before VAT. Work out any cost and price in the margin vs markup calculator; the sums are the same in any currency.

How to work it out

Margin: price = cost ÷ (1 − margin) = £150 ÷ 0.85 = £176.47. Check: £26.47 profit ÷ £176.47 = 15%.

Markup: price = cost × (1 + markup) = £150 × 1.15 = £172.50.

Frequently asked questions

What price gives a 15% margin on £150?

£176.47: £150 ÷ (1 − 0.15) = £150 ÷ 0.85. Profit £26.47 is 15% of £176.47.

What is a 15% markup on £150?

£172.50: £150 × 1.15. That's a £22.50 profit, which is only a 13% margin.

What markup do I need for a 15% margin?

17.6%. Markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin): 0.15 ÷ 0.85 = 0.18.

What's the difference between margin and markup?

Both measure the same profit. Margin divides it by the selling price; markup divides it by the cost. So a margin is always smaller than the matching markup: 50% markup is a 33.3% margin, and a 50% margin needs a 100% markup.
