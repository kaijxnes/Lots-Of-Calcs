60% margin on £200

To make a 60% margin on something that costs £200, sell it for £500.

Price for a 60% margin£500
Profit£300
The markup that is150%

Careful: 60% markup is different

Adding 60% to the cost (a 60% markup) gives £320 — a profit of £120, which is only a 37.5% margin. If you meant a 60% markup, that's your price; if you need 60% of the selling price as profit, it's £500.

Prices before VAT. Work out any cost and price in the margin vs markup calculator; the sums are the same in any currency.

How to work it out

Margin: price = cost ÷ (1 − margin) = £200 ÷ 0.4 = £500. Check: £300 profit ÷ £500 = 60%.

Markup: price = cost × (1 + markup) = £200 × 1.6 = £320.

Frequently asked questions

What price gives a 60% margin on £200?

£500: £200 ÷ (1 − 0.6) = £200 ÷ 0.4. Profit £300 is 60% of £500.

What is a 60% markup on £200?

£320: £200 × 1.6. That's a £120 profit, which is only a 37.5% margin.

What markup do I need for a 60% margin?

150%. Markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin): 0.6 ÷ 0.4 = 1.5.

What's the difference between margin and markup?

Both measure the same profit. Margin divides it by the selling price; markup divides it by the cost. So a margin is always smaller than the matching markup: 50% markup is a 33.3% margin, and a 50% margin needs a 100% markup.
