15% margin on £250

To make a 15% margin on something that costs £250, sell it for £294.12.

Careful: 15% markup is different

Adding 15% to the cost (a 15% markup) gives £287.50 — a profit of £37.50, which is only a 13% margin. If you meant a 15% markup, that's your price; if you need 15% of the selling price as profit, it's £294.12.

Prices before VAT. Work out any cost and price in the margin vs markup calculator; the sums are the same in any currency.

How to work it out

Margin: price = cost ÷ (1 − margin) = £250 ÷ 0.85 = £294.12. Check: £44.12 profit ÷ £294.12 = 15%.

Markup: price = cost × (1 + markup) = £250 × 1.15 = £287.50.

Frequently asked questions

What price gives a 15% margin on £250?

£294.12: £250 ÷ (1 − 0.15) = £250 ÷ 0.85. Profit £44.12 is 15% of £294.12.

What is a 15% markup on £250?

£287.50: £250 × 1.15. That's a £37.50 profit, which is only a 13% margin.

What markup do I need for a 15% margin?

17.6%. Markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin): 0.15 ÷ 0.85 = 0.18.

What's the difference between margin and markup?

Both measure the same profit. Margin divides it by the selling price; markup divides it by the cost. So a margin is always smaller than the matching markup: 50% markup is a 33.3% margin, and a 50% margin needs a 100% markup.
