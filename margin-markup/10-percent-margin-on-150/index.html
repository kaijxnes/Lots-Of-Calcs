10% margin on £150

To make a 10% margin on something that costs £150, sell it for £166.67.

Careful: 10% markup is different

Adding 10% to the cost (a 10% markup) gives £165 — a profit of £15, which is only a 9.1% margin. If you meant a 10% markup, that's your price; if you need 10% of the selling price as profit, it's £166.67.

Prices before VAT. Work out any cost and price in the margin vs markup calculator; the sums are the same in any currency.

How to work it out

Margin: price = cost ÷ (1 − margin) = £150 ÷ 0.9 = £166.67. Check: £16.67 profit ÷ £166.67 = 10%.

Markup: price = cost × (1 + markup) = £150 × 1.1 = £165.

Frequently asked questions

What price gives a 10% margin on £150?

£166.67: £150 ÷ (1 − 0.1) = £150 ÷ 0.9. Profit £16.67 is 10% of £166.67.

What is a 10% markup on £150?

£165: £150 × 1.1. That's a £15 profit, which is only a 9.1% margin.

What markup do I need for a 10% margin?

11.1%. Markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin): 0.1 ÷ 0.9 = 0.11.

What's the difference between margin and markup?

Both measure the same profit. Margin divides it by the selling price; markup divides it by the cost. So a margin is always smaller than the matching markup: 50% markup is a 33.3% margin, and a 50% margin needs a 100% markup.
