30% margin on £40

To make a 30% margin on something that costs £40, sell it for £57.14.

Careful: 30% markup is different

Adding 30% to the cost (a 30% markup) gives £52 — a profit of £12, which is only a 23.1% margin. If you meant a 30% markup, that's your price; if you need 30% of the selling price as profit, it's £57.14.

Prices before VAT. Work out any cost and price in the margin vs markup calculator; the sums are the same in any currency.

How to work it out

Margin: price = cost ÷ (1 − margin) = £40 ÷ 0.7 = £57.14. Check: £17.14 profit ÷ £57.14 = 30%.

Markup: price = cost × (1 + markup) = £40 × 1.3 = £52.

Frequently asked questions

What price gives a 30% margin on £40?

£57.14: £40 ÷ (1 − 0.3) = £40 ÷ 0.7. Profit £17.14 is 30% of £57.14.

What is a 30% markup on £40?

£52: £40 × 1.3. That's a £12 profit, which is only a 23.1% margin.

What markup do I need for a 30% margin?

42.9%. Markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin): 0.3 ÷ 0.7 = 0.43.

What's the difference between margin and markup?

Both measure the same profit. Margin divides it by the selling price; markup divides it by the cost. So a margin is always smaller than the matching markup: 50% markup is a 33.3% margin, and a 50% margin needs a 100% markup.
