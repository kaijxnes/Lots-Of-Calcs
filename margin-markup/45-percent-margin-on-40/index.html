Careful: 45% markup is different

Adding 45% to the cost (a 45% markup) gives £58 — a profit of £18, which is only a 31% margin. If you meant a 45% markup, that's your price; if you need 45% of the selling price as profit, it's £72.73.

Prices before VAT. Work out any cost and price in the margin vs markup calculator; the sums are the same in any currency.

How to work it out

Margin: price = cost ÷ (1 − margin) = £40 ÷ 0.55 = £72.73. Check: £32.73 profit ÷ £72.73 = 45%.

Markup: price = cost × (1 + markup) = £40 × 1.45 = £58.

Frequently asked questions

What price gives a 45% margin on £40?

£72.73: £40 ÷ (1 − 0.45) = £40 ÷ 0.55. Profit £32.73 is 45% of £72.73.

What is a 45% markup on £40?

£58: £40 × 1.45. That's a £18 profit, which is only a 31% margin.

What markup do I need for a 45% margin?

81.8%. Markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin): 0.45 ÷ 0.55 = 0.82.

What's the difference between margin and markup?

Both measure the same profit. Margin divides it by the selling price; markup divides it by the cost. So a margin is always smaller than the matching markup: 50% markup is a 33.3% margin, and a 50% margin needs a 100% markup.
