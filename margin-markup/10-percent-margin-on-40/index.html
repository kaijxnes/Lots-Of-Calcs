10% margin on £40

To make a 10% margin on something that costs £40, sell it for £44.44.

Careful: 10% markup is different

Adding 10% to the cost (a 10% markup) gives £44 — a profit of £4, which is only a 9.1% margin. If you meant a 10% markup, that's your price; if you need 10% of the selling price as profit, it's £44.44.

Prices before VAT. Work out any cost and price in the margin vs markup calculator; the sums are the same in any currency.

How to work it out

Margin: price = cost ÷ (1 − margin) = £40 ÷ 0.9 = £44.44. Check: £4.44 profit ÷ £44.44 = 10%.

Markup: price = cost × (1 + markup) = £40 × 1.1 = £44.

Frequently asked questions

What price gives a 10% margin on £40?

£44.44: £40 ÷ (1 − 0.1) = £40 ÷ 0.9. Profit £4.44 is 10% of £44.44.

What is a 10% markup on £40?

£44: £40 × 1.1. That's a £4 profit, which is only a 9.1% margin.

What markup do I need for a 10% margin?

11.1%. Markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin): 0.1 ÷ 0.9 = 0.11.

What's the difference between margin and markup?

Both measure the same profit. Margin divides it by the selling price; markup divides it by the cost. So a margin is always smaller than the matching markup: 50% markup is a 33.3% margin, and a 50% margin needs a 100% markup.
