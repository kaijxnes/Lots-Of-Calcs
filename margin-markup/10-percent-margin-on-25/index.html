10% margin on £25

To make a 10% margin on something that costs £25, sell it for £27.78.

Price for a 10% margin£27.78
Profit£2.78
The markup that is11.1%

Careful: 10% markup is different

Adding 10% to the cost (a 10% markup) gives £27.50 — a profit of £2.50, which is only a 9.1% margin. If you meant a 10% markup, that's your price; if you need 10% of the selling price as profit, it's £27.78.

Prices before VAT. Work out any cost and price in the margin vs markup calculator; the sums are the same in any currency.

How to work it out

Margin: price = cost ÷ (1 − margin) = £25 ÷ 0.9 = £27.78. Check: £2.78 profit ÷ £27.78 = 10%.

Markup: price = cost × (1 + markup) = £25 × 1.1 = £27.50.

Frequently asked questions

What price gives a 10% margin on £25?

£27.78: £25 ÷ (1 − 0.1) = £25 ÷ 0.9. Profit £2.78 is 10% of £27.78.

What is a 10% markup on £25?

£27.50: £25 × 1.1. That's a £2.50 profit, which is only a 9.1% margin.

What markup do I need for a 10% margin?

11.1%. Markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin): 0.1 ÷ 0.9 = 0.11.

What's the difference between margin and markup?

Both measure the same profit. Margin divides it by the selling price; markup divides it by the cost. So a margin is always smaller than the matching markup: 50% markup is a 33.3% margin, and a 50% margin needs a 100% markup.
