75% margin on £5

To make a 75% margin on something that costs £5, sell it for £20.

Price for a 75% margin£20
Profit£15
The markup that is300%

Careful: 75% markup is different

Adding 75% to the cost (a 75% markup) gives £8.75 — a profit of £3.75, which is only a 42.9% margin. If you meant a 75% markup, that's your price; if you need 75% of the selling price as profit, it's £20.

Prices before VAT. Work out any cost and price in the margin vs markup calculator; the sums are the same in any currency.

How to work it out

Margin: price = cost ÷ (1 − margin) = £5 ÷ 0.25 = £20. Check: £15 profit ÷ £20 = 75%.

Markup: price = cost × (1 + markup) = £5 × 1.75 = £8.75.

Frequently asked questions

What price gives a 75% margin on £5?

£20: £5 ÷ (1 − 0.75) = £5 ÷ 0.25. Profit £15 is 75% of £20.

What is a 75% markup on £5?

£8.75: £5 × 1.75. That's a £3.75 profit, which is only a 42.9% margin.

What markup do I need for a 75% margin?

300%. Markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin): 0.75 ÷ 0.25 = 3.

What's the difference between margin and markup?

Both measure the same profit. Margin divides it by the selling price; markup divides it by the cost. So a margin is always smaller than the matching markup: 50% markup is a 33.3% margin, and a 50% margin needs a 100% markup.
