20% margin on £150

To make a 20% margin on something that costs £150, sell it for £187.50.

Price for a 20% margin£187.50
Profit£37.50
The markup that is25%

Careful: 20% markup is different

Adding 20% to the cost (a 20% markup) gives £180 — a profit of £30, which is only a 16.7% margin. If you meant a 20% markup, that's your price; if you need 20% of the selling price as profit, it's £187.50.

Prices before VAT. Work out any cost and price in the margin vs markup calculator; the sums are the same in any currency.

How to work it out

Margin: price = cost ÷ (1 − margin) = £150 ÷ 0.8 = £187.50. Check: £37.50 profit ÷ £187.50 = 20%.

Markup: price = cost × (1 + markup) = £150 × 1.2 = £180.

Frequently asked questions

What price gives a 20% margin on £150?

£187.50: £150 ÷ (1 − 0.2) = £150 ÷ 0.8. Profit £37.50 is 20% of £187.50.

What is a 20% markup on £150?

£180: £150 × 1.2. That's a £30 profit, which is only a 16.7% margin.

What markup do I need for a 20% margin?

25%. Markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin): 0.2 ÷ 0.8 = 0.25.

What's the difference between margin and markup?

Both measure the same profit. Margin divides it by the selling price; markup divides it by the cost. So a margin is always smaller than the matching markup: 50% markup is a 33.3% margin, and a 50% margin needs a 100% markup.
