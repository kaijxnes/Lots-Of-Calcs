20% margin on £1,000

To make a 20% margin on something that costs £1,000, sell it for £1,250.

Careful: 20% markup is different

Adding 20% to the cost (a 20% markup) gives £1,200 — a profit of £200, which is only a 16.7% margin. If you meant a 20% markup, that's your price; if you need 20% of the selling price as profit, it's £1,250.

Prices before VAT. Work out any cost and price in the margin vs markup calculator; the sums are the same in any currency.

How to work it out

Margin: price = cost ÷ (1 − margin) = £1,000 ÷ 0.8 = £1,250. Check: £250 profit ÷ £1,250 = 20%.

Markup: price = cost × (1 + markup) = £1,000 × 1.2 = £1,200.

Frequently asked questions

What price gives a 20% margin on £1,000?

£1,250: £1,000 ÷ (1 − 0.2) = £1,000 ÷ 0.8. Profit £250 is 20% of £1,250.

What is a 20% markup on £1,000?

£1,200: £1,000 × 1.2. That's a £200 profit, which is only a 16.7% margin.

What markup do I need for a 20% margin?

25%. Markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin): 0.2 ÷ 0.8 = 0.25.

What's the difference between margin and markup?

Both measure the same profit. Margin divides it by the selling price; markup divides it by the cost. So a margin is always smaller than the matching markup: 50% markup is a 33.3% margin, and a 50% margin needs a 100% markup.
