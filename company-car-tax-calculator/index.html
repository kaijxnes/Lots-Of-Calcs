How company car tax is worked out

The taxable benefit is the car's list price, plus optional accessories, less anything you paid towards it (up to £5,000), multiplied by its appropriate percentage. The percentage depends on the car's CO2 emissions and, for cars emitting 1 to 50g/km, its electric range. Diesels that don't meet Euro 6d (RDE2) add 4%, up to the 37% maximum. The benefit is reduced for days the car wasn't available (before you got it, after you gave it back, or a spell of 30 days or more) and by anything you pay for private use. You pay Income Tax on it at your rate, usually through your tax code, and your employer pays Class 1A National Insurance at 15%.

Some plug-in hybrids are treated as emitting 1g/km: those registered from 1 January 2025 to 5 April 2028 with 51g/km or more on the registration certificate, an electric range of at least 1 mile, and no Euro 6d-ISC-FCM or Euro 6e in the Euro status section. Fuel your employer pays for private journeys is taxed separately. Rates already set: electric cars rise by 2 percentage points a year in 2028/29 and 2029/30, and 1 to 50g/km hybrids go to 18% and 19%.

Frequently asked questions

How is company car tax calculated?

List price plus accessories, less up to £5,000 you paid towards it, times the appropriate percentage for its CO2 (and electric range for 1 to 50g/km). Then reductions for days it wasn't available and payments for private use. The result is taxed at your Income Tax rate.

What is the BIK rate for electric cars in 2026/27?

4% (it was 3% in 2025/26). It rises by 2 percentage points a year in 2028/29 and 2029/30.

What is the diesel supplement?

4% added to the appropriate percentage for diesel cars that don't meet Euro 6d (RDE2), up to the 37% maximum.

Is fuel included?

No. If your employer pays for fuel for private journeys, that's a separate taxable benefit.

What list price is used?

The manufacturer's published price on the day before the car was first registered, including standard accessories, VAT and delivery but not the registration fee. It isn't the price paid, which may include discounts. Second-hand cars use the same list price as when new.
