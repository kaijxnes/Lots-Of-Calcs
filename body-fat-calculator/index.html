Frequently asked questions

How does the US Navy body fat method work?

It estimates body fat from tape measurements: neck and waist (and hips for women) and height, using equations published by the Naval Health Research Center (Hodgdon and Beckett, 1984). Measurements go into the formula in inches; the calculator converts centimetres for you.

Where do I measure?

Neck just below the larynx; waist at the navel for men, at the narrowest point for women; hips at the widest point. Keep the tape flat and snug without squeezing, and measure two or three times.

How accurate is it?

It's an estimate from a few measurements — useful for tracking change over time with the same method, less so as an exact figure for one person.

What does an example give?

A man 5 ft 10 in (70 in) tall with a 34 in waist and 15 in neck comes out at about 17.5% body fat.

An estimate for general information, not medical advice. Source: Hodgdon & Beckett — Prediction of percent body fat for U.S. Navy women from body circumferences and height (Naval Health Research Center, 1984); LibreTexts — The U.S. Navy body fat estimation formula.
