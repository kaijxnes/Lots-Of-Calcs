How a Lifetime ISA works

You can open one from 18 until you're 40, and pay in up to £4,000 a year until you're 50. The government adds a 25% bonus, up to £1,000 a year. The £4,000 counts towards your £20,000 yearly ISA allowance (2026/27). It can hold cash, stocks and shares, or both. From 50 you can't pay in or get the bonus, but the account stays open.

Taking money out

You can take it out without a charge to buy your first home, from age 60, or if you're terminally ill with less than 12 months to live. For a home, you must buy at least 12 months after your first payment, with a mortgage and a conveyancer or solicitor; if the property costs more than £450,000, there's a 20% withdrawal charge. For anything else there's a 25% charge on the whole amount, bonus included, which takes back more than the bonus: pay in £4,000, get £1,000 of bonus, and taking the £5,000 out leaves £3,750, £250 less than you put in.

Frequently asked questions

How much is the Lifetime ISA bonus?

25% of what you pay in, up to £1,000 a year on the maximum £4,000. Paying in until you're 50, that's up to £32,000 if you start at 18.

What's the Lifetime ISA withdrawal penalty?

25% of the amount you take out, including the bonus, unless it's for a first home, you're 60 or over, or you're terminally ill. On £4,000 paid in plus £1,000 bonus, you'd get back £3,750.

What's the house price limit for a Lifetime ISA?

£450,000. GOV.UK says that above it you'll pay a 20% withdrawal charge; at £450,000 or under there's no charge.

Who can open a Lifetime ISA?

UK residents (and some crown servants and armed forces members abroad) aged 18 or over but under 40. You can keep paying in until 50.

When can I use my Lifetime ISA to buy a house?

At least 12 months after your first payment into it, buying your first home with a mortgage and a conveyancer or solicitor, who receives the money from the ISA provider.
