Frequently asked questions

How many calories should I eat to lose weight?

About 600 fewer than you need to stay the same weight, as the NHS advises. For a 35-year-old woman who is 165 cm, 75 kg and lightly active that's about 1,640 a day (from 2,240). The NHS 12-week weight loss plan gives most men 1,900 and most women 1,400 calories a day.

How many calories do I need a day?

On average about 2,000 for women and 2,500 for men (NHS), but it depends on your size, age and how active you are — the calculator works out an estimate for you.

How is it worked out?

Resting energy comes from the Mifflin–St Jeor equation: 10 × weight in kg + 6.25 × height in cm − 5 × age, plus 5 for men or minus 161 for women. That's multiplied by a physical activity level from the FAO/WHO/UNU report on human energy requirements: 1.40–1.69 for a sedentary or light lifestyle, 1.70–1.99 active, 2.00–2.40 vigorous.

How fast should I lose weight?

The NHS advises aiming to lose 0.5 to 1 kg (1 to 2 lb) a week, and cutting around 600 calories a day from what you need to stay the same weight. Diets of 800 calories a day or less are very low calorie diets; NICE and the NHS advise them only with medical supervision.

How accurate is it?

In studies comparing the common equations with measured resting energy, Mifflin–St Jeor was the most likely to come within 10% — but some people's needs are still higher or lower. Track your weight for a few weeks and adjust: if you aren't losing at about the rate expected, eat a little less or move a little more.
