The NHS advises aiming to lose 0.5 to 1 kg (1 to 2 lb) a week, and cutting around 600 calories a day from what you need to stay the same weight. Time from calories uses the usual rule of thumb that about 7,700 calories make up 1 kg of body fat (3,500 per lb). In practice weight loss slows over time, as a lighter body burns fewer calories, so long goals take longer than this.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to lose 4 kg?

4 to 8 weeks at the NHS's advised 0.5 to 1 kg a week. 4 kg is 8.8 lb, or 0st 9lb. Cutting around 600 calories a day — the NHS's suggestion — loses about 0.5 kg a week, so about 7 weeks.

If I start on 1 January, when will I have lost 4 kg?

At 600 fewer calories a day, around 21 February 2027; at 0.5 to 1 kg a week, between 29 January 2027 and 26 February 2027.

How many calories should I eat to lose weight?

Work out what you need to stay the same weight — the calculator above does this from your age, height, weight and how active you are — and eat about 600 calories a day less. The NHS's 12-week plan gives most men 1,900 and most women 1,400 calories a day.

Can I lose it faster?

The NHS advises aiming to lose 0.5 to 1 kg (1 to 2 lb) a week, and cutting around 600 calories a day from what you need to stay the same weight. Losing weight much faster usually means eating too little to get the nutrients you need, and it's harder to keep off. Diets of 800 calories a day or less are very low calorie diets; NICE and the NHS advise them only with medical supervision.

Why does weight loss slow down?
