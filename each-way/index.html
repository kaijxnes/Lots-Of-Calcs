About this calculator

An each-way bet is really two separate bets of the same size: one that your selection wins, and one that it finishes in the places. That's why a "£10 each-way" bet costs £20 — the unit stake is what goes on each part, and the total staked is always double it.

The place part is settled at a fraction of the win odds, set by the bookmaker's place terms. At 1/5 terms, a 10/1 selection pays 2/1 for placing, which is 3.00 in decimal. The general rule this uses is place decimal odds = 1 + (win decimal odds − 1) × the place fraction.

If your selection wins, both parts pay out, because a winner has also placed. If it only places, the win part is lost and the place part pays. If it does neither, you lose the whole stake. This calculator follows those standard UK each-way rules.

Assumptions: the number of places offered and the place fraction are whatever your bookmaker advertised for that race or market, so enter the terms they quoted. Dead heats and Rule 4 deductions are not modelled, and either will change the actual settled return. Mark a non-runner as Void: in a multiple it counts as odds of 1.00, and on its own the stake is returned.

How to work out an each-way bet

1. Double the stake. £5 each-way is a £5 win bet plus a £5 place bet: £10 in total.

2. Work out the place odds. Take the profit part of the odds and apply the place terms. At 8/1 with 1/5 terms, the place part is paid at 8/5, which is 1.6/1 (2.60 in decimal).

3. Settle each part. The win part pays at the full odds only if it wins; the place part pays if it finishes anywhere in the places, winning included.

Each-way doubles and accumulators

An each-way double is a win double plus a place double, so £5 each-way costs £10. Take a 5/1 and a 3/1 at 1/5 terms: the win double pays 6.00 × 4.00 = 24.00, and the place double pays 2.00 × 1.60 = 3.20.

If both win, you get both doubles: £120 + £16 = £136. If either one only places, the win double is lost and the place double returns £16, a £6 profit on the £10. If either misses the places, both doubles lose. Each-way trebles and accumulators work the same way with more legs. Add selections in the calculator above to work any of them out.

Frequently asked questions

How do you work out an each-way bet?

Double the unit stake for the total cost, work out the place odds as the win odds' profit part times the place terms (8/1 at 1/5 is 8/5), then add up whichever parts pay. £5 each-way at 8/1, 1/5 terms, returns £58 if it wins and £13 if it only places.

Is each-way worth it on a favourite?

Rarely. At short odds the place part pays so little that a placed finish loses money overall: at 2/1 with 1/5 terms, £5 each-way that only places returns £7 against £10 staked. Each-way suits bigger prices in races with good place terms.

What is an each-way double?

Two bets: a win double and a place double, each at the unit stake. Each double needs both selections to do their part, so if one of them misses the places, the whole each-way double is lost.

Does a £10 each-way bet cost £10 or £20?

It costs £20. Each-way is two bets of £10 — one on the win, one on the place — so the unit stake you enter is doubled to give the total staked.

Do I get both parts if my selection wins?

Yes. A winner has also placed, so the win part and the place part both pay out. That is why the return on a winning each-way bet is much larger than on one that only places.

What do place terms like 1/5 mean?

They are the fraction of the win odds paid on the place part. At 1/5 terms a 10/1 selection is paid at 2/1 for placing, so a £10 place part returns £30 including the stake.
