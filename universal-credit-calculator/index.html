How your wages affect Universal Credit

For every £1 you earn, your Universal Credit goes down by 55p, so each extra £1 of take-home pay leaves you 45p better off. It's worked out on your take-home pay, after tax, National Insurance and workplace pension, over each monthly assessment period. There's no limit to the hours you can work and still get it. If you or your partner are responsible for a child, or have a health condition or disability that affects your ability to work, you get a work allowance: you can earn £710 a month before your payment starts to reduce, or £427 if you get help with housing costs (or live in temporary accommodation arranged by the council). Statutory pay, such as Statutory Sick Pay or Maternity Pay, counts as earnings.

What Universal Credit pays a month (2026/27)

You get one standard allowance for your household, plus extra amounts that apply to you. Since 6 April 2026 there's an amount for every child: the 2-child limit has ended. The health amount is for people who can't work because of a health condition or disability; there are different rules if you reported a health condition before 6 April 2026. A single person can't get the carer amount as well as the health amount. Childcare is paid back at 85% when you're working (both of you, if you're a couple, unless one of you can't work because of a disability or health condition).

What else reduces it

Savings: nothing comes off for the first £6,000; then £4.35 a month for every £250 (or part of £250) up to £16,000. With more than £16,000 you can't get Universal Credit. Other benefits such as Carer's Allowance, New Style JSA or ESA, Maternity Allowance and the State Pension are taken off pound for pound. The benefit cap limits total benefits, including Child Benefit, to £1,835 a month for a couple or single parent (£1,229.42 single, no children) outside Greater London, and £2,110.25 (£1,413.92) inside it. It doesn't apply if you earn £881 or more a month between you, get the health or carer amount, or someone in the household gets a disability benefit such as PIP or DLA. Payments can also be reduced to repay an advance, an overpayment or debts.

This is an estimate for employed earnings. Paid weekly, every 2 weeks or every 4 weeks, some months have an extra payday, and Universal Credit is lower that month. For your own figures, your online account shows each month's statement; for after-tax pay, try the salary calculator.

Frequently asked questions

How much does Universal Credit go down when I earn?

55p for every £1 of take-home pay, after any work allowance. So an extra £100 of take-home pay reduces Universal Credit by £55 and leaves you £45 better off.

What is the Universal Credit work allowance?

If you or your partner are responsible for a child or have a health condition or disability that affects your ability to work, you can earn £710 a month before Universal Credit reduces, or £427 if you get help with housing costs (2026/27).

How much can I earn before Universal Credit stops?

It depends on your household. A single person over 25 with no children and no help with rent gets nothing from about £772.55 a month of take-home pay; a couple over 25 with 2 children and £800 of rent, from about £4,199.45.

Is Universal Credit based on gross or net pay?

Take-home pay: earnings after Income Tax, National Insurance and workplace pension contributions. Statutory pay such as Statutory Sick Pay counts as earnings.

Does the 2-child limit still apply?

No. It ended on 6 April 2026, and Universal Credit now pays the child amount for every child. The benefit cap still applies.

Do savings affect Universal Credit?

Savings over £6,000 reduce it by £4.35 a month for every £250 or part of £250, up to £16,000. Above £16,000 you can't get Universal Credit.
