How long it really took: about 6 years 7 months. NASA's MESSENGER launched 3 August 2004 and went into orbit on 18 March 2011, after flybys of Earth, Venus and Mercury to slow it down.

These are straight-line times at a steady speed, as if the planets stood still. Real spacecraft can't fly straight there: they launch into a curved path round the Sun that meets the planet where it will be, so the route is longer, and they coast most of the way.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to get to Mercury?

About 6 years 7 months for NASA's MESSENGER, which launched 3 August 2004 and went into orbit on 18 March 2011, after flybys of Earth, Venus and Mercury to slow it down. Flying in a straight line at Apollo 10's top speed (24,791 mph) would take 81 days at its closest.

How far away is Mercury?

At its closest, 77.3 million km (48 million miles); at its farthest, 221.9 million km (137.9 million miles). The distance changes as Earth and Mercury go round the Sun at different speeds.

How long would it take to drive to Mercury?

78.3 years at 70 mph without stopping at its closest, or 225 years at its farthest.

How long does light take to get to Mercury?

4 minutes 18 seconds at its closest and 12 minutes 20 seconds at its farthest. That is also how long a radio message takes.
