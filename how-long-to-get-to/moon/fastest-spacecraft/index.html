Frequently asked questions

How long would it take to get to the Moon at the fastest spacecraft's speed?

33 minutes 20 seconds on average, covering 384,400 km (238,855 miles) non-stop at 692,018 km/h (430,000 mph). At its farthest, 405,500 km (251,966 miles), it would take 35 minutes 9 seconds.

How is that worked out?

Distance divided by speed: 384,400 km ÷ 692,017.92 km/h = 1 hours. These are straight-line times at a steady speed, as if the planets stood still. Real spacecraft can't fly straight there: they launch into a curved path round the Sun that meets the planet where it will be, so the route is longer, and they coast most of the way.

How long did it actually take to get to the Moon?

About 3 days 4 hours for NASA's Apollo 11, which launched 16 July 1969 and went into orbit round the Moon on 19 July.

How long does light take to get to the Moon?

1.3 seconds on average.
