How long it really took: about 9 years 6 months. NASA's New Horizons launched 19 January 2006 and flew past Pluto on 14 July 2015, with a speed boost from Jupiter on the way.

These are straight-line times at a steady speed, as if the planets stood still. Real spacecraft can't fly straight there: they launch into a curved path round the Sun that meets the planet where it will be, so the route is longer, and they coast most of the way.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to get to Pluto?

About 9 years 6 months for NASA's New Horizons, which launched 19 January 2006 and flew past Pluto on 14 July 2015, with a speed boost from Jupiter on the way. Flying in a straight line at Apollo 10's top speed (24,791 mph) would take 12.3 years at its closest.

How far away is Pluto?

At its closest, 4.3 billion km (2.7 billion miles); at its farthest, 7.5 billion km (4.7 billion miles). The distance changes as Earth and Pluto go round the Sun at different speeds.

How long would it take to drive to Pluto?

4,339 years at 70 mph without stopping at its closest, or 7,623 years at its farthest.

How long does light take to get to Pluto?

3 hours 58 minutes at its closest and 6 hours 59 minutes at its farthest. That is also how long a radio message takes.
