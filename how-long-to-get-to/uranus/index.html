How long it really took: about 8 years 5 months. NASA's Voyager 2 launched 20 August 1977 and flew past Uranus on 24 January 1986 (the only visit so far).

These are straight-line times at a steady speed, as if the planets stood still. Real spacecraft can't fly straight there: they launch into a curved path round the Sun that meets the planet where it will be, so the route is longer, and they coast most of the way.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to get to Uranus?

About 8 years 5 months for NASA's Voyager 2, which launched 20 August 1977 and flew past Uranus on 24 January 1986 (the only visit so far). Flying in a straight line at Apollo 10's top speed (24,791 mph) would take 7.4 years at its closest.

How far away is Uranus?

At its closest, 2.6 billion km (1.6 billion miles); at its farthest, 3.2 billion km (2 billion miles). The distance changes as Earth and Uranus go round the Sun at different speeds.

How long would it take to drive to Uranus?

2,613 years at 70 mph without stopping at its closest, or 3,193 years at its farthest.

How long does light take to get to Uranus?

2 hours 23 minutes at its closest and 2 hours 55 minutes at its farthest. That is also how long a radio message takes.
