Frequently asked questions

How long does light take to get to Jupiter?

32 minutes 43 seconds at its closest, covering 588.5 million km (365.7 million miles) non-stop at 299,792 km/s. At its farthest, 968.5 million km (601.8 million miles), it would take 53 minutes 51 seconds.

How is that worked out?

Distance divided by speed: 588.5 million km ÷ 1,079,252,848.8 km/h = 1 hours. These are straight-line times at a steady speed, as if the planets stood still. Real spacecraft can't fly straight there: they launch into a curved path round the Sun that meets the planet where it will be, so the route is longer, and they coast most of the way.

How long did it actually take to get to Jupiter?

About 4 years 11 months for NASA's Juno, which launched 5 August 2011 and went into orbit on 5 July 2016, after swinging back past Earth in 2013.
