How long it really took: about 12 years. NASA's Voyager 2 launched 20 August 1977 and flew past Neptune on 25 August 1989 (the only visit so far).

These are straight-line times at a steady speed, as if the planets stood still. Real spacecraft can't fly straight there: they launch into a curved path round the Sun that meets the planet where it will be, so the route is longer, and they coast most of the way.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to get to Neptune?

About 12 years for NASA's Voyager 2, which launched 20 August 1977 and flew past Neptune on 25 August 1989 (the only visit so far). Flying in a straight line at Apollo 10's top speed (24,791 mph) would take 12.3 years at its closest.

How far away is Neptune?

At its closest, 4.3 billion km (2.7 billion miles); at its farthest, 4.7 billion km (2.9 billion miles). The distance changes as Earth and Neptune go round the Sun at different speeds.

How long would it take to drive to Neptune?

4,374 years at 70 mph without stopping at its closest, or 4,771 years at its farthest.

How long does light take to get to Neptune?

4 hours at its closest and 4 hours 22 minutes at its farthest. That is also how long a radio message takes.
