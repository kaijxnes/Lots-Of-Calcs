How it works

With relief at source, your provider adds basic-rate relief to what you pay and higher-rate taxpayers claim the rest through self assessment; with net pay or salary sacrifice it comes off before tax. Salary sacrifice also saves National Insurance, which makes the pension better still — this calculator leaves that out. Employer contributions only go into a pension, so any you get tip the result that way.

Limits for 2026

ISA: up to £20,000 a year in total across your ISAs (2026/27), and withdrawals are tax-free at any time.

Pension: tax relief at your highest rate on contributions up to the £60,000 annual allowance (or your earnings, if lower). You can usually take money out from 55 — 57 from April 2028.

Up to 25% of a pension can be taken tax-free, within the £268,275 lump sum allowance; the rest is taxed as income.

Frequently asked questions

Is a ISA or a pension better?

It comes down to your tax rate now against your tax rate when you take the money out. For the same cost to you, the pension wins if you'll pay less tax later than you save now, and the 25% tax-free part tips it further; the ISA wins if you'll pay more. At 20% now the two break even at about 26.7% later. In the example, £200 a month for 25 years leaves £117,147 in the ISA and £124,469 from the pension after tax.

Why does the same monthly amount buy a bigger contribution?

Because pension contributions are made before tax. Paying £200 from take-home at a 20% rate funds £250 a month of contributions, so the pension grows to £146,434 — but £21,965 of that is tax when it comes out.

What are the limits?

ISA: up to £20,000 a year in total across your ISAs (2026/27), and withdrawals are tax-free at any time. Pension: tax relief at your highest rate on contributions up to the £60,000 annual allowance (or your earnings, if lower). You can usually take money out from 55 — 57 from April 2028. Up to 25% of a pension can be taken tax-free, within the £268,275 lump sum allowance; the rest is taxed as income.

Should I just use both?

Many people do: the pension for money taxed at a high rate now, the ISA for flexibility, since ISA money can be taken out at any time. If you aren't sure what your tax rate will be later, splitting between them hedges the guess.
