Money when you have a baby

The pay, benefits and childcare help you can get, with 2026/27 figures.

Maternity pay

Statutory Maternity Pay is paid for up to 39 weeks: 90% of your average weekly earnings for the first 6 weeks, then £194.32 a week or 90%, whichever is lower, for the other 33 (2026/27). You need average earnings of at least £129 a week. On £30,000 a year (£576.92 a week) that's £519.23 a week for 6 weeks and £194.32 after: £9,527.94 in all, before tax. Your employer may pay more under a company scheme. The maternity pay calculator works it out for your pay.

Paternity pay and leave

Your partner can take up to 2 weeks of Paternity Leave, together or as two separate weeks, any time from the birth until 52 weeks after it. Statutory Paternity Pay is £194.32 a week or 90% of average weekly earnings, whichever is lower, for those weeks: up to £388.64. They need 26 weeks with their employer by the 15th week before the due date and average earnings of at least £129 a week. Tax and National Insurance come off.

Shared Parental Leave and Pay

You can share up to 50 weeks of leave and 37 weeks of pay between you in the first year, by ending maternity leave and pay early. Shared Parental Pay is the same £194.32 a week or 90% of earnings, whichever is lower. GOV.UK's example: after 22 weeks of maternity leave and pay, you can share 30 weeks of leave and 17 weeks of pay. You give your employer 8 weeks' notice. The paternity pay calculator shows what's left to share.

Child Benefit

Child Benefit is £27.05 a week for the eldest or only child and £17.90 for each other child: £2,337 a year for two children. If either parent's adjusted net income is over £60,000, the High Income Child Benefit Charge takes back 1% for every £200 above it, all of it from £80,000; the Child Benefit charge calculator works it out.

Universal Credit

If you get Universal Credit, it adds £303.94 a month for each child (2026/27); the 2-child limit ended on 6 April 2026, so that's every child. Having a child also gives you a work allowance, so you can earn £710 a month (£427 with help for housing) before it starts to reduce by 55p for each £1. The Universal Credit calculator estimates it.

Help with childcare

When you go back to work, there are two main ways to get help with childcare costs, and you can't have both. Tax-Free Childcare adds £2 for every £8 you pay in, up to £2,000 a year per child (£4,000 if disabled), if you each earn at least £2,643.68 over 3 months and neither has income over £100,000. Universal Credit pays back up to 85% of costs, up to £1,071.09 a month for one child, but as part of an award that shrinks as you earn more. The childcare help calculator shows which gives you more.

Frequently asked questions

How much is maternity pay?

90% of your average weekly earnings for the first 6 weeks, then £194.32 a week or 90% (whichever is lower) for 33 weeks (2026/27). On £30,000 a year that's £9,527.94 in total before tax.

How much is paternity pay?

£194.32 a week or 90% of average weekly earnings, whichever is lower, for up to 2 weeks.

How much Child Benefit do you get for a baby?

£27.05 a week for an eldest or only child and £17.90 for each other child.

Can we share parental leave?

Yes: up to 50 weeks of leave and 37 weeks of pay, by ending maternity leave and pay early. After 22 weeks of maternity leave, that's 30 weeks of leave and 17 of pay to share.

Does Universal Credit go up when you have a baby?

Yes, by £303.94 a month for each child (2026/27), and you get a work allowance. The 2-child limit ended on 6 April 2026.
