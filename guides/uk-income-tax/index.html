Income tax bands for 2026/27

In England, Wales and Northern Ireland, the first £12,570 of your income each tax year is tax-free: that's the personal allowance. Income above it is taxed in bands, and each rate applies only to the slice of income inside its band, not to everything you earn. The 2026/27 tax year runs from 6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027.

BandRateIncome (with the full personal allowance)
Personal allowance0%up to £12,570
Basic rate20%£12,571 to £50,270
Higher rate40%£50,271 to £125,140
Additional rate45%over £125,140

So on £50,000 you pay nothing on the first £12,570 and 20% on the remaining £37,430: £7,486 of income tax. Moving into the higher rate band doesn't change the tax on the income below it: only the pounds above £50,270 are taxed at 40%.

The personal allowance taper and the 60% band

Once your income goes over £100,000, the personal allowance shrinks by £1 for every £2 above it, so it's gone completely at £125,140. Each extra £100 in that range costs 40% tax on the £100 itself and another 40% on the £50 of allowance it takes away: an effective 60% income tax rate.

Add National Insurance and someone paid £110,000 keeps only about 38% of a pay rise: a marginal rate of 62%, against 42% at £60,000 and 28% at £30,000. Pension contributions bring the income used for the taper down, which is why paying more into a pension is popular in this band; the pay rise calculator shows what a rise is really worth.

National Insurance

Employees also pay Class 1 National Insurance on their pay: 8% between £12,570 and £50,270 a year, and 2% above that. It's worked out on pay before pension contributions (unless they're made by salary sacrifice), and per pay period rather than over the year. On £50,000 it comes to £2,994.

If you're self-employed you pay Class 4 National Insurance on your profits through Self Assessment instead: the self-employed tax calculator works it out, and the payments on account calculator shows when it's due.

Scotland

If you live in Scotland, income tax on your wages follows the Scottish bands instead (National Insurance and the personal allowance are the same across the UK). There are six rates:

BandRateIncome (with the full personal allowance)
Starter rate19%£12,571 to £16,537
Basic rate20%£16,538 to £29,526
Intermediate rate21%£29,527 to £43,662
Higher rate42%£43,663 to £75,000
Advanced rate45%£75,001 to £125,140
Top rate48%over £125,140

On £50,000 in Scotland the income tax comes to £8,982, against £7,486 in the rest of the UK. The Scottish salary calculator works out take-home pay with these bands.

Other things that change your tax

Pension contributions made by salary sacrifice or a net pay arrangement come off your pay before tax (salary sacrifice saves National Insurance too: see the salary sacrifice calculator); with relief at source, the pension provider adds the basic rate and higher rate taxpayers claim the rest back (the pension tax relief calculator works it out). Student loans take 9% of earnings above your plan's threshold (the student loan calculator shows how long repayments last). Dividends have their own rates and allowance: see the dividend tax calculator.

If you or your partner get Child Benefit and either of you earns over £60,000, the High Income Child Benefit Charge takes some or all of it back.

Frequently asked questions

What are the UK income tax bands for 2026/27?

The personal allowance is £12,570. Then 20% basic rate up to £50,270, 40% higher rate up to £125,140 and 45% additional rate above that, for England, Wales and Northern Ireland. Scotland has its own six bands.

Do I pay the higher rate on all my income?

No. Each rate applies only to the income inside its band. On £60,000 you pay 40% only on the £9,730 above £50,270.

What is the 60% tax trap?

Between £100,000 and £125,140, the personal allowance is withdrawn by £1 for every £2 of income, so each extra pound is effectively taxed at 60%, or about 62% with National Insurance.

How much tax do I pay on £50,000?

£7,486 income tax and £2,994 National Insurance in 2026/27, leaving £39,520 a year or £3,293 a month (standard tax code, no pension or student loan).

How much National Insurance do employees pay?

8% of pay between £12,570 and £50,270 a year, and 2% of pay above £50,270.
