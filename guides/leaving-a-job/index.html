Leaving a job: notice, holiday pay and redundancy

What you're owed and what to check when you leave, from GOV.UK's rules.

Notice

If you resign, you must give at least a week's notice once you've been in the job for more than a month, or more if your contract says so. If your employer ends your job, they must give at least the notice in your contract or the statutory minimum, whichever is longer: a week from one month's service, then a week for each complete year from 2 years, up to 12 weeks (5 weeks after 5 years). Notice usually runs from the start of the day after it's given. The notice period calculator gives your last day.

Pay during your notice

You'll usually get your normal pay rate during your notice, and bonuses and commission unless your contract says otherwise. Your employer can ask you not to come in (garden leave) on the same pay and benefits, or pay you in lieu of notice if your contract allows it or you agree; tax and National Insurance come off that payment.

Holiday you haven't taken

During your notice you may be able to take whatever's left of your statutory holiday. Leaving is the only time you can be paid instead of taking statutory leave, and your employer must pay for statutory leave you haven't taken, even if you're dismissed for gross misconduct. If you've taken more than you'd built up, your employer can only take money from your final pay if you agreed to that in writing beforehand. The holiday entitlement calculator works out what you've built up.

Redundancy pay

With at least 2 years' service, statutory redundancy pay is half a week's pay for each full year you were under 22, a week's pay for each full year from 22 to 40, and a week and a half for each full year from 41. Weekly pay is capped at £751 and service at 20 years, so the most is £22,530 (for redundancies from 6 April 2026). The redundancy pay calculator works it out.

Tax after you leave

If you've paid too much or too little tax by the end of the tax year (5 April), HMRC sends a tax calculation letter (P800) or a Simple Assessment letter between June and the end of November. If you're on an emergency tax code, tax is worked out as if you're paid that amount every week or month of the year, and if you've paid too much you can get a refund. The tax refund calculator estimates what you might be owed.

Frequently asked questions

How much notice do I have to give?

At least a week once you've been in the job for more than a month, or more if your contract says so.

How much notice does my employer have to give?

At least your contract's notice or the statutory minimum, whichever is longer: a week from one month's service, then a week per complete year from 2 years, up to 12 weeks.

Do I get paid for holiday I haven't taken when I leave?

Yes. Your employer must pay for statutory leave you haven't taken, even if you're dismissed for gross misconduct. You may also be able to take it during your notice.

How much is statutory redundancy pay?

It depends on your age and years of service, with weekly pay capped at £751 and service at 20 years: at most £22,530. You need at least 2 years' service.

Is pay in lieu of notice taxed?

Yes: tax and National Insurance are deducted from it.
