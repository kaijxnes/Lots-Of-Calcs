Your first job: tax, pay and pension

What comes off your pay and what you're entitled to, with 2026/27 figures.

Your tax code

Your employer uses a tax code to work out how much tax to take. Most people with one job get 1257L: the L means "You're entitled to the standard tax-free Personal Allowance." That's £12,570 you can earn tax-free in the tax year (2026/27). If your employer doesn't have your details yet you might see 0T ("Your Personal Allowance has been used up, or you've started a new job and your employer does not have the details they need to give you a tax code.") or an emergency code ending in W1, M1 or X. Giving your employer a P45 from a previous job helps; the tax code checker explains any code.

Tax and National Insurance on your pay

Income Tax is 20% of what you earn over £12,570, and employee National Insurance comes off too. Here's what that means on a few starting salaries in 2026/27, with tax code 1257L and nothing else taken off:

SalaryIncome Tax a yearNational Insurance a yearTake-home a month
£18,000£1,086£434£1,373.30
£22,000£1,886£754£1,613.30
£25,000£2,486£994£1,793.30
£30,000£3,486£1,394£2,093.30

Tax is usually worked out on your pay so far in the tax year, so your first payslip can look different from later ones, especially if you start part-way through the year. The salary calculator works out any salary and the payslip checker compares a payslip with what it should be.

Workplace pension

Your employer must put you into a workplace pension if you're aged between 22 and State Pension age and earn at least £10,000 a year. The minimum is 5% from you and 3% from your employer, and in most schemes it's worked out on earnings between £6,240 and £50,270. On £25,000 that's about £938 a year from you and £563 from your employer. The pension tax relief calculator shows the tax relief on what you pay in.

The minimum wage

From April 2026: £12.71 an hour if you're 21 or over (the National Living Wage), £10.85 at 18 to 20, £8.00 under 18, and £8.00 for apprentices under 19 or in the first year of their apprenticeship. The minimum wage checker checks your pay against it.

Holiday

Almost all workers get at least 5.6 weeks' paid holiday a year, at most 28 days, and your employer can count bank holidays towards it. Part-time workers get the same number of weeks, so fewer days. The holiday entitlement calculator works out yours.

Student loan repayments

If you have a student loan, repayments come out of your pay through PAYE once you earn over your plan's threshold: 9% of what you earn above it. The student loans guide explains the plans and the student loan calculator works out your repayments.

Frequently asked questions

How much tax will I pay in my first job?

With tax code 1257L in 2026/27 you pay no Income Tax on the first £12,570. On £25,000 that's £2,486 of Income Tax and £994 of National Insurance a year, leaving about £1,793.30 a month.

What tax code will I get in my first job?

Usually 1257L. Until your employer has your details you might be on 0T or an emergency code ending in W1, M1 or X.

Will I be put in a workplace pension?

Yes, if you're aged between 22 and State Pension age and earn at least £10,000 a year. The minimum is 5% from you and 3% from your employer.

What's the minimum wage for my age?

From April 2026: £12.71 an hour at 21 or over, £10.85 at 18 to 20 and £8.00 under 18.

How much holiday do I get?

At least 5.6 weeks a year, at most 28 days, which can include bank holidays.
