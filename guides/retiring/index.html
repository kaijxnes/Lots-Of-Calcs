Retiring: State Pension, pension tax and more

When you can get your State Pension, how much, and how to take your pension without paying more tax than you need to.

When you can get your State Pension

Your State Pension age depends on your date of birth. For people born after 5 April 1969 but before 6 April 1977, under the Pensions Act 2007, State Pension age was already 67. Under the Pensions Act 2007 the State Pension age for men and women will increase from 67 to 68 between 2044 and 2046. However the timetable for the increase in the State Pension age from 67 to 68 could change as a result of a future review. Before any future changes could become law Parliament would need to approve the plans. You will not get your new State Pension automatically - you have to claim it. The State Pension age calculator gives your exact date.

How much State Pension you'll get

The full new State Pension is £241.30 a week (£12,548 a year, 2026/27). You need 10 qualifying years of National Insurance to get any and 35 for the full amount if your record started after April 2016; each year in between is worth 1/35 of it, £6.89 a week. The new State Pension increases each year by whichever is the highest: earnings – the average percentage growth in wages (in Great Britain) prices – the percentage growth in prices in the UK measured by the Consumer Prices Index ( CPI ) 2.5%

If you have gaps, a year of voluntary Class 3 contributions costs £956.80 (£18.40 a week) and, if it adds a qualifying year that raises your pension, it's back in about 2.7 years. You can usually pay for the past 6 years. The State Pension forecast calculator works out yours.

Taking money from your pension

You can usually take up to 25% of the amount built up in any pension as a tax-free lump sum. The most you can take is £268,275. When you can take your pension depends on your pension scheme’s rules. It’s usually 55 at the earliest. The rest is taxed as income in the tax year you take it, so spreading it out can save tax: taking £40,000 from a pot you haven't touched, with no other income, costs £3,486.00 in tax in one tax year (2026/27) but £972.00 over two.

A first payment is usually taxed on an emergency code on a month 1 basis, so far more can come off at first: £11,878.88 on that £40,000. You can claim it back from HMRC (forms P55, P53Z or P50Z). The pension withdrawal tax calculator works it out, and the pension drawdown calculator shows how long a pot lasts.

Tax in retirement

You’ll usually pay tax on income from your pensions. This includes your State Pension. When you get your State Pension, it is not taxed before you receive it. You may have to pay tax on it depending on your total income for the tax year. If you’re working or getting a private pension, you’ll usually pay more tax to include any tax you need to pay on your State Pension. Your tax code will change to collect this. With the full State Pension, £10,000 from a private pension pot you haven't touched costs £1,495.52 in tax (2026/27). If you keep working after State Pension age, you’ll stop paying National Insurance.

Winter Fuel Payment

For winter 2026 to 2027, you can get a Winter Fuel Payment if you were born on or before 27 June 1960 and live in England, Wales or Northern Ireland: £200 living alone, or £300 if you were born before 28 September 1946 (different amounts if you live with someone else who gets it, or in a care home). If your own income is over £35,000, HMRC takes it back. The Winter Fuel Payment calculator checks yours.

Frequently asked questions

How much is the full State Pension?

£241.30 a week (£12,548 a year) in 2026/27, with 35 qualifying years of National Insurance.

How many years do I need for a State Pension?

10 qualifying years to get any new State Pension and 35 for the full amount if your record started after April 2016. Each year is worth £6.89 a week.

How much of my pension can I take tax-free?

Usually 25%, up to £268,275 in all. The rest is taxed as income.

Is the State Pension taxed?

It's taxable income but paid without tax taken off. If you also have a private pension or wages, your tax code usually collects the tax on your State Pension.

Do I still pay National Insurance after State Pension age?

No: if you keep working after State Pension age you stop paying National Insurance.
