Universal Credit explained

What you can get, how earnings and savings change it, with 2026/27 amounts.

What Universal Credit is

Universal Credit is a monthly payment for people on a low income or out of work. It's made up of a standard allowance plus extra amounts for children, disability, caring and housing, then reduced as you earn more. There’s no limit to how many hours you can work and still get Universal Credit.

Single, under 25£338.58
Single, 25 or over£424.90
Couple, both under 25£528.34 (for you both)
Couple, either 25 or over£666.97 (for you both)

Extra amounts

Children: £303.94 a month for each child (£351.88 for a first child born before 6 April 2017). The 2-child limit ended on 6 April 2026. A disabled child adds £164.79 or £514.71.

Health: £217.26 a month if you have limited capability for work and work-related activity (£429.80 for the most severe conditions). Caring: £209.34 a month if you care for a severely disabled person for at least 35 hours a week. Childcare: up to 85% of your costs if you work, up to £1,071.09 a month for one child and £1,836.16 for two or more.

Working and Universal Credit

For every £1 you earn from working, your Universal Credit payment goes down by 55p. Your income will be your wages plus your new Universal Credit payment. If you're responsible for a child, or have limited capability for work, you also get a work allowance: you can earn £710 a month before it starts to reduce, or £427 if you get help with housing costs. It's based on your take-home pay, after tax, National Insurance and pension contributions.

For example, a single parent aged 25 or over with one child and no help with housing gets up to £728.84 a month. Taking home £1,200 a month, £269.50 comes off (55% of the £490 over the work allowance), leaving £459.34 of Universal Credit, so £1,659.34 a month in all. Savings

You can't get Universal Credit with more than £16,000 in money, savings and investments. Between £6,000 and £16,000, your payment is reduced by £4.35 for every £250 (or part of £250) above £6,000: £69.60 a month with £10,000.

Other benefits and the benefit cap

Other benefits such as Carer's Allowance reduce Universal Credit by the same amount: Carer's Allowance is £86.45 a week. The benefit cap limits the total of most benefits to £1,835.00 a month for a couple or single parent outside Greater London (£2,110.25 in London) and £1,229.42 for a single adult (£1,413.92 in London). It doesn't apply if you and your partner earn £881 or more a month between you after tax and National Insurance.

Frequently asked questions

How much is Universal Credit?

The standard allowance is £424.90 a month for a single person aged 25 or over and £666.97 for a couple where either is 25 or over (2026/27), plus extra for children, disability, caring, childcare and housing.

How much can I earn on Universal Credit?

There's no limit on hours. For every £1 you take home, Universal Credit goes down by 55p, after a work allowance of £710 a month (£427 with housing help) if you're responsible for a child or have limited capability for work.

How much is Universal Credit for a child?

£303.94 a month for each child, for every child since the 2-child limit ended on 6 April 2026.

Can I have savings on Universal Credit?

Up to £16,000. Above £6,000, your payment goes down by £4.35 a month for every £250.

Does Carer's Allowance affect Universal Credit?

Yes: Universal Credit is reduced by the same amount as your Carer's Allowance (£86.45 a week), though you may get the carer element of £209.34 a month.
