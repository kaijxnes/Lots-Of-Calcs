National Insurance explained

What it pays for, what you pay in 2026/27 as an employee, an employer or self-employed, and how it builds your State Pension.

What National Insurance is for

National Insurance is a tax on earnings that also builds your right to the State Pension and some benefits. Each tax year you pay it (or get credits for, for example while unemployed, ill, or as a parent or carer) counts as a qualifying year. You need 10 qualifying years for any new State Pension and 35 for the full rate, currently £241.30 a week. Gaps can sometimes be filled with voluntary contributions.

Employees: Class 1

If you're employed you pay 8% on earnings between £12,570 and £50,270 a year, and 2% on everything above £50,270. It's worked out on each payday's earnings, not over the year as Income Tax is, and on gross pay: pension contributions under net pay or relief at source don't reduce it, but salary sacrifice does. You stop paying it once you reach State Pension age.

Employers' National Insurance

Employers pay 15% on each employee's earnings above £5,000 a year, on top of the salary, with no upper limit. Many can take up to £10,500 a year off the bill through the Employment Allowance (not companies whose only paid employee is the director). It's why salary sacrifice saves employers money too, and why company directors often pay themselves a small salary plus dividends: see salary vs dividends.

Self-employed: Class 2 and Class 4

On profits, the self-employed pay Class 4: 6% between £12,570 and £50,270 and 2% above, through Self Assessment. Class 2 is no longer charged: with profits of £7,105 or more it's treated as paid, so the year still counts for your State Pension, and below that you can pay it voluntarily at £3.65 a week to keep the year. The self-employed tax calculator works out both with Income Tax.

Checking your record

Your National Insurance record and State Pension forecast are in your personal tax account and the HMRC app. They show which years count and whether filling a gap with voluntary Class 3 contributions would raise your State Pension.

Frequently asked questions

How much National Insurance do I pay on £30,000?

£1,394 a year in 2026/27 if you're employed: 8% of the £17,430 above £12,570. Self-employed, Class 4 on £30,000 of profit is £1,046.

What is the National Insurance rate?

8% on employees' earnings between £12,570 and £50,270 a year and 2% above that; 6% and 2% for the self-employed on profits; 15% for employers above £5,000.

How many years of National Insurance do I need for a full State Pension?

35 qualifying years for the full new State Pension (£241.30 a week), and at least 10 to get any. Credits count, as do voluntary contributions.

Do I pay National Insurance after State Pension age?

No: employees stop paying Class 1 National Insurance once they reach State Pension age. Income Tax carries on as normal.

Does a pension contribution reduce National Insurance?

Only through salary sacrifice, which lowers your pay before National Insurance is worked out. Contributions taken under net pay or paid through relief at source come off after National Insurance.
