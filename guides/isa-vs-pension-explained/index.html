ISA vs pension explained

How each is taxed, the 2026/27 allowances, when you can get at your money, and which comes out ahead.

ISAs: tax-free, any time

You can put up to £20,000 a year (2026/27) into ISAs, split across cash, stocks and shares, innovative finance and Lifetime ISAs as you like (at most £4,000 into a Lifetime ISA). There's no tax relief going in, but interest, dividends and gains are tax-free and you can take money out whenever you like.

Pensions: relief now, tax later

Money paid into a pension gets tax relief: with relief at source you pay 80p and the provider claims 20p from the government, and higher and additional rate taxpayers claim more back. You can get relief on up to 100% of your earnings (£3,600 gross if you have none), within the £60,000 annual allowance. Workplace pensions usually add an employer contribution too, which an ISA can't match.

The catch is access: you usually can't take money out until 55 at the earliest. Then up to 25% is tax-free (at most £268,275 over your lifetime) and the rest is taxed as income. From April 2027 most unused pension pots will also count towards your estate for Inheritance Tax.

Which leaves more?

The same £100 of pre-tax income, put in either. Into a pension it all goes in; into an ISA you pay tax first. Taking the pension out, 25% is tax-free and the rest is taxed at your rate in retirement. (Growth is the same in both, so it doesn't change which wins.)

Because of the 25% tax-free part, a pension usually comes out ahead unless you'd pay more tax in retirement than now, and the gap is widest for higher-rate taxpayers and with an employer contribution. An ISA wins on flexibility: it's yours to spend at any age. Try your own numbers in the ISA vs pension calculator, and see the relief on a contribution with the pension tax relief calculator.

Frequently asked questions

Is a pension better than an ISA?

For retirement saving, usually yes for the same cost: tax relief going in and 25% tax-free coming out beat an ISA unless you'll pay a higher rate of tax in retirement than now. An ISA is better for money you might need before 55.

How much can I put in an ISA in 2026/27?

£20,000 in total across all your ISAs, of which at most £4,000 can go into a Lifetime ISA.

How much can I pay into a pension?

Up to 100% of your earnings with tax relief, within the £60,000 annual allowance (lower for very high earners). With no earnings you can still pay in £2,880, which becomes £3,600 with relief.

How much of my pension can I take tax-free?

Usually 25%, up to £268,275 in total across your pensions. The rest is taxed as income when you take it.

When can I take money out of my pension?

Usually from 55 at the earliest, depending on your scheme's rules. ISAs can be taken out at any time (a Lifetime ISA has its own rules and a charge for early withdrawal).
