Student loans explained

Which plan you're on, what you repay in 2026/27, how the interest works and when the rest is written off.

Which plan you're on

Which plan you have depends on where you lived and when your course started. You repay 9% of your income above your plan's threshold (6% above £21,000 for a Postgraduate Loan, on top of any undergraduate plan), and whatever is left is written off after a set number of years.

What you repay

Employees repay through PAYE, so it comes off each payslip alongside tax. Repayments depend only on what you earn, not on how much you owe: someone on £40,000 with Plan 2 repays £955 a year whether they owe £20,000 or £60,000.

Interest and write-off

Interest is added to the balance, not taken from your pay. Plan 2 charges 4.1% on incomes up to £29,385, rising to 6% at £52,885 and above. Because repayments are a share of income above a threshold, many Plan 2 and Plan 5 borrowers never clear the balance and have the rest written off after 30 or 40 years: for them it works more like a 9% tax on earnings above the threshold than a normal loan.

That's why paying extra off early only helps if you'd otherwise clear the loan before write-off: usually higher earners or smaller balances. The student loan calculator projects your balance year by year so you can see which applies to you.

Frequently asked questions

How much is the Plan 2 student loan threshold?

£29,385 a year in 2026/27. You repay 9% of your income above it: £55 a year on £30,000.

How much will I repay on £40,000?

Plan 1 £1,179, Plan 2 £955, Plan 4 £558, Plan 5 £1,350 a year in 2026/27.

When is a student loan written off?

Plan 1 after 25 years; Plan 2 after 30 years; Plan 4 after 30 years; Plan 5 after 40 years; Postgraduate Loan after 30 years, counted from the April after you were first due to repay.

Should I pay off my student loan early?

Only if you'd clear it before it's written off anyway: otherwise extra payments reduce a balance that would have been written off. It depends on your plan, balance and expected earnings, which the student loan calculator can project.
