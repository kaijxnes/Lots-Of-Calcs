UK tax changes from April 2027

What's different in the 2027/28 tax year, which starts on 6 April 2027, from the Budget on 28 October 2026 — with what it means in pounds.

Savings and property income: a new 24% basic rate

From 6 April 2027 savings income (interest) and property income (rent) have their own rates, each 4 points higher than the rates on wages: 24% basic, 44% higher and 49% additional. The Personal Savings Allowance (£1,000 for basic-rate taxpayers, £500 at the higher rate) and the £5,000 starting rate for savings are unchanged, and interest in an ISA stays tax-free.

Pensioners: the new Pension Tax Allowance

The full new State Pension rises to £250.70 a week (£13,036.40 a year), which is more than the £12,570 Personal Allowance. So that pensioners whose only income is the State Pension don't get small tax bills, a Pension Tax Allowance applies from 2027/28 to 2029/30: it takes 20% of the gap between the full new State Pension and the Personal Allowance off the tax bill, in full for income up to £13,037, withdrawn by £1 for every £3 above that and gone at £13,319.

Also from 6 April 2027, most unused pension pots and pension death benefits count towards the value of an estate for Inheritance Tax.

Side income and the self-employed

The trading allowance triples from £1,000 to £3,000: if your gross income from selling, freelancing or a side business is £3,000 or less, you don't need to declare it or pay tax on it. Someone with £2,500 of side income and no claimed expenses paid tax on £1,500 at 20% (£300) in 2026/27; in 2027/28 they pay £0.

The Class 2 Small Profits Threshold rises to £7,355 and voluntary Class 2 contributions to £3.80 a week; Class 4 stays at 6% and 2%. Anyone joining Making Tax Digital for Income Tax in April 2027 won't get late-submission penalty points on quarterly updates for 2027/28. Estimate the bill with the self-employed tax calculator.

Selling a business or shares

The lifetime limit for Business Asset Disposal Relief halves from £1 million to £500,000 for disposals from 6 April 2027 (the rate stays 18%). A new Founders' relief taxes qualifying gains of founders and early investors in high-growth companies at 10%, up to £10 million over a lifetime. Capital Gains Tax rates (18% and 24%) and the £3,000 annual exempt amount don't change; see the capital gains tax calculator.

Pay, benefits and employers

The National Living Wage rises to £13.21 an hour from 1 April 2027 (18 to 20: £11.27; under 18 and apprentices: £8.31). Child Benefit goes up to £28.00 a week for the eldest child and £18.55 for others. Employers must put company cars, vans, fuel and medical benefits through payroll from 6 April 2027, so tax on them is taken from pay as it goes instead of through a P11D. The approved mileage rate above 10,000 business miles rises from 25p to 35p.

Student loan thresholds for 2027/28: Plan 2 stays at £29,385, Plan 5 rises to £26,025 and the postgraduate (Plan 3) threshold stays at £21,000. See what you'll repay with the student loan calculator.

Bills and later changes

The 0% VAT rate on household electricity and the 5p fuel duty cut both run until 30 June 2027; fuel duty then returns to its pre-2022 level and rises with inflation from 1 July 2027. Energy-saving materials go back to 5% VAT from 1 April 2027, and car tax (VED) rises with RPI inflation from the same date. Further ahead: pay-per-mile Electric Vehicle Excise Duty (3p a mile for electric cars) and the High Value Council Tax Surcharge on homes worth £2 million or more start in April 2028, and the cash ISA limit stays at £20,000.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Personal Allowance changing in April 2027?

No. It stays at £12,570 in 2027/28, with the basic rate band at £37,700 and the additional rate from £125,140. All three are frozen in 2027/28.

What is the new tax rate on savings interest from April 2027?

24% for basic-rate taxpayers, 44% for higher-rate and 49% for additional-rate, on interest above the Personal Savings Allowance (£1,000, or £500 for higher-rate taxpayers). ISAs stay tax-free.

How much is the State Pension from April 2027?

The full new State Pension is £250.70 a week (£13,036.40 a year), up from £241.30. The basic (old) State Pension is £192.10 a week.

Will pensioners pay tax on the State Pension in 2027/28?

Not if the State Pension is their only income: the new Pension Tax Allowance removes the tax on income up to £13,037, tapering away to £13,319.

What is the trading allowance from April 2027?

£3,000, up from £1,000. If your total side income is no more than that you don't need to report it to HMRC.

What is the minimum wage from April 2027?

£13.21 an hour for workers aged 21 and over, £11.27 for 18 to 20 year olds, and £8.31 for under-18s and apprentices, from 1 April 2027.
