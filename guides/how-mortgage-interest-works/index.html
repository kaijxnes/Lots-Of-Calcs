How mortgage interest works

How a lender turns a loan, a rate and a term into your monthly payment, worked through for a £200,000 mortgage.

How the monthly payment is worked out

A repayment mortgage is set up so that the same payment every month clears the loan and all its interest by the end of the term. With P the amount borrowed, r the monthly interest rate (the yearly rate ÷ 12) and n the number of monthly payments, the payment is:

payment = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)−n)

For £200,000 over 25 years at 4.5%: r = 4.5% ÷ 12 = 0.375% a month, n = 300 payments, and the payment comes to £1,111.66 a month. Over the whole term you pay back £333,499: the £200,000 you borrowed and £133,499 of interest. The mortgage calculator does this sum for any loan, with a chart of the balance.

Why early payments are mostly interest

Each month, interest is charged on the balance still owed: in the first month that's £200,000 × 0.375% = £750.00. The rest of the payment, £361.66, pays off the loan. As the balance falls, so does the interest, and more of each payment goes on the loan itself. On this mortgage, the yearly repayment of the loan doesn't overtake the interest until year 11.

Fixed rates only last for the deal period (often 2 or 5 years); after that the payment is recalculated at the new rate on the balance left. Rerun the numbers at a higher rate before you commit; the mortgage affordability calculator shows how much lenders might let you borrow.

Interest-only mortgages

With an interest-only mortgage you pay just the interest each month, £200,000 × 4.5% ÷ 12 = £750.00 here, and still owe the whole £200,000 at the end of the term, which you need a plan to repay. Over 25 years the interest comes to £225,000, against £133,499 on a repayment mortgage, because the balance never goes down.

Overpaying

An overpayment goes straight off the balance, so every later month's interest is worked out on a smaller amount. Paying an extra £100 a month on this mortgage clears it in 21 years and 6 months instead of 25 years, 3 years and 6 months early, and saves about £21,142 of interest. Many fixed-rate deals let you overpay up to 10% of the balance a year without an early repayment charge; check yours. The mortgage overpayment calculator works out the saving for your loan.

Some lenders work interest out daily rather than monthly; the difference is small, and these figures use monthly interest, as most mortgage illustrations do. Buying? The stamp duty calculator covers the tax on the purchase, and rent vs buy compares the two.

Frequently asked questions

How is mortgage interest calculated?

On the balance still owed: each month the lender charges the yearly rate ÷ 12 on what you owe. On £200,000 at 4.5% that's £750.00 in the first month, falling as the loan is paid off.

What is the monthly payment on a £200,000 mortgage?

£1,111.66 a month over 25 years at 4.5%, with £133,499 of interest over the term. At 4% it's £1,055.67; at 5%, £1,169.18.

Why do I pay so much interest at the start of a mortgage?

Because the interest is charged on the balance, which is at its highest at the start. As you pay the loan down, less of each payment goes on interest and more on the loan.

Is it worth overpaying my mortgage?

It saves interest at your mortgage rate: £100 a month extra on £200,000 at 4.5% saves about £21,142 and ends the mortgage 3 years and 6 months early. Check your deal's overpayment limit, and weigh it against savings rates and any more expensive debt.

How much more does an interest-only mortgage cost?

On £200,000 at 4.5% over 25 years, about £225,000 of interest against £133,499 on a repayment mortgage, and you still owe the £200,000 at the end.
