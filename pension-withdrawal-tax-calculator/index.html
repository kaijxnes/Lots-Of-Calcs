How taking money from a pension is taxed

Tax-free part: you can usually take up to 25% of a pension tax-free, up to £268,275 in all (the lump sum allowance). If you take a lump sum straight from a pot you haven't touched (an uncrystallised funds pension lump sum), 25% of each payment is tax-free. If you took your tax-free cash first, later withdrawals are all taxable.

Taxable part: it's added to your other income for the tax year (6 April to 5 April), including the State Pension, and taxed at your usual rates, so a large withdrawal can push some of it into the 40% or 45% band and above £100,000 reduce your Personal Allowance. Spreading withdrawals over more than one tax year can mean less tax. You can usually take money out from 55, depending on your scheme's rules.

Emergency tax: a first, one-off payment is usually taxed on an emergency tax code on a month 1 basis: as if you were paid that much every month, so only a twelfth of the Personal Allowance and of each band applies, and too much tax comes off. You can claim it back from HMRC: form P55 if you took part of your pension, P53Z if you took all of it, or P50Z if you took all of it and have stopped work (or online).

Paying in afterwards: taking cash this way can lower your annual allowance for future pension saving (the money purchase annual allowance), so check before you take it if you're still paying in.

Frequently asked questions

How much tax do I pay when I take money out of my pension?

Usually 25% is tax-free and the rest is added to your income for the tax year and taxed at your normal rates. Taking £40,000 from an untouched pot with no other income: £10,000.00 tax-free and £3,486.00 of tax (2026/27).

Why was so much emergency tax taken from my pension?

A first payment is usually taxed on an emergency code on a month 1 basis, as if you were paid that amount every month. On £40,000 that takes off about £11,878.88, £8,392.88 more than the year's tax with no other income.

How do I claim back emergency tax on a pension?

Online, or by post with form P55 if you took part of your pension, P53Z if you took all of it, or P50Z if you took all of it and stopped work.

How much can I take from my pension tax-free?

Usually 25%, up to the lump sum allowance of £268,275 across all your pensions, unless you have a protected allowance.

Can I avoid paying higher rate tax on a pension withdrawal?

Spreading withdrawals over more than one tax year can keep more of each one in your Personal Allowance and the 20% band. £40,000 over 2 tax years with no other income costs £972.00 instead of £3,486.00.
