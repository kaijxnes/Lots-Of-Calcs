Frequently asked questions

How much tax will I pay on a £60,000 pension withdrawal?

From a pot you haven't touched, £15,000.00 is tax-free and £45,000.00 taxable. With no other income that's £6,486.00 of tax for 2026/27 (10.8%), leaving £53,514.00. With the full State Pension as well, it's £10,451.04.

How much emergency tax is taken off £60,000?

About £18,628.88 if it's a first payment taxed on an emergency code on a month 1 basis, so you'd receive £41,371.12. With no other income, £12,142.88 of that is too much and can be claimed back from HMRC.

How do I get emergency tax back on a pension withdrawal?

Claim it from HMRC online or by post: form P55 if you've taken part of your pension, P53Z if you've taken all of it, or P50Z if you've taken all of it and stopped work.

Is it better to take £60,000 over more than one tax year?

Often. With no other income, taking it over 2 tax years costs £3,972.00 in tax instead of £6,486.00, because each year gets its own Personal Allowance and basic rate band.

What if I've already taken my tax-free cash?

Then all of it is taxable: £11,432.00 of tax on £60,000 with no other income.
