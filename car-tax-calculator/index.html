Cars registered from 1 April 2017

The first payment, when the car is registered, depends on its CO2 emissions and covers 12 months. After that it's the standard rate of £200 a year for petrol, diesel, electric and alternative fuel cars (hybrids, bioethanol and LPG). If the list price was over £40,000 (£50,000 for an electric car), you pay an extra £440 a year, £640 in all, for 5 years from the second time it's taxed; a zero emission car registered before 1 April 2025 doesn't.

Frequently asked questions

How much is car tax a year?

For cars registered from 1 April 2017, the standard rate is £200 a year from 1 April 2026, for petrol, diesel, electric and alternative fuel cars alike. Older cars pay by CO2 band (£20 to £790) or engine size.

Do electric cars pay car tax?

Yes. An electric car registered now pays £10 for the first year, then the standard £200. The extra £440 applies if its list price was over £50,000, but not to a zero emission car registered before 1 April 2025.

What is the expensive car tax?

An extra £440 a year on top of the standard rate, for 5 years from the second time the car is taxed, if its list price was over £40,000 (£50,000 for an electric car). The list price is the published price before any discounts.

How much is the first year's car tax?

It depends on CO2: from £10 at 0g/km to £5,690 over 255g/km. Diesels that don't meet RDE2 pay more. A 120g/km petrol car pays £455.

Is it cheaper to pay car tax monthly?

No, slightly dearer: £200 in one go or £210 in total by monthly Direct Debit, at the standard rate.
