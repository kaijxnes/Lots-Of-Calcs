How it works

In week 1 you save the starting amount, in week 2 twice it, and so on up to 52 times it in week 52. Add those up and you get 1,378 × the starting amount: £1,378 from £1, £2,756 from £2, £689 from 50p.

The catch is the end of the year. Starting on 1 January, the classic version asks for £202 in December, just when Christmas costs land. The reverse version front-loads it, so December needs only £10; it's the same £1,378 either way.

The 365-day version goes up a little every day instead: 1p on day 1, 2p on day 2 and £3.65 on day 365, for £667.95. Smaller steps, but you have to remember daily.

Making it stick: set up a weekly standing order into a separate savings account, print the chart for the fridge, or tick off each week here and the page remembers your progress. If you'd rather save the same amount every week, the savings goal calculator works out what you need, and the compound interest calculator shows what it grows to.

Frequently asked questions

How much do you save with the 52-week challenge?

£1,378 if you start at £1 and add £1 each week: £1 + £2 + … + £52. Starting at £2 doubles it to £2,756, and £5 a week rising gives £6,890. The total is always the starting amount × 1,378.

What is the reverse 52-week challenge?

The same deposits in the opposite order: £52 in week 1 down to £1 in week 52. You still save £1,378, but the big amounts come in January rather than December, when money is tightest — December needs £10 instead of £202.

How much is the 1p a day challenge?

£667.95. You save 1p on day 1, 2p on day 2 and so on to £3.65 on day 365. The 2p version saves £1,335.90.

What is the 100 envelope challenge?

100 envelopes numbered 1 to 100. Each day you pick one and put in that many pounds, so after 100 days you've saved £5,050. The calculator shuffles the order for you and dates each envelope; the 50p version saves £2,525.

How do I save £1,000 with the 52-week challenge?

Start at 73p and add 73p each week: 73p × 1,378 = £1,005.94. Put a target into the calculator's 'Aim for a total' box and it works out the amount for any total.

Where should I keep the money?

Somewhere separate that you won't dip into but can reach if you need to, such as an easy-access savings account or a cash ISA. The interest is a bonus; add the rate to see roughly what it adds.

What if I miss a week?

Catch up the next week or spread it over the following few. The chart keeps a running total, so you can always see where you should be.
