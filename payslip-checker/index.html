Why your payslip might be different

Income Tax: GOV.UK: "Usually, your tax is worked out based on your total income so far in that tax year. If you’re on an emergency tax code your tax is worked out based on what you’re paid in that week or month only. You get taxed as if you’re paid that amount every week or month of the year." So the first payday in a new job, a month with a bonus or overtime, or a changed tax code can take more or less tax than a normal month. If your tax code looks wrong, the tax code checker explains it.

National Insurance is worked out on each payday's pay on its own, not the year so far: for 2026/27, £242 to £967 a week (£1,048 to £4,189 a month) 8%. Over £967 a week (£4,189 a month) 2%. So a bigger payday pays more, and it isn't evened out later.

Student loans are also worked out on each payday's pay, for example: Employee earnings threshold for student loan plan 2: £29,385 per year, £2,448.75 per month, £565.09 per week. Student loan deductions 9%.

Frequently asked questions

How do I check my payslip is right?

Enter your gross pay, pay period and tax code (all on your payslip), plus any pension and student loan, and the checker works out the tax, National Insurance and net pay you should see. Then enter your payslip's figures to compare them line by line.

How much tax should I pay on £2,500.00 a month?

On tax code 1257L in 2026/27, about £290.50 Income Tax and £116.20 National Insurance, leaving £2,093.30, if you're paid the same every month and have no pension or student loan.

Why is my tax different this month?

Tax is usually worked out on your pay so far in the tax year, so a bonus, overtime, a pay rise, starting a new job or a new tax code can change one month's tax. On an emergency code it's worked out on that payday's pay only.

Why is my National Insurance higher this month?

National Insurance is worked out on each payday's pay on its own, so a payday with a bonus or overtime pays more, and it isn't evened out over the year.

What does M1 or W1 on my tax code mean?

It's an emergency tax code: tax is worked out on what you're paid in that week or month only, as if you were paid that amount every week or month of the year.
