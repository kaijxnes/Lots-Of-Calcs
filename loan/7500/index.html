How the repayment is worked out

Each month the lender charges interest on what you still owe, and your fixed payment covers that interest plus part of the loan, so the balance falls faster towards the end. The payment is P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1), with P the amount borrowed, r the APR divided by 12 and n the number of months.

Frequently asked questions

What are the monthly repayments on a £7,500 loan?

At 7% APR over 3 years, £231.58 a month, £8,336.82 in total. At 20% APR the same loan costs £278.73 a month. The table on this page covers every common rate and term.

How much is a £7,500 loan over 3, 5 or 10 years?

At 7% APR: £231.58 a month over 3 years (£837 interest); £148.51 a month over 5 years (£1,411 interest); £87.08 a month over 10 years (£2,950 interest). A longer term lowers the payment but adds interest, so check which terms your lender offers.

How much interest will I pay on £7,500?

At 7% over 3 years, £836.82. Stretching it to 5 years cuts the monthly payment to £148.51 but raises the interest to £1,410.54.

How much is a £7,500 car loan a month?

A car loan from a bank works like any personal loan: at 7% APR, £179.60 a month over 4 years or £148.51 over 5. Dealer finance with a balloon payment at the end (PCP, or HP with a final payment) has lower monthly payments but a large sum to pay or the car to hand back at the end — the car finance calculator works those out.

Is a longer loan term better?

It makes each payment smaller but costs more overall, because you pay interest for longer. Choose the shortest term whose payment you can comfortably afford — and check whether the lender charges for paying off early.

What APR will I get on a £7,500 loan?

It depends on your credit history and the lender. The advertised representative APR only has to be offered to at least 51% of successful applicants, so others can be offered more. Loans of a few thousand pounds often carry higher rates than loans of £7,500 to £25,000.

How are these repayments worked out?

With the standard repayment formula, charging the APR divided by twelve each month on what is still owed. A lender's quote can differ by a little, since APR also includes any fees and lenders round differently.

Should I borrow £7,500 or use savings?

If you have savings earning less than the loan's APR, using them usually costs less overall. Keep an emergency fund first, though — a loan can be cheaper than having nothing to fall back on.
