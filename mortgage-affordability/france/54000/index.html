Frequently asked questions

How much can I borrow on €54,000 in France?

About €249,014 over 25 years at an example 3.5%. Banks must keep repayments — including interest and borrower insurance — to at most 35% of your net income before income tax (the HCSF's binding rule since 2022): €42,741 a year, so €1,247 a month.

What is the 35% rule (taux d'endettement)?

Since 1 January 2022 the Haut Conseil de stabilité financière has required lenders to keep a borrower's 'taux d'effort' — all loan repayments, interest and insurance included — to 35% of net income before tax, and loans to 25 years (27 if the start of repayments is deferred, for example on a new build). Banks can depart from it on 20% of their new lending, mostly for main homes and first-time buyers.

Does a shorter loan change it?

Yes — the same €1,247 a month repays less over a shorter term: about €214,950 over 20 years. The 35% also includes any other loans you're repaying, which reduce what's left for the mortgage.

What about the deposit (apport)?

Banks usually expect a deposit that covers at least the purchase costs, such as the notary's fees (frais de notaire). With a 10% deposit, €249,014 would buy a home of about €276,682.
