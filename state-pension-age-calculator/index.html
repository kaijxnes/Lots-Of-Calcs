The rise from 66 to 67

The Pensions Act 2014 brought the rise to 67 forward to 2026–2028. People born between 6 April 1960 and 5 March 1961 reach State Pension age at 66 and a set number of months rather than on a fixed date. If that would be a day the month doesn't have, it's the last day of the month: born 31 July 1960, you reach 66 years and 4 months on 30 November 2026.

The rise from 67 to 68

Under the Pensions Act 2007, State Pension age rises from 67 to 68 between 2044 and 2046, for people born from 6 April 1977, and is 68 for anyone born on or after 6 April 1978. That part of the timetable could change after a future review: the government has said the next review will reconsider the rise to 68, and that it's committed to giving 10 years' notice of changes. Any change would need Parliament's approval.

How much State Pension you get

The full new State Pension is £241.30 a week (£12,547.60 a year). You need 10 qualifying years on your National Insurance record to get any, and if your record started after April 2016, 35 qualifying years for the full amount. It goes up each year by the highest of earnings growth, CPI inflation or 2.5%. Your State Pension forecast on GOV.UK shows what you're on track for. It doesn't come automatically: you have to claim it. For the rest of your retirement income, try the pension drawdown calculator.

Frequently asked questions

What is the State Pension age now?

66 for people born from 6 October 1954 to 5 April 1960. It's rising to 67 between 2026 and 2028 for people born from 6 April 1960, and is 67 for people born from 6 March 1961 to 5 April 1977.

When does the State Pension age go up to 67?

Between 2026 and 2028. People born between 6 April 1960 and 5 March 1961 reach it at 66 and a number of months, from 66 years and 1 month to 66 years and 11 months; anyone born from 6 March 1961 reaches it at 67.

When does the State Pension age go up to 68?

Under the Pensions Act 2007, between 2044 and 2046, for people born from 6 April 1977; it's 68 for anyone born on or after 6 April 1978. That timetable could change after a future review, which will reconsider the rise to 68.

What if the date doesn't exist in that month?

You reach State Pension age on the last day of the month. The timetable's example: born 31 July 1960, you reach 66 years and 4 months on 30 November 2026.

How much is the State Pension?

The full new State Pension is £241.30 a week. You need at least 10 qualifying years of National Insurance to get any, and 35 for the full amount if your record started after April 2016.
