How much State Pension you get

The full new State Pension is £241.30 a week (£12,547.60 a year). You need 10 qualifying years on your National Insurance record to get any, and if your record started after April 2016, 35 qualifying years for the full amount. It goes up each year by the highest of earnings growth, CPI inflation or 2.5%. Your State Pension forecast on GOV.UK shows what you're on track for. It doesn't come automatically: you have to claim it. For the rest of your retirement income, try the pension drawdown calculator.

Frequently asked questions

What is my State Pension age if I was born in 1994?

68. You reach State Pension age on your 68th birthday, in 2062.

When can I get my State Pension if I was born in 1994?

On your 68th birthday, in 2062, under current law. You need to claim it; you can get it from your State Pension age, not before.

Could my State Pension age change?

The rise from 67 to 68, set for people born from 6 April 1977, could change after a future review. The government has said it's committed to giving 10 years' notice of changes, and Parliament would need to approve them.

How much is the full State Pension?

£241.30 a week. You need 35 qualifying years on your National Insurance record for the full new State Pension (if your record started after April 2016), and at least 10 for any.
