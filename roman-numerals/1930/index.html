Frequently asked questions

What is 1930 in Roman numerals?

MCMXXX. It breaks down as M (1,000) + CM (900) + X (10) + X (10) + X (10) = 1930.

How do you read MCMXXX?

Read left to right, adding each symbol — except where a smaller symbol sits before a larger one, which means subtract it: CM is 1,000 − 100 = 900.

Can 1930 be written another way?

MCMXXX is the standard form. Clocks often show 4 as IIII rather than IV, and older inscriptions vary, but modern usage — film credits, document numbering, monarchs and years — uses the subtractive form shown here.

How do you write a date in 1930 in Roman numerals?

Write each part separately: the day and month as numerals and the year as MCMXXX. For example, 14 March 1930 is XIV.III.MCMXXX written day first, or III.XIV.MCMXXX month first as in the US.
