Frequently asked questions

What is 1492 in Roman numerals?

MCDXCII. It breaks down as M (1,000) + CD (400) + XC (90) + I (1) + I (1) = 1492.

How do you read MCDXCII?

Read left to right, adding each symbol — except where a smaller symbol sits before a larger one, which means subtract it: CD is 500 − 100 = 400; XC is 100 − 10 = 90.

Can 1492 be written another way?

MCDXCII is the standard form. Clocks often show 4 as IIII rather than IV, and older inscriptions vary, but modern usage — film credits, document numbering, monarchs and years — uses the subtractive form shown here.

How do you write a date in 1492 in Roman numerals?

Write each part separately: the day and month as numerals and the year as MCDXCII. For example, 14 March 1492 is XIV.III.MCDXCII written day first, or III.XIV.MCDXCII month first as in the US.
