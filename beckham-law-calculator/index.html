How the Beckham law works

Spain's special regime for workers moving there (régimen especial para trabajadores desplazados, article 93 of the income tax law — nicknamed after David Beckham, one of the first to use it) taxes your Spanish employment income at a flat 24% up to €600,000 and 47% above, instead of the normal progressive IRPF scales.

It's on gross pay. The 24% is charged on your whole salary: there's no personal allowance, no reduction for employment income and no deduction for social security, and the regional scales don't apply (the tax is split half to the state and half to your region on the return, but the rate is the same everywhere). That's why it only pays off above about €64,000 in Comunidad de Madrid — below that, normal income tax with its allowances comes out lower.

Who can use it. You mustn't have been resident in Spain in the previous 5 tax years, and you must be moving for a job with a Spanish employer (or one that posts you there, or remote work for a foreign company under Spain's international teleworking (digital nomad) visa), to become a company director, or for some entrepreneurial and highly qualified roles. You opt in on form 149 within six months of the start date on your Spanish social security registration.

How long. The year you become resident and the following five — up to six tax years. You still pay Spanish social security as normal, unless you stay in your home country's system on a posting certificate. Savings income such as dividends, interest and gains is taxed separately, at 19% to 30%.

Sources: BOE — Ley 35/2006 del IRPF, artículo 93 (régimen especial para trabajadores desplazados); Agencia Tributaria — Modelo 151, instrucciones de cumplimentación (ejercicio 2023 y siguientes); BOE — Reglamento del IRPF (RD 439/2007), artículos 113–120.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Beckham law?

Spain's special tax regime for people who move there to work. Instead of normal income tax, whose top rate is well over 40% in every region, your employment income is taxed at a flat 24% up to €600,000 a year and 47% above, for the year you arrive and the next five.

Is the Beckham law worth it?

On a high salary, yes: on €100,000 it saves €6,747 a year in Comunidad de Madrid. On a lower salary, no — the 24% is on your gross pay with no allowances, so below about €64,000 you'd pay more than under normal income tax.

Is the Beckham law tax on gross salary?

Yes. The flat rate applies to your full employment income. The allowances and reductions in normal income tax — the personal minimum, the reduction for employment income, social security contributions — don't come off first.

How long does the Beckham law last?

For the tax year in which you become resident in Spain and the five following tax years — up to six years in all. You must not have been resident in Spain in the five years before you moved.

How do I apply for the Beckham law?

File form 149 with the Agencia Tributaria within six months of the start date on your Spanish social security registration. Each year you then file form 151 instead of the normal income tax return.

Does the Beckham law depend on the region?

No. The flat rate is the same everywhere in Spain (half is credited to the region on the return). Normal income tax does depend on the region, so whether the regime saves you money does too — pick yours above.
