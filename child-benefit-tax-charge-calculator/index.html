How the charge works

If you or your partner get Child Benefit and either of you has an adjusted net income over £60,000, the one with the higher income pays some of it back through the High Income Child Benefit Charge: 1% of the Child Benefit for every £200 of income over £60,000. At £80,000 or more, the charge is the whole of it.

Child Benefit is £27.05 a week for the eldest or only child and £17.90 a week for each other child: about £1,407 a year for one child, £2,337 for two and £3,268 for three.

Adjusted net income is your taxable income, including savings interest and dividends, less personal pension contributions and Gift Aid donations (both gross). Paying more into a pension is the usual way to bring it back under £60,000: with an income of £70,000, an extra £10,000 gross into a pension clears the charge as well as saving higher rate tax. To see what that does to your take-home pay, use the salary calculator, or compare the options with ISA or pension.

The charge is paid through Self Assessment, or, if you register for PAYE by 31 January after the tax year ends, through your tax code. If your income is well over £80,000 you can opt out of the payments instead: you still claim Child Benefit, which keeps your National Insurance credits towards your State Pension.

Frequently asked questions

What is the High Income Child Benefit Charge?

A tax charge that takes back some or all of your family's Child Benefit if you or your partner has an adjusted net income over £60,000. It's paid by whichever of you has the higher income.

How much Child Benefit do I pay back?

1% of it for every £200 of adjusted net income over £60,000, and all of it at £80,000 or more. With two children and an income of £70,000, that's 50%: £1,168 of £2,337.

What is the threshold for the Child Benefit charge?

£60,000 of adjusted net income for each parent, since the 2024 to 2025 tax year. It's paid back in full at £80,000.

Does my partner's income count?

Only to decide who pays. Each income is tested on its own, not added together: if both of you are over £60,000, the one with the higher income pays the charge.

How can I avoid the High Income Child Benefit Charge?

Bring your adjusted net income down to £60,000 or less, usually by paying more into a pension or giving to charity with Gift Aid. Both are taken off your income at their gross amount.

Should I still claim Child Benefit if I earn over £80,000?

It's worth claiming and opting out of the payments: you won't owe the charge, but the claim still gets you National Insurance credits towards your State Pension if you're not working, and gets your child a National Insurance number.
