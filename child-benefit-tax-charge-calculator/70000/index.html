£70,000 is £10,000 over the £60,000 threshold, so the charge is 50% of your Child Benefit (1% for every £200 over). It's paid by whichever parent has the higher adjusted net income, through Self Assessment or your tax code.

To keep it all, bring your adjusted net income down to £60,000 with £10,000 of personal pension contributions or Gift Aid, both counted gross. Into a relief at source pension that's £8,000 paid in, as the provider adds basic rate relief, and as a higher rate taxpayer you can claim more relief back on your tax return. Frequently asked questions

How much Child Benefit do I pay back on £70,000?

50% of it: £703 a year for one child, £1,168 for two and £1,634 for three. It's 1% for every £200 of adjusted net income over £60,000.

How do I avoid the Child Benefit charge on £70,000?

Bring your adjusted net income down to £60,000: £10,000 of personal pension contributions or Gift Aid donations, counted gross. Into a relief at source pension that's £8,000 paid in, as the provider adds basic rate relief.

Does my partner's income count?

Only to decide who pays. Each income is tested on its own, not added together: if both of you are over £60,000, the one with the higher income pays the charge.
