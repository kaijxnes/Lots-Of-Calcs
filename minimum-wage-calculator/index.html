Minimum wage rates

The minimum hourly rate depends on your age and whether you're an apprentice, and the rates change on 1 April every year. From 21 you're entitled to the National Living Wage, £12.71 an hour from April 2026, rising to £13.21 from April 2027 (up 3.9%). Workers under 21, down to school leaving age, get the National Minimum Wage for their age.

Apprentices

You get the apprentice rate (£8.00 an hour from April 2026) if you're under 19, or 19 or over and in the first year of your apprenticeship. Once you're 19 or over and past the first year, you're entitled to the minimum wage for your age: GOV.UK's example is a 21-year-old apprentice who has finished their first year getting £12.71.

Frequently asked questions

What is the minimum wage in April 2026?

£12.71 an hour if you're 21 or over (the National Living Wage), £10.85 at 18 to 20, £8.00 under 18 and £8.00 for apprentices.

What will the minimum wage be from April 2027?

£13.21 an hour for 21 and over, £11.27 for 18 to 20, £8.31 for under 18s and £8.31 for apprentices.

How much is the National Living Wage a year?

£24,784.50 a year on 37.5 hours a week, or £26,436.80 on 40 hours, before tax, at £12.71 an hour.

When does the minimum wage go up?

On 1 April every year.

What is the apprentice minimum wage?

£8.00 an hour from April 2026, for apprentices under 19 or in the first year of their apprenticeship. Apprentices aged 19 or over who have finished their first year get the minimum wage for their age.

What age do you get the National Living Wage?

From 21. Workers aged 20 and under get the National Minimum Wage for their age group.
