How pension tax relief works

Money you pay into a pension gets tax relief at your highest rate of income tax, on contributions up to 100% of your earnings for the year (or £3,600 gross, if that's more). How you get it depends on the scheme:

Relief at source (personal pensions, SIPPs and many workplace schemes): you pay in from your take-home pay and the provider adds basic rate relief, so £80 becomes £100. If you pay 40% or 45% tax, you claim the rest through Self Assessment: an extra 20% on the slice of income taxed at 40% and 25% on any taxed at 45%.

Net pay (many workplace schemes): the contribution comes out of your pay before income tax, so you get all the relief straight away. On £60,000, £5,000 through net pay saves £2,000 of tax.

On £60,000, paying £4,000 into a relief at source pension puts £5,000 in: the provider adds £1,000, and you claim £1,000 back, so it costs you £3,000. With £4,000 paid in at each income (2026/27, England, Wales and Northern Ireland):

Limits

The annual allowance is £60,000 a year in total, including your employer's contributions; above it you may owe tax, though you can carry forward unused allowance from the previous three years. It's lower if you've flexibly taken money from a pension, or with threshold income over £200,000 and adjusted income over £260,000.

If your employer offers salary sacrifice, it saves National Insurance as well as tax. To weigh a pension against an ISA, see ISA or pension; for taking money out, the pension drawdown calculator.

Frequently asked questions

How much tax relief do I get on pension contributions?

Relief at your highest rate of income tax. Relief at source pensions add 20% (so £80 becomes £100), and higher and additional rate taxpayers claim the rest through Self Assessment. On £60,000, £4,000 paid in gets £2,000 of relief.

How do I claim higher rate pension tax relief?

Through your Self Assessment tax return, or if you don't file one, by contacting HMRC (who may adjust your tax code). It's only needed for relief at source pensions; net pay pensions give it through payroll.

How much extra can I claim as a higher rate taxpayer?

20% of the gross contribution, up to the amount of income you paid 40% tax on. Earning £60,270, you can claim the extra on up to £10,000 of contributions.

What if I don't pay income tax?

With a relief at source pension you still get 20% added, on up to £3,600 gross a year (£2,880 paid in). A net pay pension gives no relief if you don't pay income tax.

What is the pension annual allowance?

£60,000 a year across all your pensions, including employer contributions, with unused allowance carried forward from the previous three years. It's tapered for very high incomes.

Is pension tax relief different in Scotland?

Providers add 20%, even for starter rate taxpayers. Intermediate, higher, advanced and top rate taxpayers claim the difference between their rate and 20% through Self Assessment.
