You pay £2,000 from your take-home pay and the provider adds basic rate relief, so £2,500.00 goes into your pension. If you pay 40% or 45% tax, you claim the rest through Self Assessment: an extra 20% on the slice of income taxed at 40% and 25% on any taxed at 45%. Between £100,000 and £125,140 contributions also win back Personal Allowance: on £110,000, £2,000 costs you £1,000.00. With a net pay scheme the relief comes through payroll instead; the pension tax relief calculator works that out.

Frequently asked questions

How much tax relief do I get on £2,000 paid into a pension?

Your provider adds £500.00, making £2,500.00. A higher rate taxpayer on £60,000 claims another £500.00 back, so it costs £1,500.00; on £150,000 you claim £625.00 (2026/27, England, Wales and Northern Ireland).

How do I claim higher rate relief on £2,000?

Through your Self Assessment tax return, or if you don't file one, by contacting HMRC (who may adjust your tax code). It's only needed for relief at source pensions; net pay pensions give it through payroll.

Can I pay this much in?

You get relief on contributions up to 100% of your earnings for the year, or £3,600 gross (£2,880 paid in) if that's more, and the annual allowance is £60,000 including your employer's contributions.
