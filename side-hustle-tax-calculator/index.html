The £1,000 trading and property allowances

You can earn up to £1,000 a year tax-free from trading (self-employment, casual work such as babysitting or gardening, or hiring out personal equipment) and another £1,000 from property or land. Above that you can take off either the £1,000 allowance or your actual expenses, whichever is more, but not both. If you own a property jointly, you each get the property allowance against your share of the rent.

Telling HMRC: with gross trading income over £1,000, register for Self Assessment by 5 October after the end of the tax year. With property income over £1,000 up to £2,500, contact HMRC; over £2,500, register for Self Assessment. You can't use the allowances for income from your employer, from a company or partnership you or someone connected to you controls, or for a room let under the Rent a Room Scheme.

Frequently asked questions

How much can I earn from a side hustle before paying tax?

£1,000 a year of gross trading income is tax-free under the trading allowance, and another £1,000 from property. Above that, tax is on the income less the allowance or your expenses.

How much tax on £3,000 of side income?

With a £30,000 job and no expenses, £400.00 of Income Tax: 20% of the £2,000 left after the £1,000 allowance (2026/27). No Class 4 National Insurance until profits pass £12,570.

Do I need to tell HMRC about selling online?

If it's trading and your gross income is over £1,000 a year, you must register for Self Assessment by 5 October after the tax year. At £1,000 or less you don't need to tell HMRC.

Can I claim expenses and the trading allowance?

No: you take off one or the other. If your expenses are more than £1,000, claiming them is better.

Do I pay National Insurance on side income?

On trading profits, Class 4 National Insurance at 6% on profits over £12,570 and 2% over £50,270. Not on property income.
