How it's worked out

Without interest it's simply the goal divided by the months: £10,000 ÷ 13 = £769.23.

With interest, each deposit earns interest for the months left after it goes in, so you need to pay in less. The monthly amount is goal × i ÷ ((1 + i)n − 1), where i is the monthly rate (4% ÷ 12 = 0.333%) and n is 13 months: £753.97.

Frequently asked questions

How much do I need to save a month to have £10,000 by 2027?

£769.23 a month, starting in December 2026 and saving every month until December 2027 — 13 payments. That's about £177.51 a week.

How much less do I need to save with interest?

At 4% a year, compounded monthly, £753.97 a month gets you there, because the interest makes up £198 of the £10,000. At 2% it's £761.57 and at 5% £750.19.

What if I start later?

Every month you wait spreads the same total over fewer payments. Starting in June 2027 instead of December 2026, you'd need £1,428.57 a month without interest.

What if I already have some saved?

Take it off the goal first, then divide what's left by the months. With £2,500 already saved, you'd need £576.92 a month without interest. The savings goal calculator does this with interest on both.
