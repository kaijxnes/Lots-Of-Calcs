How it's worked out

Each payment on account is half of the income tax and Class 4 National Insurance on your last return, paid by 31 January and 31 July towards the next year's bill. The balancing payment is what's left of the year's bill once the payments on account you've made are taken off; it also takes in any capital gains tax and student loan repayments, which payments on account don't cover.

GOV.UK's example: a £3,000 bill for the year, with two payments on account of £900 already made, leaves a balancing payment of £1,200. On top comes the first payment on account for the next year, half of £3,000: £1,500. So £2,700 is due by 31 January and the second £1,500 by 31 July.

You don't make payments on account if your bill was under £1,000, or if more than 80% of your tax was paid outside Self Assessment, for example through your tax code. Frequently asked questions

What are payments on account?

Payments towards your next tax bill, including Class 4 National Insurance if you're self-employed, made in two instalments by 31 January and 31 July. Each is half of the tax you owed the year before.

How much is my payment on account?

Half of your Self Assessment bill for income tax and Class 4 National Insurance. A £3,000 bill for 2025/26 means two payments of £1,500 towards 2026/27, due by 31 January 2027 and 31 July 2027.

When don't I have to make payments on account?

If the tax you owed last year was less than £1,000, or if you paid more than 80% of the tax you owed outside Self Assessment, for example through your tax code or because your bank had already taken tax off your savings interest.

What is a balancing payment?

The tax you still owe for the year once your payments on account are taken off. It's due by 31 January after the tax year, and also includes any capital gains tax and student loan repayments, which payments on account don't cover.

Why is my January bill so big in my first year?

In your first year there are no payments on account to take off, so on 31 January you pay the whole year's bill and the first payment on account for the next year, half as much again. A £3,000 first-year bill means £4,500 by 31 January and £1,500 in July.

What if I earn less this year?

Payments on account are based on last year, so if you earn less, you may have paid too much and be due a refund once the year's return is in.
