How Marriage Allowance works

If you're married or in a civil partnership and one of you earns less than the £12,570 Personal Allowance, that person can transfer £1,260 of their allowance to the other. The other partner's tax falls by up to £252 a year (20% of £1,260). The partner receiving it must pay tax at the basic rate, which usually means an income between £12,571 and £50,270 (£12,571 to £43,662 in Scotland, where the starter, basic and intermediate rates count).

The lower earner's own allowance falls to £11,310, so if they earn more than that they pay a little tax themselves, but the couple still comes out ahead. In GOV.UK's example, a lower earner on £11,500 and a partner on £20,000 save £214 between them: £252 off the partner's tax, less £38 the lower earner now pays.

Backdating. You can backdate a claim to 6 April 2022 for any year you were both eligible. The Personal Allowance was £12,570 in each of those years, so each one is worth up to £252: up to £1,008 for 2022/23, 2023/24, 2024/25, 2025/26, paid as a lump sum, on top of this year's saving.

The lower earner applies, online or through Self Assessment, and the allowance carries on each year until you cancel it. Your tax codes change: the receiving partner's ends in M and the transferring partner's in N. If either of you was born before 6 April 1935, Married Couple's Allowance is usually worth more instead. Frequently asked questions

How much is Marriage Allowance worth?

Up to £252 a year: the lower earner transfers £1,260 of their Personal Allowance and the partner's tax falls by 20% of it. Backdated to 6 April 2022 it can add up to £1,008 more.

Who can claim Marriage Allowance?

Married couples and civil partners where one earns less than £12,570 and the other pays tax at the basic rate, usually an income between £12,571 and £50,270 (up to £43,662 in Scotland). Couples who live together but aren't married or in a civil partnership can't claim.

Can I get Marriage Allowance if my partner is a higher-rate taxpayer?

No. The partner receiving the allowance must not pay the higher rate, so with an income over £50,270 (£43,662 in Scotland) they can't receive it.

How far back can I claim Marriage Allowance?

To 6 April 2022, the 2022 to 2023 tax year, for any year you were both eligible. That's up to £252 for each of 2022/23, 2023/24, 2024/25, 2025/26.

What if the lower earner earns between £11,310 and £12,570?

Transferring £1,260 leaves them with a £11,310 allowance, so they pay 20% on income above that. The couple usually still saves: on £12,000 the lower earner pays £138.00 and the couple saves £114.00.

What do the M and N tax codes mean?

A tax code ending in M means you receive a transferred Marriage Allowance; one ending in N means you've transferred it to your partner. With the usual codes that's 1383M and 1131N.
