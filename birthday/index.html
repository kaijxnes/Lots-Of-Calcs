How your age is worked out

Your age is counted the way you'd count it on a calendar: complete years since your date of birth, then the whole months since your last birthday, then the days left over. Dividing your total days by 365 would drift because of leap years, so it isn't done that way.

Dates go in as day, month, year, in that order. The month is a dropdown of names rather than a number, so there is no chance of 03/04 being read as the 4th of March when you meant the 3rd of April.

Worked example: someone born on 14 May 1990 was born on a Monday. On 2 September 2026 they are 36 years, 3 months and 19 days old, having lived 13,260 days — and their 10,000th day was Friday 29 September 2017.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find out what day of the week I was born?

Enter your date of birth and the calculator works it out from the calendar, along with your exact age and how long until your next birthday.

How is my exact age calculated?

By counting complete years from your birth date, then the remaining whole months, then the leftover days — which is why it does not simply divide the total days by 365.

How many days old am I?

The “total days lived” figure counts every day from your birth date to today, leap days included. On your 30th birthday you have lived 10,957 or 10,958 days, depending on how many 29 Februarys fell in between.

When is my birthday if I was born on 29 February?

In leap years, on the 29th. In other years the calculator counts down to 1 March, the day after 28 February; many people born on a leap day choose to celebrate on 28 February instead.

How old will I be on a future date?

The birthday calculator works from today. For your age on any other date, put your date of birth and that date into the date difference calculator; the years figure is your age then.

Is my date of birth stored anywhere?

No. The calculation runs in your browser and nothing you type is sent anywhere.
