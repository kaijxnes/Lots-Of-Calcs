How much is enough?

Three months of essential spending is the common starting point, and covers most surprise bills and a short gap between jobs. Six months or more makes sense if you're self-employed, on a variable income, the only earner in your home, or in work that would take a while to replace.

Count essentials, not everything. In an emergency you'd stop eating out and pause subscriptions, so the fund only needs to cover what you couldn't cut. That makes the target smaller and quicker to reach than it first seems.

Frequently asked questions

How much should an emergency fund be?

Three to six months of essential spending is the usual guide: enough to cover rent or mortgage, bills, food, transport and minimum debt payments if your income stopped or a big bill arrived. Lean towards six months or more if you're self-employed, the only earner, or your income varies.

What counts as essential spending?

What you'd still have to pay if you cut back hard: housing, council tax, utilities, food, insurance, transport to work, childcare and minimum debt repayments. Leave out holidays, eating out and subscriptions you'd cancel.

Where should I keep my emergency fund?

Somewhere safe and easy to reach without penalty, such as an easy-access savings account or cash ISA, separate from your current account so it doesn't get spent. Not in investments that could fall just when you need the money.

Should I pay off debt or build an emergency fund first?

Many people build a small buffer first — even one month of essentials — so an unexpected bill doesn't go on a credit card, then tackle expensive debt, then finish the fund.

How long does it take to save £5,280?

22 months at £250 a month, or 11 months at £500. Add the interest rate on your savings account to see how much sooner it gets there.
