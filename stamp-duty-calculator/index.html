Frequently asked questions

How is stamp duty worked out?

Like income tax: each rate applies only to the part of the price within its band. On £295,000 in England that's nothing on the first £125,000, 2% on the next £125,000 (£2,500) and 5% on the last £45,000 (£2,250) — £4,750 in all, as GOV.UK's own example shows.

Do first-time buyers pay stamp duty?

In England and Northern Ireland, nothing up to £300,000 and 5% from £300,001 to £500,000; above £500,000 the relief doesn't apply at all. In Scotland first-time buyers pay nothing up to £175,000. Wales has no first-time buyer relief.

How much extra on a second home?

In England and Northern Ireland, 5% on top of every band (for properties of £40,000 or more). In Scotland, the Additional Dwelling Supplement is 8% of the whole price. Wales has a separate set of higher residential rates, from 5% up to 17%.

Is there stamp duty in Scotland and Wales?

Not stamp duty itself: Scotland charges Land and Buildings Transaction Tax (LBTT) and Wales Land Transaction Tax (LTT), each with its own bands, set out below.

Are these rates current?

They were last checked on 2026-11-16 against GOV.UK, Revenue Scotland and the Welsh Government, and are checked every month.
