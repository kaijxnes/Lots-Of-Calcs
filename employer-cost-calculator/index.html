What an employer pays on top of salary

Employer National Insurance is 15% of earnings above £5,000 a year (2026/27), with no upper limit. For employees under 21 and apprentices under 25 it's 0% up to £50,270, and 15% above. Benefits in kind such as a company car carry Class 1A National Insurance at 15% too.

Employment Allowance takes up to £10,500 a year off an eligible employer's National Insurance bill, across all its staff. It's not available to public bodies (unless they're a charity), or to a company whose only employee paying secondary Class 1 National Insurance is its sole director.

Pension: an employer must automatically enrol workers aged between 22 and State Pension age who earn at least £10,000 a year. The minimum is 3% from the employer and 5% from the employee (8% in total), and in most schemes it's worked out on earnings between £6,240 and £50,270. Scheme rules can set it higher. The salary sacrifice calculator shows how paying in by salary sacrifice changes the National Insurance both sides pay.

Apprenticeship Levy is 0.5% of the annual pay bill for employers whose pay bill is more than £3,000,000, less an allowance of £15,000 a year, so most small employers don't pay it.

Frequently asked questions

How much does an employee cost on top of their salary?

Employer National Insurance at 15% of pay above £5,000 a year, and at least 3% pension on earnings between £6,240 and £50,270 for workers who are auto-enrolled. On £30,000 that's £4,462.80, or 14.9% on top.

What is the employer National Insurance rate?

15% on earnings above the secondary threshold of £5,000 a year (2026/27). For under-21s and apprentices under 25 it's 0% up to £50,270.

What is Employment Allowance?

It reduces an eligible employer's annual National Insurance bill by up to £10,500. Companies whose only employee paying secondary Class 1 National Insurance is the sole director can't claim it.

What's the minimum employer pension contribution?

3% from the employer and 5% from the employee, usually on earnings between £6,240 and £50,270, for workers aged 22 to State Pension age earning at least £10,000.
