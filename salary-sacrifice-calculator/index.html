How salary sacrifice saves you money

With salary sacrifice you agree to a lower salary and your employer pays the difference into your pension. Because your pay is lower, you pay less income tax and less National Insurance, and if you have a student loan, smaller repayments. A pension paid from your pay before tax (a net pay arrangement) saves the income tax but not the National Insurance, so salary sacrifice usually leaves you better off by the National Insurance on the amount: 8% for most basic rate taxpayers, 2% on pay above £50,270.

On £50,000, sacrificing 5% (£2,500 a year) saves £500 of income tax and £200 of National Insurance, so £2,500 goes into your pension for £1,800 out of your take-home pay: 72p for each £1. Your employer saves 15% employer National Insurance too, £375 here; if they add it to your pension, £2,875 goes in for the same £1,800.

Between £100,000 and £125,140 the saving is biggest: sacrificing £10,000 on £110,000 gives back £6,000 of income tax, because it also restores personal allowance, so £10,000 goes into the pension for £3,800. Salary sacrifice also lowers the income used for the High Income Child Benefit Charge.

Student loans: repayments are worked out on your pay after the sacrifice, so on £40,000 with a Plan 2 loan, sacrificing £2,000 also cuts repayments by £180 a year. In Scotland the income tax saving follows the Scottish bands: £1,050 on the same £2,500 from £50,000.

Things to know

Minimum wage. A salary sacrifice can't take your cash pay below the National Minimum Wage, so your employer may cap how much you can give up.

Your lower salary counts elsewhere. Some things are worked out on your contractual salary, such as mortgage applications, life cover or statutory pay; ask your employer which salary they use.

From April 2029, only the first £2,000 a year of pension contributions through salary sacrifice will be free of National Insurance; anything above will have employee and employer National Insurance taken, like other workplace pension contributions. It will still be free of income tax, and employer contributions stay free of National Insurance. The figures above are for 2026/27.

Frequently asked questions

How much does salary sacrifice save?

The income tax and National Insurance on the amount, plus any student loan repayment. On £50,000, sacrificing £2,500 saves £500 tax and £200 National Insurance, so it costs £1,800 out of your take-home pay.

Is salary sacrifice better than a normal workplace pension?

Usually, by the National Insurance on the contribution: 8% for basic rate taxpayers and 2% for higher rate in 2026/27. A net pay pension gives the same income tax relief but not the National Insurance saving.

What is the salary sacrifice change in 2029?

From April 2029, only the first £2,000 a year of employee pension contributions through salary sacrifice will be free of National Insurance. The rest will still be free of income tax.

Does salary sacrifice reduce my student loan repayments?

Yes: repayments are worked out on your pay after the sacrifice. On £40,000 with a Plan 2 loan, sacrificing £2,000 cuts repayments by £180 a year.

Can salary sacrifice get me out of the 60% tax trap?

It brings your income down, so it can restore your personal allowance. Sacrificing £10,000 on £110,000 costs £3,800 in take-home pay for £10,000 in your pension.

Does my employer save money too?

Yes, 15% employer National Insurance on the amount sacrificed (on pay above £5,000 a year). Some employers add some or all of it to your pension.
