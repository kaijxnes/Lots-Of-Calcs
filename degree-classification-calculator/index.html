Frequently asked questions

How is a UK degree classification worked out?

Usually from a weighted average of your module marks in the years that count — often the second and final years, with the final year weighted more. The usual boundaries are 70% for a first, 60% for a 2:1, 50% for a 2:2 and 40% for a third, but each university sets its own weighting and rules.

What do I need in final year for a 2:1?

With second year counting a third and final year two-thirds (a 1:2 weighting), you need a final-year average of 1.5 × 60 − 0.5 × your second-year average. A 62% second year needs 59% in final year; a 55% second year needs 62.5%.

What weighting does my university use?

It varies: some weight the final year more heavily (1:2, 1:3, 40:60), some count only the final year, and some take the better of two methods. Check your programme handbook or degree regulations and enter the weighting here.

What happens if I'm on a borderline?

Many universities have rules for marks just below a boundary — for example, awarding the higher class if enough of your credits are in that class. They differ between universities, so check your regulations; this calculator uses the plain boundaries.
