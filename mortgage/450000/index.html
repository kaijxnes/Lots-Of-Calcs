Can you afford it?

Most UK lenders will lend up to around 4 to 4.5 times your annual income, sometimes more for higher earners. On that rule of thumb, borrowing £450,000 usually needs a household income of roughly £100,000 to £112,500 — and lenders also check your outgoings and how the payment would look if rates rose.

With a 10% deposit, £450,000 buys a home costing about £500,000; with 25% down, about £600,000. The salary after tax pages show what an income leaves you each month.

How the payment is worked out

A repayment mortgage charges interest each month on what you still owe, and the fixed monthly payment covers that interest plus a slice of the loan. Early on most of the payment is interest; by the end it is almost all capital. The payment is P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1), where P is the amount borrowed, r is the annual rate divided by 12 and n is the number of monthly payments.

A longer term lowers the monthly payment but means paying interest for longer, so the total cost rises: compare the 20- and 25-year columns in the interest table above.

Frequently asked questions

What is the monthly payment on a £450,000 mortgage?

At 4.5% over 25 years, £2,501.25 a month on a repayment basis. It ranges from £1,731.83 (3% over 35 years) to £4,171.56 (7.5% over 15 years); the table on this page has every combination in between.

How much interest will I pay on a £450,000 mortgage?

At 4.5% over 25 years, £300,373.85 in interest, on top of the £450,000 borrowed. Over 20 years at the same rate it would be £233,261.33 — £67,112.52 less, for £345.68 more a month.

How much does a 1% rate rise add to a £450,000 mortgage?

Going from 4.5% to 5.5% over 25 years adds about £262.15 a month, or £3,145.77 a year.

What income do I need for a £450,000 mortgage?

As a rule of thumb, lenders lend around 4 to 4.5 times income, so roughly £100,000 to £112,500 a year. Each lender sets its own limits and checks your spending too.

What would an interest-only £450,000 mortgage cost?

At 4.5%, £1,687.50 a month — just the interest, so after 25 years you still owe the full £450,000 and need a plan to repay it, such as savings, investments or selling the property.

Can I pay off a £450,000 mortgage faster?

Yes — overpaying each month or paying a lump sum cuts the interest and the term. The mortgage overpayment calculator shows how much you'd save; check first whether your deal charges for overpaying more than a set amount.
