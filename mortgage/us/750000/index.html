Can you afford it?

A common lender guideline keeps housing costs to about 28% of gross monthly income. Principal and interest of $4,740.51 at 6.5% over 30 years would need an income of about $203,165 a year — more once property tax, insurance and any HOA fees are added, which this payment doesn't include.

With 20% down, a $750,000 loan buys a home costing about $937,500; with 10% down, about $833,333, though under 20% down usually means paying mortgage insurance too.

How the payment is worked out

A repayment mortgage charges interest each month on what you still owe, and the fixed monthly payment covers that interest plus a slice of the loan. Early on most of the payment is interest; by the end it is almost all capital. The payment is P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1), where P is the amount borrowed, r is the annual rate divided by 12 and n is the number of monthly payments.

A longer term lowers the monthly payment but means paying interest for longer, so the total cost rises: compare the 20- and 30-year columns in the interest table above.

Frequently asked questions

What is the monthly payment on a $750,000 mortgage?

At 6.5% over 30 years, $4,740.51 a month on a repayment basis. It ranges from $3,580.61 (4% over 30 years) to $9,099.57 (8% over 10 years); the table on this page has every combination in between.

How much interest will I pay on a $750,000 mortgage?

At 6.5% over 30 years, $956,583.66 in interest, on top of the $750,000 borrowed. Over 20 years at the same rate it would be $592,031.64 — $364,552.02 less, for $851.29 more a month.

How much does a 1% rate rise add to a $750,000 mortgage?

Going from 6.5% to 7.5% over 30 years adds about $503.60 a month, or $6,043.18 a year.

What income do I need for a $750,000 mortgage?

Using the common guideline that housing costs stay under about 28% of gross income, around $203,165 a year at 6.5% over 30 years, before property tax and insurance. Lenders also look at your other debts.

Does this payment include taxes and insurance?

No. It is principal and interest only. A monthly mortgage bill usually also collects property tax and homeowners insurance into escrow, plus mortgage insurance if you put down less than 20%, so expect the total to be higher.

Can I pay off a $750,000 mortgage faster?

Yes — overpaying each month or paying a lump sum cuts the interest and the term. The mortgage overpayment calculator shows how much you'd save; check first whether your deal charges for overpaying more than a set amount.
