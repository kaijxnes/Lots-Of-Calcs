How qualifying years set your State Pension

The full new State Pension is £241.30 a week (2026/27). You need 10 qualifying years on your National Insurance record to get any, and 35 for the full rate if your record started after April 2016. In between, the Pensions Act 2014 gives 1/35 of the full rate for each qualifying year: £6.89 a week, or £358.50 a year.

Filling gaps: voluntary Class 3 contributions are £18.40 a week (2026/27), so a full year is £956.80. If it adds a qualifying year that raises your pension, that comes back in about 2.7 years of State Pension. You can usually only pay for the past 6 years, with a 5 April deadline each year. Voluntary contributions don't always increase your State Pension, for example if you were contracted out, so check your forecast or ask the Future Pension Centre before paying.

If your record started before April 2016, your amount could be different if you were contracted out before 2016 (you'll usually need more than 35 years for the full rate) or paid into the Additional State Pension. Your forecast is the best guide; this calculator shows the rule for records that started after April 2016. The State Pension rises each year by the highest of earnings growth, prices (CPI) or 2.5%, so these figures are in today's money.

Frequently asked questions

How much State Pension will I get?

£241.30 a week with 35 qualifying years (2026/27). With fewer, you get 1/35 of that for each year (£6.89 a week), as long as you have at least 10. Records from before April 2016 can differ; your GOV.UK forecast shows yours.

How many years of National Insurance do I need for a full State Pension?

35 qualifying years if your record started after April 2016. If you were contracted out before 2016, you'll usually need more than 35.

What's the minimum for any State Pension?

10 qualifying years on your National Insurance record. With fewer you get no new State Pension, unless years in some other countries count towards the 10.

How much does a year of voluntary National Insurance cost?

£18.40 a week for Class 3 in 2026/27, so £956.80 for a full year. A year that raises your pension adds £358.50 a year, so it pays back in about 2.7 years.

How far back can I pay voluntary National Insurance?

Usually the past 6 years, with a deadline of 5 April each year.
