How to add VAT

Multiply the net price by 1 plus the VAT rate as a decimal: gross = net × (1 + rate). At the UK standard rate of 20% that means multiplying by 1.2.

Example: a £150 invoice before VAT. 150 × 1.2 = £180 including VAT, of which £30 is VAT. At the 5% reduced rate it would be 150 × 1.05 = £157.50.

How to remove VAT

Divide the gross price by 1 plus the VAT rate: net = gross ÷ (1 + rate). At 20%, divide by 1.2.

Example: a £180 receipt that includes 20% VAT. 180 ÷ 1.2 = £150 before VAT, so the VAT is £30.

Taking 20% off £180 gives £144, which is wrong: the VAT was 20% of the smaller net price, not of the total. At 20%, the VAT inside any VAT-inclusive price is exactly one sixth of it (£180 ÷ 6 = £30). At 5% it is one twenty-first. HMRC calls this the VAT fraction.

The income tax bands tab

The second tab applies a set of progressive bands to an income: each rate charges only the slice of income inside its band. It starts with the UK's 2026/27 bands for England, Wales and Northern Ireland, and you can edit the thresholds and rates to model any scheme.

It is a band splitter, not a payslip: it doesn't taper the personal allowance above £100,000 or add National Insurance. For your actual take-home pay, use the salary after tax calculator.

Frequently asked questions

How do I remove VAT from a gross price?

Divide by 1 plus the VAT rate. At 20% VAT you divide the gross price by 1.2 to get the net price — subtracting 20% does not work, because the VAT was added to the smaller net figure.

How much VAT is in £100?

If £100 is the price before VAT, 20% VAT adds £20, making £120. If £100 already includes 20% VAT, the VAT within it is £16.67 (one sixth) and the price before VAT is £83.33.

What is the difference between zero-rated and exempt?

Both mean no VAT is charged to the customer, but a business selling zero-rated goods is still in the VAT system and can reclaim the VAT on its own costs. A business making only exempt supplies cannot register for VAT or reclaim the VAT it pays.

When does a business have to register for VAT?

In the UK, when its VAT-taxable turnover goes over £90,000 in any rolling 12 months, or is expected to within the next 30 days. Businesses below the threshold can register voluntarily. Check the current threshold on GOV.UK before relying on it.

Does this work for VAT rates in other countries?

Yes. Change the rate to the one you need and pick your currency; the add and remove formulas are the same everywhere. Sales tax in the US is usually quoted before tax, so use the net price box.

Does a higher tax band apply to my whole income?

No. In a progressive system each rate applies only to the slice of income inside that band, so moving into a higher band never reduces your take-home pay — only the income above the threshold is taxed at the higher rate.
