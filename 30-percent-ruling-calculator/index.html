How the 30% ruling works

If you're recruited from abroad for a job in the Netherlands, your employer can pay up to 30% of your salary as a tax-free allowance for the extra costs of living away from home (the expatregeling). The 30% is of your pay including the allowance: on €80,000, €24,000 is paid tax-free and income tax is worked out on the other €56,000.

The salary norm. What's left after the allowance must still be more than €48,013 a year in 2026 (€36,497 if you're under 30 and have a master's degree; there's no minimum for scientific researchers). Close to the norm the allowance is cut back to keep you above it — on €60,000 it's €11,987, not €18,000.

The cap. The allowance is only paid on salary up to €262,000 — the top-salary limit (WNT norm) — so the most it can be is €78,600 for a full year.

How long. Up to 5 years, less any time you've already lived or worked in the Netherlands. You must have lived more than 150 km from the Dutch border for more than 16 of the 24 months before you started, and your employer applies to the Belastingdienst for it.

What's changing. The maximum stays at 30% in 2025 and 2026 and drops to 27% from 2027, with a higher salary norm for anyone who started using the ruling from 2025. People who had it by the end of 2023 keep 30% and the current norm for their whole 5 years.

Sources: Rijksoverheid — Expatregeling hoogopgeleide buitenlandse werknemers; Belastingdienst — Kan ik de expatregeling aanvragen als ik in Nederland kom werken?.

Frequently asked questions

What is the 30% ruling?

A Dutch tax concession for employees recruited from abroad: the employer can pay up to 30% of salary as a tax-free allowance for 5 years, so income tax is only charged on the rest. It's officially called the expatregeling (30%-regeling).

How much is the 30% ruling worth?

On €80,000 a year it adds €12,199 to your take-home pay — €1,016.61 a month. Because the tax saved is at your top rate on the allowance, it's worth more the more you earn, up to the cap on €262,000 of salary.

What is the 30% ruling salary norm for 2026?

Your salary not counting the tax-free allowance must be more than €48,013 a year in 2026, or €36,497 if you're under 30 with a master's degree. Scientific researchers at designated institutions have no minimum.

Is the 30% ruling being reduced?

Yes, from 2027: the maximum falls from 30% to 27%, and a higher salary norm applies to people who started using it from 2025. It stays at 30% in 2025 and 2026, and people who had it by the end of 2023 keep 30% for their whole term.

Do I still get the tax credits with the 30% ruling?

Yes. The general tax credit and the labour tax credit are worked out on your taxable pay — the salary less the allowance — so on a high salary you can get slightly more of them than you would without the ruling.

Can I still choose partial non-resident status?

Not any more for new users: from the 2025 tax return the option of being treated as a partial non-resident for savings and investments (box 2 and box 3) was abolished. People who used the ruling before 2024 can keep it until their 2026 return.
