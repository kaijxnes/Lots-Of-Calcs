How the tip is worked out

The tip is the bill multiplied by the tip percentage and divided by 100; the total is the bill plus the tip. Split between several people, both are divided evenly.

Example: a £86 dinner for four with a 12.5% tip. The tip is 86 × 0.125 = £10.75, the total £96.75, and each person pays £24.19 (£24.1875, rounded). If people ordered very different amounts, the itemised bill splitter shares the tip by what each person spent.

Quick mental maths: 10% is the bill with the decimal point moved one place left. Add half of that again for 15%, or double it for 20%. On £86: 10% is £8.60, 15% is £12.90, 20% is £17.20.

Tipping in the UK and elsewhere

UK: 10–12.5% is usual in restaurants when service isn't included. Many restaurants add a discretionary service charge, often 12.5%, to the bill — if it's there, you don't need to tip on top, and because it is discretionary you can ask for it to be removed if the service was poor. Since October 2024 employers in Great Britain must pass all tips and service charges on to their staff in full.

US and Canada: tipping is expected and makes up a large part of servers' pay. 15–20% of the pre-tax bill is standard in restaurants, with 18–20% common in cities.

Much of Europe: service is usually included in the price, and rounding up or leaving 5–10% for good service is normal. Japan: tipping isn't customary and can cause confusion.

Frequently asked questions

How much should I tip?

It varies by country and setting. Around 15-20% is customary in US restaurants, while in the UK 10-12.5% is typical and service is often already added to the bill — worth checking before adding more.

Should I tip on the pre-tax or post-tax total?

Either is accepted, though tipping on the pre-tax subtotal is the more common convention. The difference on a normal bill is usually small.

Do I have to pay a service charge in the UK?

A discretionary or optional service charge can be removed if you weren't happy with the service — ask before paying. A compulsory service charge has to be made clear before you order, for example on the menu, and then forms part of the price.

How do I work out a 15% tip?

Find 10% by moving the decimal point one place left, then add half of it again. On a £64 bill, 10% is £6.40, half of that is £3.20, so 15% is £9.60.

How do I split the bill and tip between friends?

Set the number of people and the calculator divides the tip and the total evenly. If some people ordered much more than others, use the split bill calculator's itemised tab so each person's tip matches what they spent.

Do staff get the tips I leave?

In Great Britain, the Employment (Allocation of Tips) Act requires employers to pass on all tips, gratuities and service charges to workers without deductions, and to have a written policy on how they are shared. Cash tips given directly to a member of staff are theirs to keep or share as their workplace agrees.
