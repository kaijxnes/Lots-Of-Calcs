How the calculator works

A price index tracks what a fixed basket of everyday goods and services costs over time. Dividing the index at the later date by the index at the earlier one gives how much prices rose: an index of 72.7 in 2000 and 144.1 in September 2026 means prices are 1.98 times higher, so £100 then buys what costs £198.21 now.

The figure is an average across the whole basket. Some things have risen far faster than inflation — house prices, rent, university fees — and others, like electronics, have got cheaper. For your own savings, the compound interest calculator shows whether a rate of return beats inflation.

Frequently asked questions

What is £100 from 2000 worth today?

£198.21 in September 2026 money, using the Consumer Prices Index. Prices have risen 98.2% since 2000, an average of 2.6% a year.

What is £100 from 1970 worth today?

About £2,278, using the ONS long-run price index (the only official UK series that goes back before 1988). Prices have risen roughly 23-fold since 1970.

Should I use CPI, CPIH or RPI?

CPI is the official measure of UK inflation, the one the Bank of England targets and most people mean. CPIH adds housing costs for homeowners and council tax. RPI is an older measure that usually runs higher than CPI and is no longer a national statistic, but its long-run series is the only official one that goes back before 1988 — so use it for older years.

How is the value worked out?

The amount is multiplied by the price index for the later date and divided by the index for the earlier year. Years use the average index across that year; 'latest' uses the most recent month published. The result is what you'd need then and now to buy the same basket of goods and services.

What is the current rate of inflation?

UK CPI inflation was 3.4% in the 12 months to September 2026, and US CPI-U inflation 3.4% in the 12 months to August 2026, from the latest official figures.

What is $100 from 1990 worth today?

$256.30 in August 2026 dollars, using the US Bureau of Labor Statistics' CPI-U. (£100 from 1990 is £257.78 on UK CPI.)

How often is this updated?

Monthly, after the ONS and the US Bureau of Labor Statistics publish each month's figures. The date of the latest figures is shown next to the result.
