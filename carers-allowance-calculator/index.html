How earnings are counted

Carer's Allowance is £86.45 a week if you care for someone at least 35 hours a week and they get a qualifying disability benefit. You can work too, as long as your earnings are £204.00 or less a week after tax, National Insurance and expenses. The limit is a cliff edge: a penny over and you get no Carer's Allowance for that week.

From your pay, the Carer's Allowance Unit takes off Income Tax, National Insurance and half of your pension contributions. If you pay someone who isn't a close relative to look after the person you care for, or a child under 16, while you work, those costs come off too, up to half of your earnings after tax, NI and pension. Paying more into a pension or paying for care can bring you back under the limit.

Carer's Allowance is taxable if your income is over the Personal Allowance, you get National Insurance credits for each week you're paid it, and Universal Credit is reduced by the same amount. In Scotland you apply for Carer Support Payment instead.

Frequently asked questions

What's the Carer's Allowance earnings limit?

£204.00 a week after tax, National Insurance and expenses such as half of your pension contributions. That's about £884 a month before tax if you have no pension or care costs.

How many hours can I work and get Carer's Allowance?

It depends on your pay: at the £12.71 National Living Wage, about 16 hours a week stays within the limit. There's no limit on hours worked itself, but you must also care for at least 35 hours a week.

Do pension contributions count for Carer's Allowance?

Half of what you pay into a pension is taken off your earnings. Paying £9.23 a week into a pension takes £4.62 off your counted earnings.

What happens if I earn over the Carer's Allowance limit?

You don't get Carer's Allowance for any week your counted earnings are over £204.00, even by a penny. You must report changes to your income.

How much is Carer's Allowance?

£86.45 a week. It's taxable if your income is over the Personal Allowance, and you get National Insurance credits for each week you're paid it.
