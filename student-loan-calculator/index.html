Frequently asked questions

How much do I repay on my student loan?

9% of what you earn over your plan's threshold (6% for a Postgraduate Loan), taken through your pay. For 2026/27 the thresholds are Plan 1 £26,900, Plan 2 £29,385, Plan 4 £33,795, Plan 5 £25,000, Postgraduate Loan £21,000.

Will my student loan be written off?

Many will be. Plan 2 and Plan 4 loans are written off 30 years after the April you were first due to repay, Plan 5 after 40 years and Plan 1 after 25 (for loans from September 2006). On £45,000 of Plan 2 debt and a £30,000 salary rising 3% a year, the calculator shows about £41,863 repaid and £122,184 written off.

Is it worth overpaying my student loan?

Only if you'd clear it before it's written off anyway. If you won't, every extra pound is money you'd never have had to pay. Put an extra monthly amount into the calculator: it compares the total you'd repay with and without it.

What interest rate is charged?

Plan 1: 4.1%; Plan 2: 4.1% to 6% depending on income; Plan 4: 4.1%; Plan 5: 4.1%; Postgraduate Loan: 6% — as GOV.UK lists them now. Interest is added even while you're earning under the threshold.

Does the student loan affect my take-home pay?

Yes — it comes out of your pay alongside tax and National Insurance. The salary calculator shows take-home pay with your plan included.
