What it does

Beyond basic arithmetic, this calculator adds trigonometric functions (sin, cos, tan), logarithms (log base 10 and natural log), powers, square roots, parentheses for grouping, and the constants π and e.

Type the whole expression and press = to evaluate it. It follows the standard order of operations (BODMAS, or BIDMAS): brackets first, then powers and roots (orders), then division and multiplication from left to right, then addition and subtraction from left to right.

Degrees, radians and logs

Use the DEG/RAD toggle to switch how trigonometric functions interpret angles — degrees (0–360°) or radians (0–2π) — before calculating sin, cos or tan. In degrees, sin(30) = 0.5, cos(60) = 0.5 and tan(45) = 1. A full turn is 360° or 2π radians, so to convert degrees to radians multiply by π ÷ 180.

log is the logarithm to base 10: log(1000) = 3, because 103 = 1000. ln is the natural logarithm, to base e (about 2.71828): ln(e) = 1. Logs of zero or negative numbers aren't defined, so they show as an error.

Parentheses let you control the order of operations directly, e.g. 2×(3+4) or sin(30+15) — type the full expression and press = to evaluate.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between DEG and RAD?

It sets how the trigonometric functions read your input. In DEG mode a right angle is 90; in RAD mode the same angle is about 1.5708 (pi divided by 2). Switch mode before calculating sin, cos or tan, since the same number means a very different angle in each.

Why does sin(90) give 1 in one mode and a different number in the other?

Because 90 degrees is a quarter turn and gives exactly 1, while 90 radians is over fourteen full turns and lands somewhere else entirely. If a trig answer looks wrong, the mode toggle is almost always the reason.

Does this calculator use BODMAS?

Yes. Brackets come first, then powers and roots, then multiplication and division from left to right, then addition and subtraction from left to right. 2 + 3 × 4 gives 14, not 20.

Why is −3 squared shown as −9?

Written as −3^2, the power applies to the 3 before the minus sign does, so it means −(3 × 3) = −9. This matches the usual mathematical convention and most scientific calculators. To square negative three, type (−3)^2, which gives 9.

How do I calculate a cube root or other roots?

Raise the number to a fractional power: the cube root of 27 is 27^(1÷3) = 3, and the fourth root of 16 is 16^(1÷4) = 2. Put the fraction in brackets so it is worked out before the power.

Why do I sometimes see a tiny number instead of zero?

Computers store numbers like π to a fixed precision, so sin(180) in degrees can come out as a very small number rather than exactly 0. Results are rounded to 10 decimal places to hide most of these, but the occasional tiny remainder is a rounding artefact, not a mistake in your sum.
