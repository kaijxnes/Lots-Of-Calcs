Frequently asked questions

How much tax do I pay when self-employed?

Income tax at the same rates as everyone else (in England, Wales and Northern Ireland: 20% from £12,571, 40% from £50,271, 45% over £125,140, with the personal allowance tapering from £100,000), plus Class 4 National Insurance at 6% on profits from £12,570 to £50,270 and 2% above. Both are worked out on your profit — income minus allowable expenses.

When is the Self Assessment deadline?

For the 2026/27 tax year, the online return and the bill are due by 31 January 2028. Payments on account for the next year are due on 31 January and 31 July.

What are payments on account?

Payments on account are advance payments towards next year's bill: each is half of the tax and Class 4 National Insurance you owed for the year before, due on 31 January and 31 July. You don't make them if that bill was under £1,000, or if more than 80% of your tax was already taken at source (GOV.UK). In your first year of self-employment the first January payment is the whole bill plus the first payment on account for the next year — often a shock, so put money aside as you go.

How much should I put aside for tax?

Look at the effective rate for your profit in the table: at £30,000 it's about 15%, at £60,000 about 23%. Putting aside 25–30% of each payment you receive covers most people, plus the first payment on account.

Are these figures up to date?

They use the 2026/27 rates from the same data files as the salary calculators, which are checked against GOV.UK every month; the "last checked" date is on the tax rates page.
