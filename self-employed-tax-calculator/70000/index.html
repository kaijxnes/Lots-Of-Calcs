Paying the bill

The 2026/27 tax year ends on 5 April 2027; the return and the bill of £18,088.60 are due by 31 January 2028. As it's over £1,000 you'll also pay £9,044.30 on account on 31 January and again on 31 July, towards the following year. In your first year that makes £27,132.90 due on 31 January 2028.

Payments on account are advance payments towards next year's bill: each is half of the tax and Class 4 National Insurance you owed for the year before, due on 31 January and 31 July. You don't make them if that bill was under £1,000, or if more than 80% of your tax was already taken at source (GOV.UK). In your first year of self-employment the first January payment is the whole bill plus the first payment on account for the next year — often a shock, so put money aside as you go.

Frequently asked questions

How much tax do I pay on £70,000 self-employed?

£15,432.00 income tax and £2,656.60 Class 4 National Insurance on £70,000 of profit in England, Wales and Northern Ireland, £18,088.60 in all, leaving £51,911.40 (£4,325.95 a month). In Scotland, with its own income tax bands, you'd keep £49,961.35.

When do I pay it?

You don't make them if that bill was under £1,000, or if more than 80% of your tax was already taken at source (GOV.UK). Do I pay more or less than an employee?

Less National Insurance: Class 4 is 6% on profits from £12,570 to £50,270 and 2% above, against 8% and 2% for employees. On £70,000 an employee would keep £51,157.40, £754.00 less — but employees also get employer pension contributions, sick pay and holiday pay.

Do I pay Class 2 National Insurance?

Not on profits of £7,105 or more: since April 2024 it's treated as paid, so you still build up State Pension entitlement. Below that you can choose to pay voluntary Class 2 (£3.65 a week in 2026/27) to protect your record.
