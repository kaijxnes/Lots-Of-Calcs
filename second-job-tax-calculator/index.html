How tax works with a second job

You get one Personal Allowance (£12,570) however many jobs you have, and it usually goes to the job that pays the most. That job's code is usually 1257L; the second job gets a code with no allowance, so every pound of it is taxed. GOV.UK lists the usual second job codes as BR (all of it at 20%), D0 (40%) and D1 (45%), chosen by your total income from all your jobs. There's no extra tax for having two jobs: the code just makes sure the allowance isn't used twice.

When your main job pays less than the allowance

If your main job pays less than £12,570, the rest of the allowance can be moved to the second job: contact HMRC to ask. In GOV.UK's example, jobs paying £8,000 and £7,000 leave £4,570 of allowance for job 2, so only £2,430 of it is taxed: £486 for the year (codes of about 800L and 457L). Left on BR, job 2 would have £1,400.00 taken and you'd wait until after 5 April for £914.00 back. Each job's allowance is spread evenly over the year, so if your pay jumps around you can still pay some tax in a busy week or month.

National Insurance on two jobs

National Insurance is worked out on each job separately: each employer takes 8% of your pay over £242 a week from that job, and 2% over £967. With two smaller jobs you can pay less than with one job paying the same, because each has its own threshold; if your main job already pays over £967 a week, the second job's pay over £242 a week is charged 8% where one bigger job would pay 2% on it, so a larger second job can cost more. Jobs paying £60,000 and £30,000 take £4,605.00 in National Insurance, against £3,810.60 for one job paying £90,000. If you earn at least £967 a week from one job and £242 from the other, GOV.UK says you may be able to defer National Insurance on the second job and pay 2% on its earnings between £242 and £967 a week instead.

In Scotland

Scottish codes start with S, and the flat-rate codes follow Scotland's bands: SBR is 20%, SD0 the 21% intermediate rate, SD1 42%, SD2 45% and SD3 48%.

Starting a second job

You won't have a P45 because you're still in your first job, so fill in the new employer's starter checklist and say you already have another job; that gets you the right code from your first payday. Check both jobs and their codes in the HMRC app or your personal tax account, and if a code looks wrong the tax code checker explains it. HMRC checks your tax across all your jobs after 5 April and sends a P800 if you paid the wrong amount: the tax refund calculator estimates it.

Frequently asked questions

How much tax will I pay on a second job?

Usually 20% of all of it (code BR) if your total income is in the basic rate band, 40% (D0) in the higher rate band, or 45% (D1) above £125,140, because your Personal Allowance is used by your main job. With £28,000 and £8,000, the second job takes £1,600.00 in tax and £0.00 in National Insurance.

Why is my second job taxed so much?

Because your tax-free allowance is used by your main job, so the second job has none and every pound is taxed. If it's on an emergency code or the wrong flat-rate code it can take more than you owe; over the year you should pay the same tax as on one job paying both amounts.

Do I pay more tax with two jobs?

No. Income Tax depends on your total income, so two jobs paying £25,000 and £10,000 cost the same tax as one job paying £35,000. National Insurance is different: it's worked out per job, so two jobs can cost less (or, if your main job is over the upper earnings limit, more) than one.

What tax code should my second job have?

Usually BR, D0 or D1, depending on your total income from all your jobs (SBR, SD0 to SD3 in Scotland). If your main job pays less than the Personal Allowance, HMRC can split the allowance and give the second job a code ending in L.

Do I pay National Insurance on both jobs?

Yes, separately on each, if you earn over £242 a week from that job. If one job pays at least £967 a week you may be able to defer National Insurance on the other.

Can I split my Personal Allowance between two jobs?

If your main job pays less than £12,570, contact HMRC to check whether the rest can go to your second job. With jobs paying £8,000 and £7,000 that cuts the tax taken from the second job to £486 for the year.
