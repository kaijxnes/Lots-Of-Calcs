Why a day rate isn't rate × 260

There are about 260 weekdays in a year, but an employee is paid for 5.6 weeks' holiday (at most 28 days, which can include bank holidays) and gets at least 3% employer pension on earnings between £6,240 and £50,270 if they're auto-enrolled. A contractor is only paid for the days they bill, and usually has gaps between contracts too. So a fair comparison takes those days off: with 28 days' holiday and 10 other unpaid days, you'd bill 222 days a year.

This compares money before tax. Inside IR35, an umbrella company or the client runs your pay through PAYE, and outside IR35 your own limited company pays corporation tax and you take salary and dividends: the contractor calculator shows take-home for both, and the salary calculator what an employee keeps.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert a day rate to a salary?

Multiply the day rate by the days you'd actually bill, not all 260 weekdays: take off the 28 days' holiday an employee would be paid for and any gaps between contracts. £400 a day over 222 days is £88,800, like a salary of about £87,479 once employer pension is allowed for.

How do I work out a day rate from a salary?

Add the employer pension the job would pay to the salary and divide by the days you'd bill. £60,000 works out at about £276.22 a day over 222 days, against £230.77 by simply dividing by 260.

How many days a year does a contractor work?

There are about 260 weekdays. Taking off 28 days' holiday leaves 232, and gaps between contracts, sickness and training usually take off more: 222 days allows 10.

Is a day rate before or after tax?

Before. Inside IR35 an umbrella company or client deducts PAYE tax and National Insurance; outside IR35 your company pays corporation tax and you pay tax on what you take out. The contractor calculator works out take-home both ways.
