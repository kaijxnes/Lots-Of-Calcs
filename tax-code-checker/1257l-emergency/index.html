    What W1, M1 and X mean

    • You're entitled to the standard tax-free Personal Allowance.
    • You can earn £12,570 tax-free from this job or pension in the tax year (about £1,047 a month).
    • That's the standard Personal Allowance: the usual code for someone with one job or pension.
    • You're on an emergency tax code: your tax is worked out on what you're paid in that week or month only, as if you're paid that amount every week or month of the year. This is usually temporary: giving your employer your P45 helps, and HMRC normally sorts it within 35 days of starting a job.

    Frequently asked questions

    What does tax code 1257L M1 mean?

    You're entitled to the standard tax-free Personal Allowance. You can earn £12,570 tax-free from this job or pension in the tax year (about £1,047 a month). That's the standard Personal Allowance: the usual code for someone with one job or pension. You're on an emergency tax code: your tax is worked out on what you're paid in that week or month only, as if you're paid that amount every week or month of the year. This is usually temporary: giving your employer your P45 helps, and HMRC normally sorts it within 35 days of starting a job.

    How much tax will I pay on £30,000 with an emergency code?

    About £3,486 a year (£290.50 a month) in Income Tax at 2026/27 rates, if you're paid the same each month: on an emergency code each payday is taxed on its own, so a pay that varies can mean paying too much or too little. National Insurance comes on top.

    How do I get off an emergency tax code?

    Give your new employer your P45 from your last job (ask your old employer for one if you didn't get it). HMRC usually updates your code once it has the details from both employers, which can take up to 35 days from when you start. If you've paid too much, the refund normally comes through your pay.

    Is 1257L an emergency tax code?

    Not on its own. It's only an emergency code if it's followed by W1, M1 or X (or your payslip says NONCUM).
