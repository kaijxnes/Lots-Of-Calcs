How a bonus is taxed

A cash bonus is pay. GOV.UK tells employers it "counts as earnings", so they add it to your other pay and take Income Tax and National Insurance through PAYE, and HMRC's manual includes Christmas, productivity and completion bonuses. There's no special bonus tax rate: the bonus sits on top of your salary, so it's taxed at your highest rate, and can push part of your pay into the next band.

Why a bonus can cost less National Insurance than a pay rise

Income Tax is worked out on your pay for the whole tax year, but National Insurance is worked out on each payday on its own: 8% between £1,048 and £4,189 a month and 2% above that. A bonus pushes that month's pay over £4,189, so much of it is charged only 2%. On a £30,000 salary a £5,000 bonus paid in one month costs £201.35 in National Insurance; the same £5,000 spread across the year as a pay rise would cost £400.00. Student loan repayments work the same way, per payday, with each plan's threshold split by month or week, so a bonus month takes 9% of everything over that month's threshold.

Why so much tax came off your bonus payslip

On a normal tax code, GOV.UK explains, "your tax is worked out based on your total income so far in that tax year". Your tax-free allowance and bands are shared out across the year, so the month with the bonus can show tax at a higher rate than you'll pay overall, particularly early in the tax year; your later payslips even it out, and the calculator shows where you end up by 5 April. On an emergency code (ending W1, M1 or X) "you get taxed as if you're paid that amount every week or month of the year", which can take far too much from a bonus month: the tax code checker explains your code and the tax refund calculator estimates what comes back.

Bonuses and the 60% band

Your Personal Allowance "goes down by £1 for every £2 that your adjusted net income is above £100,000", and is gone at £125,140. A bonus that takes you into that band is taxed at an effective 60% there, plus National Insurance. On a £95,000 salary, £5,000 of a £10,000 bonus falls in it and you keep £4,800.00 (48%). Adjusted net income is your taxable income less pension contributions (grossed up), so paying the part over £100,000 into a pension keeps the allowance: some employers let you sacrifice a bonus into your pension, or you can make a personal contribution. The salary sacrifice calculator and the pension tax relief calculator show what that's worth.

Frequently asked questions

How much tax will I pay on my bonus?

It's taxed as pay at your highest Income Tax rate, plus National Insurance. On a £35,000 salary in England a £5,000 bonus paid in one month costs £1,000.00 in Income Tax and £176.35 in National Insurance, so you keep £3,823.65.

Is a bonus taxed at a higher rate than salary?

No. A bonus counts as earnings and is added to your pay, so it's taxed at the same rates. It can look that way because it comes on top of your salary, at your highest rate, and because PAYE in the bonus month can take more than you'll owe by the end of the tax year.

Do I pay National Insurance on a bonus?

Yes, Class 1 National Insurance, worked out on the pay period it's paid in. Above £4,189 in a month the rate is 2% rather than 8%, so a bonus paid in one month usually costs less National Insurance than the same amount as a pay rise.

Why was my bonus taxed at 40%?

If your salary and bonus together go over the higher rate threshold, the part above it is taxed at 40%. Even if your yearly pay doesn't, PAYE works on your pay so far in the tax year, so a big bonus month can be taxed at 40% for that month and the following months even it out. On an emergency code (W1, M1 or X) the bonus month is taxed as if you were paid that much every month.

How much of a £1,000 bonus will I keep?

In England, Wales and Northern Ireland about £720.00 as a basic rate taxpayer and £580.00 as a higher rate taxpayer, before any student loan or pension.

Can I put my bonus into my pension to save tax?

If your employer offers bonus sacrifice, the bonus goes into your pension before Income Tax and National Insurance. Pension contributions also reduce adjusted net income, which keeps your Personal Allowance if the bonus would take you over £100,000.
