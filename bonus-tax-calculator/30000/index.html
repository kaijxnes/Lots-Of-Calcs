A £30,000 bonus paid in one month with your normal pay, 2026/27, no student loan or pension. Income Tax is what the bonus adds over the whole tax year.

A cash bonus is pay: it's added to your other pay and taxed through PAYE, at your highest Income Tax rate. National Insurance is worked out on the month it's paid in, so a bonus month is charged 2% on pay over £4,189 rather than 8%, and a one-off bonus usually costs less National Insurance than the same pay spread over the year. Between £100,000 and £125,140 you also lose Personal Allowance, so part of a bonus is taxed at an effective 60%: on £100,000 you keep £12,129.00 (40%). Paying the part over £100,000 into a pension keeps the allowance. The bonus tax calculator explains more, and handles weekly pay, student loans and pensions.

Frequently asked questions

How much of a £30,000 bonus will I keep?

It depends on your salary, because the bonus is taxed at your highest rate. In England, Wales and Northern Ireland you keep about £21,352.65 on a £30,000 salary, £17,400.00 on £60,000 and £15,900.00 on £150,000 (2026/27, paid in one month, no student loan or pension).

How much tax is taken off a £30,000 bonus?

On a £30,000 salary, £7,946.00 Income Tax and £701.35 National Insurance. On £60,000, £12,000.00 Income Tax and £600.00 National Insurance.

Is a bonus taxed at a higher rate than salary?

No. A bonus counts as earnings and is added to your pay, so it's taxed at the same rates. It comes on top of your salary, so it's taxed at your highest rate, and PAYE in the bonus month can take more than you'll owe by the end of the tax year.

Do I pay National Insurance on a £30,000 bonus?

Yes, worked out on the pay period it's paid in: 8% up to £4,189 in the month and 2% above that.
